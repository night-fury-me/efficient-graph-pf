\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%% ===================================================================

We presented AEGIS, a framework that exploits the equilibrium structure of contractive implicit GNNs for adversarial vulnerability analysis. Building on the Banach fixed-point theorem and IFT, we characterized three vulnerability regimes around a critical budget $\ecrit$, with explicit assumptions (ReLU activation, spectral-norm constraint, linear head). Under constrained (symmetric, edge-weight-only) perturbations, the first-order sensitivity analysis achieves tightness $1.00 \pm 0.01$ across 9 datasets. AEGIS attacks outperform both surrogate-transfer (Mettack) and adaptive white-box attacks at small perturbation budgets. Its deterministic certificates are $1.4$--$2.1\times$ larger than randomized smoothing at $\sigma = 0.25$. The vulnerability spectrum independently recovers power grid N-1 contingency rankings (P@10 = $0.58$--$0.71$), bridging adversarial ML with power systems analysis.

\textbf{Limitations.} (1)~\emph{Architecture scope:} AEGIS applies to contractive implicit GNNs (IGNN-class) only, not to GAT, GIN, or other explicit architectures. (2)~\emph{First-order approximation:} certificates are tight for small $\varepsilon$ but conservative at large budgets ($\varepsilon > 0.1$); edge additions that change sparsity pattern break the smoothness assumption entirely. (3)~\emph{Scalability:} dense Jacobian computation limits subgraph size to $N \approx 200$. (4)~\emph{Power flow:} $\tau = 0.42$--$0.67$ is insufficient for standalone operational use; AEGIS serves as a screening layer, not a replacement for PTDF/LODF analysis.

\textbf{Future work.} Sparse Neumann-series solvers for larger-scale analysis. Extension to monotone operator networks~\cite{winston2020monotone} and neural ODEs on graphs. Discrete perturbation models (edge insertion/deletion) via combinatorial optimization over the constrained sensitivity spectrum.
