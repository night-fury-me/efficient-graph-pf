\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%% ===================================================================

Before AEGIS, practitioners deploying GNNs on safety-critical graphs had no principled way to determine which structural perturbations would break their model, or by how much. AEGIS fills this gap: given any trained GNN, it produces a quantitatively accurate vulnerability map identifying the weakest edges, the optimal attack direction, and per-node tolerance budgets. Across 7 architectures and 9 datasets, these predictions are tight ($1.00 \pm 0.01$ at $\varepsilon = 0.01$, within 15\% at $\varepsilon = 0.10$). On power grids, the same analysis recovers N-1 contingency rankings (P@10 = 0.66--0.81) without domain-specific inputs.

\textbf{Limitations.} (1)~\emph{First-order approximation:} sensitivity radii $r_v$ are locally tight for small $\varepsilon$ but conservative at large budgets. Without bounding the second-order remainder, they are local sensitivity measures rather than global robustness certificates. (2)~\emph{Threat model:} we perturb normalized adjacency $\Ahat$ directly with continuous edge-weight changes; discrete edge insertions/deletions are outside the formal guarantee. (3)~\emph{Scalability:} dense Jacobian computation limits subgraph size to $N \approx 200$. (4)~\emph{GAT requires modification:} standard GAT uses $A$ as a binary mask; edge-weighted GAT is needed for continuous sensitivity analysis (GATv2~\cite{brody2022how} partially addresses this via dynamic attention but retains binary neighborhood masking). (5)~\emph{Power flow:} Kendall $\tau = 0.37$--$0.67$ is insufficient for standalone operational use; AEGIS serves as a screening layer, not a replacement for domain-specific analysis~\cite{wood2014power}.

\textbf{Intended use.} AEGIS is a post-training diagnostic for GNN deployments on safety-critical graphs: it identifies vulnerable nodes/edges, determines at what perturbation budget predictions become unreliable, and prioritizes regions for protection. The per-edge ranking directly informs defense design (\cref{sec:defense_ablation}).

\textbf{Future work.} Sparse Neumann-series solvers for larger subgraphs. Second-order remainder bounds to upgrade first-order sensitivity radii into formal certificates. Extension to discrete perturbation models (edge insertion/deletion) via combinatorial optimization over the $S_c$ spectrum. Integration with GNN defense mechanisms~\cite{jin2020graph,zhang2020gnnguard} for vulnerability-aware robust training.
