\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%% ===================================================================

\AEGIS unifies per-edge vulnerability rankings, the SVD-optimal attack direction, and per-node first-order sensitivity radii under one constrained sensitivity matrix $S_c$, computed matrix-free for any GNN with continuous edge-weight-modulated message passing and supplemented by regime guarantees (\cref{thm:phase_transition}) for contractive implicit models.

\textbf{Limitations.} (i) Radii $r_v$ are first-order sensitivity thresholds, not probabilistic certificates (\cref{rem:certificates}). (ii) Standard GAT and binary-adjacency-mask architectures fall outside the framework; the seventh architecture is the edge-weighted GAT$^\dagger$ variant (\cref{sec:explicit_extension}). (iii) On large citation networks 50-node subgraph rankings correlate weakly with full-graph rankings ($\tau{=}0.16$ on Cora); the matrix-free pipeline is the recommended graph-level path. (iv) Spectral-norm-constrained IGNN ($77.5\%$ Cora) lags explicit GNNs ($\sim 82\%$); the formal track is a model-class trade-off, not a universal cost. (v) Power-flow $\tau{=}{+}0.37$ to $+0.62$ is proof-of-concept; operator-grade deployment requires admittance-weighted edges, broader operating coverage, and thermal/voltage-violation severity. (vi) The threat model is edge-deletion / weight-perturbation; insertion attacks (Nettack-style) need a candidate-edge basis $\bar E$ extension. The vulnerability map informs defence design (\cref{sec:defense_ablation}); code will be released with a 90-day stakeholder notification and tiered-access for attack-generation.
