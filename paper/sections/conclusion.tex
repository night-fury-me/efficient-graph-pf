\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%% ===================================================================

We presented AEGIS, a structural vulnerability analysis framework for graph neural networks built on the constrained sensitivity matrix $S_c$. The framework applies to any differentiable GNN: for $K$-layer explicit models, $S_c$ is derived from the unrolled Jacobian; for contractive implicit models, it is derived from the equilibrium sensitivity via the IFT, which additionally provides a critical budget $\ecrit$ and convergence guarantees. Across 7 architectures (IGNN, GCN, GIN, GAT, GraphSAGE, APPNP) and 9 datasets, constrained first-order tightness is $1.00 \pm 0.01$ at $\varepsilon = 0.01$, degrading gracefully to 1.07--1.15 at $\varepsilon = 0.10$. The SVD-optimal attack inflicts 2--8$\times$ more damage than random perturbation. The vulnerability spectrum independently recovers power grid N-1 contingency rankings (P@10 between 0.66 and 0.81).

\textbf{Limitations.} (1)~\emph{First-order approximation:} radii are locally tight for small $\varepsilon$ but conservative at large budgets. Without bounding the second-order remainder, they are local sensitivity radii rather than global certificates. (2)~\emph{Threat model:} we perturb normalized adjacency $\Ahat$ directly with continuous edge-weight changes; discrete edge insertions/deletions are outside the formal guarantee. (3)~\emph{Scalability:} dense Jacobian computation limits subgraph size to $N \approx 200$. (4)~\emph{GAT requires modification:} standard GAT uses $A$ as a binary mask; edge-weighted GAT is needed for continuous sensitivity analysis. (5)~\emph{Power flow:} Kendall $\tau = 0.37$--$0.67$ is insufficient for standalone operational use; AEGIS serves as a screening layer, not a replacement for domain-specific analysis~\cite{wood2014power}.

\textbf{Future work.} Sparse Neumann-series solvers for larger subgraphs. Second-order remainder bounds to upgrade first-order radii into formal certificates. Extension to discrete perturbation models (edge insertion/deletion) via combinatorial optimization over the $S_c$ spectrum. Integration with GNN defense mechanisms~\cite{jin2020graph,zhang2020gnnguard} for vulnerability-aware robust training.
