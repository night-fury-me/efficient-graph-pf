\section{Adversarial Equilibrium Theory}
\label{sec:theory}
%% ===================================================================

\Cref{sec:framework} read three diagnostics off the operator $S_c$; we now establish the guarantees that justify them, beginning with the safety boundary. The governing picture is a phase transition in the budget $\varepsilon$: while $\varepsilon$ is small the perturbed model stays contractive and its prediction moves boundedly with the graph; as $\varepsilon$ approaches a critical budget $\ecrit$ the equilibrium grows arbitrarily sensitive; past $\ecrit$ contraction is no longer guaranteed and the prediction can break. \Cref{thm:phase_transition} makes this precise and puts $\ecrit$ in closed form.

\begin{theorem}[Vulnerability Characterization for Contractive Implicit GNNs]
\label{thm:phase_transition}
Let $F_\theta(Z, \Ahat) = \phi(\Ahat ZW^\top + X_{\mathrm{proj}})$ be an IGNN-class operator satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(A1)] $\phi$ is ReLU or any 1-Lipschitz activation; nonsmoothness at zero is handled via the conservative IFT~\cite{bolte2021conservative} (proof below).
\item[(A2)] $\norm{W}_2 \leq c$ via spectral normalization.
\item[(A3)] $\norm{J_z}_2 \leq \kappa < 1$ verified post-training ($\kappa{=}0.14$--$0.59$ across our suite; \cref{tab:cross_domain}). (A2) does not imply (A3); we report the trained $\kappa$ so practitioners can audit, and \AEGIS still returns rankings and attacks if (A3) fails, its guarantees then scoped to the contractive regime.
\end{enumerate}
Define the critical budget
\begin{equation}
\ecrit = \frac{1 - \kappa}{\norm{W}_2}.
\end{equation}
Then structural perturbations $\delta \Ahat$ with $\norm{\delta \Ahat}_F = \varepsilon$ exhibit three regimes:

\noindent\textbf{(a) Subcritical} ($\varepsilon < \ecrit$): The perturbed operator remains contractive with a unique fixed point satisfying
\begin{equation}
\norm{\Delta \zstar}_F \leq \sigma_1(S) \cdot \varepsilon + O(\varepsilon^2),
\label{eq:shift_bound}
\end{equation}
where $S = (I - J_z)^{-1} J_A$ is the structural sensitivity matrix and $\sigma_1(S) \leq \norm{J_A}_{\mathrm{op}} / (1 - \kappa)$.

\noindent\textbf{(b) Critical} ($\varepsilon \to \ecrit$): The resolvent obeys $\norm{(I{-}J_z')^{-1}}_2 \geq 1/\min_i|1{-}\lambda_i(J_z')|$, blowing up when an eigenvalue of $J_z'$ approaches $1$ (a vanishing spectral gap to $1$, not $\norm{J_z'}_2\!\to\!1$ alone). The rate is $\Omega(1/(\ecrit{-}\varepsilon))$ when $J_z'$ is normal with a dominant real-positive (Perron) eigenvalue; in general $\ecrit$ \emph{lower-bounds} the divergence threshold, the slack being the nonnormality $\eta$ (\cref{obs:eta_bound}; $\eta{\leq}2.47$ for general ReLU, \cref{rem:eta_relu}).

\noindent\textbf{(c) Supercritical} ($\varepsilon \geq \ecrit$): The contraction certificate no longer applies ($\norm{J_z'}_2$ may exceed 1); at the worst-case $\kappa{=}\norm{\Ahat}_2\norm{W}_2$, $\varepsilon{<}\ecrit$ is a sufficient safety boundary that trained IGNNs sit well inside (a $2$--$4\times$ spectral-radius margin to $\rho{=}1$, \cref{app:phase_scal}).
\end{theorem}

\noindent\textit{Proof in \cref{app:proof_phase}.} The subcritical bound is a conservative IFT~\cite{bolte2021conservative} with Neumann $\norm{(I{-}J_z)^{-1}}_2\le1/(1{-}\kappa)$; the critical rate follows from the resolvent identity, which $\ecrit$ lower-bounds (slack $\eta$).

$\ecrit$ is a closed-form sufficient bound that $S_c$ tightens $7$--$14\times$; the Neumann-vs-spectral-radius gap is the pseudospectral index $\eta$, bounded by $W$ alone for symmetric $\Ahat$ ($\eta\le\kappa(V_W)$ all-active, $\eta\in[1.19,2.47]$ for ReLU; \cref{obs:eta_bound,rem:eta_relu}).

\Cref{thm:phase_transition}(a) certifies safety \emph{below} $\ecrit$; the same operator bounds the boundary from \emph{above}, through the nonnormality $g_W{=}\norm{W}_2/\rho(W){\geq}1$ of $W$ (the \emph{entire} norm-vs-radius gap for symmetric $\Ahat$).

\begin{theorem}[Constant-factor two-sided boundary, all-active]
\label{thm:cf2s}
Under \textnormal{(A1)--(A3)} with $\Ahat$ symmetric and $\ecrit{>}0$, the all-active contraction boundary $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{br}}$ (the smallest feasible $\norm{\delta\Ahat}_F$ at which $\rho(\Ahat'{\otimes}W){\geq}1$) obeys
\begin{equation}
\ecrit \;\leq\; \varepsilon_{\mathrm{br}} \;\leq\; \tfrac{C}{\beta}\,\ecrit,
\qquad C = g_W\,\tfrac{1+\kappa}{1-\kappa},
\label{eq:bracket}
\end{equation}
with $C$ dimension-free and computable post-training, and $\beta{\in}(0,1]$ the edge-supported Frobenius mass of $\Ahat$'s Perron mode (positive for any connected graph). The upper side is attained by a rank-one critical-driving attack aligned with that mode; the bracket collapses to equality iff $g_W{=}1$ and $\beta{=}1$.
\end{theorem}

The constant is not tight---proven slack ${\sim}10$--$16\times$ on our suite---but its value is the coupling it exposes: one operator $S_c$ supplies the certified budget $\ecrit$, the matching attack, and (through $\sigma_1(S_c)$) the defense. Empirically the norm certificate under-states the true breaking budget by $\mathbf{2}$--$\mathbf{9\times}$ ($10$ seeds); proof and nonlinear extension in \cref{app:bracket}.

The third diagnostic, the per-node radius $r_v$ named in \cref{sec:framework}, carries a first-order safety guarantee.

\begin{proposition}[Per-Node First-Order Sensitivity Radius]
\label{prop:radius}
For a linear head $f(z){=}Wz{+}b$ and a node $v$ classified as $y_v$ with positive margins $m_v^{(c)}{=}f_{y_v}(\zstar_v){-}f_c(\zstar_v){>}0$ to every competitor $c{\neq}y_v$, the first-order sensitivity radius
\begin{equation}
r_v = \min_{c\neq y_v} \frac{m_v^{(c)}}{\norm{(W_{y_v}{-}W_c)\,S_v}_2}
\label{eq:radius}
\end{equation}
preserves the classification of $v$ for any $\norm{\delta\Ahat}_F < r_v$, where $S_v$ are the block-rows of $S$ at node $v$; substituting $S_{c,v}$ gives the tighter constrained radius $r_v^{(c)}{\geq}r_v$. Minimizing over \emph{all} competitors is the distance to the nearest first-order boundary, so the bound holds even when the runner-up changes.
\end{proposition}

\noindent\textit{Proof in \cref{app:proof_radius}.} A Cauchy--Schwarz bound on each per-competitor margin change gives $r_v$ as the smallest margin divided by two amplification factors (decision-boundary and equilibrium sensitivity); high-margin, low-sensitivity nodes get large radii.

\subsection{\AEGIS-Conformal: A Distribution-Free Certificate}
\label{sec:conformal}
The radius $r_v$ (\cref{eq:radius}) is a first-order threshold---locally tight, but violable at larger $\varepsilon$. We upgrade it to a distribution-free guarantee---sound under an exchangeability condition and gated by the coverage test (\cref{app:conformal}). Split conformal prediction yields a set $C(v)$ with $\Pr[y_v\in C(v)]\geq1{-}\alpha$; making this hold \emph{robustly} over the ball $\norm{\delta\Ahat}_F\leq\varepsilon$ requires bounding the worst-case conformity-score shift, which \AEGIS supplies \emph{analytically} via the same $S_c$ sensitivity (a curvature-corrected $L_1^{c}\varepsilon{+}C_v\varepsilon^2$), replacing the ${\sim}10^4$-sample smoothing otherwise needed. Feeding it into the binary-certificate split conformal of \citet{zargarbashi2023conformal} yields a robust set with
\begin{equation}
\Pr\!\big[y_v\in C_\varepsilon(v)\big]\;\geq\;1-\alpha
\qquad\text{for all }\ \norm{\delta\Ahat}_F\leq\varepsilon .
\label{eq:conformal}
\end{equation}
We validate this certificate empirically---nominal-level coverage and the gate under the worst-case attack---in \cref{sec:certify}, and the coupled $\sigma_1(S_c)$ defense in \cref{sec:defense}.

\subsection{Transfer to Discrete Edits and Explicit GNNs}
A first-order bridge carries the continuous score to discrete edge removal: modelling single-edge deletion as fixed-normalization masking, the discrete damage equals the edge-weighted sensitivity score plus a bounded second-order remainder, so for subcritical edges the score $A_{ij}v_{ij}$ preserves the brute-force N-1 ranking (\cref{prop:transfer}); empirically it reaches $\tau{=}\mathbf{+0.996}$ on full-graph Amazon Photo (\cref{sec:explicit_extension}). The construction is not tied to implicit models either: a $K$-layer explicit GNN gets the same $S_c$ from an unrolled sensitivity $S_K$ (\cref{prop:explicit}), so 6 of our 7 architectures inherit the full pipeline; only the closed-form $\ecrit$ stays restricted to the contractive subclass. Both propositions are stated and proved in \cref{app:proofs}.
