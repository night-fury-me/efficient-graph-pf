\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%% ===================================================================

Graph neural networks are now deployed in domains where structural errors carry tangible consequences: fraudulent accounts evade detection via perturbed transaction edges~\cite{zugner2018adversarial}, drug-interaction models misfire under perturbed molecular graphs~\cite{dai2018adversarial}, and power grids can miss critical contingencies when their graph representations are structurally fragile~\cite{nakiganda2023graph}. A practitioner deploying a GNN faces a question current tools leave unanswered: \emph{which edges, if perturbed, would cause predictions to fail, and by how much?}

Each existing thread maps only part of these \emph{adversarial fault lines}. Structural attacks~\cite{zugner2018adversarial,zugner2019adversarial,geisler2021robustness,wu2019adversarial} rank edges by surrogate gradients, but return no continuous direction and no per-node budget. Smoothing~\cite{bojchevski2020efficient,bojchevski2019certifiable,schuchardt2023localized,schuchardt2021collective} and deterministic certifiers~\cite{li2025agnncert,zugner2019certifiable} return per-node certificates, but neither an edge ranking nor a direction. Robust-architecture defenses~\cite{zhu2019robust,zhang2020gnnguard,jin2020graph} harden models without ever surfacing which edges are vulnerable, and sober-look critiques~\cite{mujkanovic2022defenses,gosch2023adversarial} catalogue the evaluation pitfalls that follow. No prior method audits, certifies, and defends in a single matrix-free pass. \AEGIS (Adversarial Evaluation of Graph Integrity via Sensitivity) does, and all three capabilities come from one analytical object: the constrained sensitivity matrix $S_c$ (\cref{sec:framework}). The idea is simple. $S_c$ records how strongly each edge can push the model's equilibrium prediction, so its leading singular direction, its column norms, and its per-node margins deliver the three diagnostics together, from one randomized SVD over the equilibrium resolvent rather than three separate analyses, and the same analytic bound upgrades each per-node radius into a distribution-free certificate (\cref{sec:conformal}). Two scope boundaries are worth stating up front. The closed-form characterisation of when a model breaks (\cref{thm:phase_transition}) holds for contractive implicit GNNs, whereas the operator $S_c$ itself extends to any GNN with continuous edge-weight-modulated message passing (\cref{sec:explicit_extension}); throughout, the threat model is edge deletion and weight perturbation (\cref{sec:background}).

\textbf{Contributions.} \textbf{(1) Constrained sensitivity operator.} $S_c$ specialises equilibrium IFT sensitivity~\cite{koh2017understanding,gould2021deep} to \emph{structural} edge perturbations via the projection $P_c$. One matrix-free query reads the SVD-optimal direction, per-edge rankings, and per-node radii together, where no prior object yields all three; it matches a dense $\sigma_1$ to $0.03\%$ at $N{=}200$ and scales to $N{=}7{,}650$. \textbf{(2) Certification from sensitivity.} The same bound yields two guarantees. \AEGIS-Conformal turns it into a \emph{distribution-free} certificate: coverage $\geq 1{-}\alpha$ over the $\varepsilon$-ball, holding at the nominal level under worst-case attack and staying non-vacuous even where the deterministic radius is thin (\cref{sec:conformal}). For contractive models we add a constant-factor two-sided characterisation of the breaking point $\ecrit{=}(1{-}\kappa)/\norm{W}_2$, whose norm certificate empirically under-states the true break by $2$--$9\times$ ($10$ seeds, \cref{app:phase_scal}). \textbf{(3) Coupled defense.} Penalizing $\sigma_1(S_c)$ trades clean accuracy for certified robustness (larger certified radii, higher Conformal coverage), so the operator that finds the worst attack also tunes the defense (\cref{sec:defense}). \textbf{(4) Empirical evaluation.} 6 datasets, 7 architectures, 4 domains (390 runs): four-quadrant attack comparison, head-to-head vs.\ GR-BCD/PR-BCD~\cite{geisler2021robustness} with complementary positioning against AGNNCert~\cite{li2025agnncert} (\cref{app:baselines}), continuous-to-discrete transfer (\cref{fig:tau_heatmap}), and a fraud-detector audit (\cref{sec:fraud_case}).
