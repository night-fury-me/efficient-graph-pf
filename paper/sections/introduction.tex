\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%% ===================================================================

Graph neural networks are now deployed in domains where structural errors carry tangible consequences: fraudulent accounts evade detection via perturbed transaction edges~\cite{zugner2018adversarial}, drug-interaction models misfire under perturbed molecular graphs~\cite{dai2018adversarial}, and power grids can miss critical contingencies when their graph representations are structurally fragile~\cite{nakiganda2023graph}. A practitioner deploying a GNN faces a question current tools leave unanswered: \emph{which edges, if perturbed, would cause predictions to fail, and by how much?}

Each existing thread maps only part of these \emph{adversarial fault lines}. Structural attacks~\cite{zugner2018adversarial,zugner2019adversarial,geisler2021robustness,wu2019adversarial} rank edges by surrogate gradients but yield no continuous direction and no per-node budget; smoothing~\cite{bojchevski2020efficient,bojchevski2019certifiable,schuchardt2023localized,schuchardt2021collective} and IBP-style certifiers~\cite{li2025agnncert,zugner2019certifiable} return per-node certificates but no edge ranking or direction; robust-architecture defenses~\cite{zhu2019robust,zhang2020gnnguard,jin2020graph} harden models without surfacing per-edge vulnerability; and sober-look critiques~\cite{mujkanovic2022defenses,gosch2023adversarial} flag evaluation pitfalls. No single method returns all three at once. \AEGIS (Adversarial Evaluation of Graph Integrity via Sensitivity) does, from one analytical object: the constrained sensitivity matrix $S_c$ (\cref{sec:framework}). Intuitively, $S_c$ records how strongly each edge can push the model's equilibrium prediction, so its leading singular direction, its column norms, and its per-node margins yield the three diagnostics in a single pass. Here ``one query'' means one matrix-free $S_c$ construction (a single randomized SVD over the equilibrium resolvent), not three separate analyses. The closed-form regime characterisation (\cref{thm:phase_transition}) is established for contractive implicit GNNs, and $S_c$ extends to any GNN with continuous edge-weight-modulated message passing (\cref{sec:explicit_extension}); the threat model targets edge deletion and weight perturbation (\cref{sec:background}).

\textbf{Contributions.} \textbf{(1) Constrained sensitivity operator.} $S_c$ specialises equilibrium IFT sensitivity~\cite{koh2017understanding,gould2021deep} to \emph{structural} edge perturbations via the projection $P_c$. One matrix-free query then reads the SVD-optimal direction, per-edge rankings, and per-node radii together, where no prior object yields all three; it matches a dense $\sigma_1$ to $0.03\%$ at $N{=}200$ and scales to $N{=}7{,}650$. \textbf{(2) Phase-transition theory.} A worst-case three-regime characterisation with closed-form critical budget $\ecrit{=}(1{-}\kappa)/\norm{W}_2$ for the spectral-norm-constrained IGNN subclass. Trained IGNNs satisfy it with $2$--$4\times$ margin across our $\kappa_{\max}$ sweep, a provable structural-perturbation safety threshold at $\sim6\%$ accuracy cost (\cref{thm:phase_transition,sec:phase_transition}). \textbf{(3) Empirical evaluation.} 10 datasets, 7 architectures, 5 domains (390 runs): four-quadrant attack comparison, head-to-head vs.\ GR-BCD/PR-BCD~\cite{geisler2021robustness}, AGNNCert~\cite{li2025agnncert}, and LODF (\cref{tab:baselines}), continuous-to-discrete transfer (\cref{fig:tau_heatmap}), and a power-grid N-1 case study (\cref{sec:power_flow}).
