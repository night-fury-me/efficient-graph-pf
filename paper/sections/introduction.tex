\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%% ===================================================================

Graph neural networks (GNNs) are vulnerable to adversarial structural perturbation: small changes to the graph topology---adding or removing a few edges---can drastically alter predictions~\cite{zugner2018adversarial,zugner2019adversarial}. Defending against such attacks requires understanding \emph{which perturbations matter} and \emph{by how much}. Current approaches either provide loose certified bounds via randomized smoothing~\cite{bojchevski2020efficient} or rely on heuristic attacks without guarantees~\cite{zugner2019adversarial}.

We observe that a specific family of GNNs---contractive implicit GNNs (IGNN-class~\cite{gu2020implicit})---possesses a structural property that enables a qualitatively different kind of analysis. These models iterate a contractive operator $F_\theta$ until convergence to a fixed point $\zstar = F_\theta(\zstar, A)$, rather than propagating through a fixed number of layers. This fixed-point structure, combined with the implicit function theorem (IFT), yields the \emph{structural sensitivity matrix}
\begin{equation}
S = (I - J_z)^{-1} J_A,
\label{eq:sensitivity_matrix}
\end{equation}
where $J_z = \partial F/\partial z|_{\zstar}$ and $J_A = \partial F/\partial \mathrm{vec}(A)|_{\zstar}$. The matrix $S$ maps structural perturbations to fixed-point shifts: $\Delta \zstar \approx S \cdot \mathrm{vec}(\delta A)$.

The central finding is that, under realistic perturbation constraints (symmetric, edge-weight-only), a \emph{constrained} variant $S_c$ of this sensitivity matrix predicts adversarial vulnerability with tightness $1.00 \pm 0.01$ to first order at small perturbation budgets ($\varepsilon = 0.01$). This holds across 9 datasets spanning 4 domains and 10 random seeds. The prediction is first-order; for large perturbations ($\varepsilon > 0.1$), higher-order terms dominate and the approximation becomes conservative. AEGIS applies specifically to contractive implicit GNNs (IGNN-class); explicit architectures such as GAT, GIN, and GraphSAGE lack the fixed-point structure required for this analysis.

\textbf{Contributions.} We make three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Contraction-based vulnerability characterization.} For contractive implicit GNNs (IGNN-class~\cite{gu2020implicit}), we formalize vulnerability regimes around a critical budget $\ecrit = (1-\kappa)/\norm{W}_2$ using the operator-norm contraction constant $\kappa = \norm{J_z}_2 < 1$. We make assumptions explicit (ReLU, spectral-norm constraint, linear head) and quantify non-normality effects (\cref{sec:theory}).

\item \textbf{Constrained structural sensitivity.} We construct a constrained sensitivity matrix $S_c$ that restricts analysis to symmetric, edge-only perturbations---the physically meaningful threat model for graphs. Under these constraints, first-order predictions achieve tightness $\approx 1.00$. From $S_c$ we derive SVD-optimal structural attacks (20--50\% stronger than adaptive white-box PGD) and deterministic first-order per-node robustness radii (\cref{sec:framework,sec:experiments}).

\item \textbf{Cross-domain validation.} We validate across 9 datasets in 4 domains (citation, e-commerce, encyclopedia, power flow). Constrained first-order tightness $\approx 1.00$ holds across all domains. As a case study, the vulnerability spectrum recovers power grid N-1 contingency rankings with precision@10 = $0.66$--$0.81$ (\cref{sec:power_flow}).
\end{enumerate}
