\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%% ===================================================================

Graph neural networks are vulnerable to adversarial structural perturbation: small changes to graph topology can drastically alter predictions~\cite{zugner2018adversarial,zugner2019adversarial,xu2019topology}. Defending against such attacks requires understanding which perturbations matter and by how much. Current approaches either provide loose probabilistic bounds via randomized smoothing~\cite{bojchevski2020efficient}, require layer-by-layer propagation incompatible with infinite-depth models~\cite{zugner2019certifiable,bojchevski2019certifiable}, or rely on heuristic attacks without guarantees~\cite{jin2021adversarial}.

We observe that contractive implicit GNNs (IGNN-class~\cite{gu2020implicit}) possess a structural property that enables a qualitatively different kind of analysis. Rather than propagating through a fixed number of layers, these models iterate a contractive operator $F_\theta$ until convergence to a fixed point $\zstar = F_\theta(\zstar, A)$. This equilibrium structure, combined with the implicit function theorem (IFT), yields the \emph{structural sensitivity matrix}
\begin{equation}
S = (I - J_z)^{-1} J_A,
\label{eq:sensitivity_matrix}
\end{equation}
where $J_z = \partial F/\partial z|_{\zstar}$ is the state Jacobian and $J_A = \partial F/\partial \mathrm{vec}(A)|_{\zstar}$ is the adjacency Jacobian. The matrix $S$ maps structural perturbations directly to equilibrium shifts: $\Delta \zstar \approx S \cdot \mathrm{vec}(\delta A)$.

The central finding is that a \emph{constrained} variant $S_c$ of this matrix, restricted to symmetric edge-only perturbations (the physically meaningful threat model for graphs), predicts adversarial vulnerability with tightness $1.00 \pm 0.01$ to first order at small perturbation budgets ($\varepsilon = 0.01$). This holds across 9 datasets spanning 4 domains and 10 random seeds. At larger budgets ($\varepsilon > 0.1$), higher-order terms dominate and the approximation becomes conservative. We emphasize that AEGIS applies specifically to contractive implicit GNNs; explicit architectures such as GAT, GIN, and GraphSAGE lack the fixed-point structure required for this analysis.

\textbf{Contributions.} We make three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Contraction-based vulnerability characterization.} For contractive implicit GNNs~\cite{gu2020implicit}, we formalize vulnerability regimes around a critical budget $\ecrit = (1-\kappa)/\norm{W}_2$, where $\kappa = \norm{J_z}_2 < 1$ is the operator-norm contraction constant. We state assumptions explicitly (ReLU activation, spectral-norm constraint, linear classification head) and quantify non-normality effects (\cref{sec:theory}).

\item \textbf{Constrained structural sensitivity.} We construct the constrained sensitivity matrix $S_c$ that restricts analysis to symmetric, edge-only perturbations (\cref{sec:framework}). Under these constraints, first-order predictions achieve tightness $\approx 1.00$. From $S_c$ we derive SVD-optimal structural attacks (20--50\% stronger than adaptive white-box PGD, \cref{sec:adaptive}) and deterministic first-order per-node robustness radii (\cref{sec:experiments}).

\item \textbf{Cross-domain validation.} We validate across 9 datasets in 4 domains: citation networks~\cite{sen2008collective}, e-commerce~\cite{shchur2018pitfalls}, encyclopedia~\cite{mernyei2020wiki}, and IEEE power grids~\cite{ieee2024testcases}. Constrained first-order tightness $\approx 1.00$ holds across all domains. The vulnerability spectrum recovers power grid N-1 contingency rankings with precision@10 = $0.66$--$0.81$ (\cref{sec:power_flow}).
\end{enumerate}
