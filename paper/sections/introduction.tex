\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%% ===================================================================

Graph neural networks are now deployed in domains where structural errors carry tangible consequences: fraudulent accounts evade detection when an adversary inserts edges into a financial graph~\cite{zugner2018adversarial}, drug-interaction models misfire under perturbed molecular graphs~\cite{dai2018adversarial}, and power grids can miss critical contingencies when their graph representations are structurally fragile~\cite{nakiganda2023graph}. A practitioner deploying a GNN faces a question current tools leave unanswered: which edges, if perturbed, would cause predictions to fail, and by how much?

Structural attacks~\cite{zugner2018adversarial,zugner2019adversarial,geisler2021robustness} produce per-edge surrogate gradients but no globally optimal continuous direction or per-node budgets; randomized smoothing~\cite{bojchevski2020efficient,bojchevski2019certifiable,schuchardt2023localized,schuchardt2021collective} and IBP-style certifiers~\cite{li2025agnncert,zugner2019certifiable} give per-node certificates but no edge rankings or direction; robust-architecture defenses~\cite{zhu2019robust,zhang2020gnnguard,jin2020graph} harden models without surfacing a vulnerability map, and recent sober-look critiques~\cite{mujkanovic2022defenses,gosch2023adversarial} flag evaluation pitfalls. We unify the three outputs at the level of the analytical object $S_c$; predictive ranking transfer is quantified per architecture in \cref{tab:tau_cross}.

We introduce \AEGIS (Adversarial Evaluation of Graph Integrity via Sensitivity), built around one object: $S_c$ (\cref{sec:framework}). For contractive implicit GNNs the construction admits formal regime guarantees (\cref{thm:phase_transition}); for any GNN with continuous edge-weight-modulated message passing it is a computational tool with empirically validated first-order tightness at small $\varepsilon$ (\cref{sec:explicit_extension}).

\textbf{Contributions.} (1) $S_c$ and a matrix-free pipeline yielding the SVD-optimal direction, per-edge rankings, and per-node radii from one computation, scaling to $N{=}7{,}650$, with matrix-free / dense $\sigma_1$ agreement to $0.03\%$ at the shared $N{=}200$. (2) A worst-case three-regime characterisation along the top singular vector of $\Ahat$ for the spectral-norm-constrained IGNN subclass with critical budget $\ecrit{=}(1{-}\kappa)/\norm{W}_2$ (\cref{thm:phase_transition}); the formal track applies only to this subclass, which costs ${\sim}6\%$ accuracy. (3) Empirical evaluation over 9 datasets, 7 architectures, 4 domains (330 runs), with four-quadrant attack comparison, head-to-head benchmarks vs.\ GR-BCD / PR-BCD~\cite{geisler2021robustness} / AGNNCert / LODF (\cref{tab:baselines}), continuous-to-discrete transfer (\cref{fig:tau_heatmap}, \cref{tab:tau_cross}), and a power-grid case study (\cref{sec:power_flow}).
