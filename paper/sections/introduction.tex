\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%% ===================================================================

Graph neural networks are now deployed in domains where structural errors carry tangible consequences: fraudulent accounts evade detection when an adversary inserts edges into a financial graph~\cite{zugner2018adversarial}, drug-interaction models misfire under perturbed molecular graphs~\cite{dai2018adversarial}, and power grids can miss critical contingencies when their graph representations are structurally fragile~\cite{nakiganda2023graph}. A practitioner deploying a GNN faces a question current tools leave unanswered: which edges, if perturbed, would cause predictions to fail, and by how much?

Structural attacks~\cite{zugner2018adversarial,zugner2019adversarial,geisler2021robustness} give per-edge surrogate gradients but no continuous direction or per-node budgets; smoothing~\cite{bojchevski2020efficient,bojchevski2019certifiable,schuchardt2023localized,schuchardt2021collective} and IBP-style certifiers~\cite{li2025agnncert,zugner2019certifiable} give per-node certificates but no edge rankings or direction; robust-architecture defenses~\cite{zhu2019robust,zhang2020gnnguard,jin2020graph} harden models without surfacing a vulnerability map; sober-look critiques~\cite{mujkanovic2022defenses,gosch2023adversarial} flag evaluation pitfalls. \AEGIS (Adversarial Evaluation of Graph Integrity via Sensitivity) unifies the three outputs at the level of one analytical object, $S_c$ (\cref{sec:framework}); the formal regime guarantees of \cref{thm:phase_transition} apply to contractive implicit GNNs, and $S_c$ extends as a computational tool to any GNN with continuous edge-weight-modulated message passing (\cref{sec:explicit_extension}).

\textbf{Contributions.} (1) $S_c$ and a matrix-free pipeline yielding the SVD-optimal direction, per-edge rankings, and per-node radii from one computation, scaling to $N{=}7{,}650$ (matrix-free/dense $\sigma_1$ agree to $0.03\%$ at $N{=}200$). (2) A worst-case three-regime characterisation with critical budget $\ecrit{=}(1{-}\kappa)/\norm{W}_2$ for the spectral-norm-constrained IGNN subclass (${\sim}6\%$ accuracy cost; \cref{thm:phase_transition}). (3) Empirical evaluation over 9 datasets, 7 architectures, 4 domains (330 runs), with four-quadrant attack comparison, head-to-head vs.\ GR-BCD/PR-BCD~\cite{geisler2021robustness}/AGNNCert/LODF (\cref{tab:baselines}), continuous-to-discrete transfer (\cref{fig:tau_heatmap}), and a power-grid case study (\cref{sec:power_flow}).
