\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%% ===================================================================

Graph neural networks (GNNs) are vulnerable to adversarial structural perturbation: small changes to the graph topology---adding or removing a few edges---can drastically alter predictions~\cite{zugner2018adversarial,zugner2019adversarial}. Defending against such attacks requires understanding \emph{which perturbations matter} and \emph{by how much}. Current approaches either provide loose certified bounds via randomized smoothing~\cite{bojchevski2020efficient} or rely on heuristic attacks without guarantees~\cite{zugner2019adversarial}.

We observe that a specific family of GNNs---Deep Equilibrium GNNs (DEQ-GNNs)~\cite{bai2019deep,gu2020implicit}---possesses a structural property that enables a qualitatively different kind of analysis. These models iterate a contractive operator $F_\theta$ until convergence to a fixed point $\zstar = F_\theta(\zstar, A)$, rather than propagating through a fixed number of layers. This fixed-point structure, combined with the implicit function theorem (IFT), yields the \emph{structural sensitivity matrix}
\begin{equation}
S = (I - J_z)^{-1} J_A,
\label{eq:sensitivity_matrix}
\end{equation}
where $J_z = \partial F/\partial z|_{\zstar}$ and $J_A = \partial F/\partial \mathrm{vec}(A)|_{\zstar}$. The matrix $S$ maps structural perturbations to fixed-point shifts: $\Delta \zstar \approx S \cdot \mathrm{vec}(\delta A)$.

The central finding of this paper is that, under realistic perturbation constraints (symmetric, edge-weight-only), $S$ predicts adversarial vulnerability with tightness $1.00 \pm 0.01$ to first order. This holds across 9 datasets spanning 4 domains and 10 random seeds. The prediction is exact in the first-order sense; for large perturbations (edge additions, deletions of $>$5\% of edges), higher-order terms dominate and the approximation degrades.

\textbf{Contributions.} We make three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Vulnerability characterization.} Building on the Banach fixed-point theorem and IFT as applied to contractive implicit GNNs~\cite{gu2020implicit}, we characterize three vulnerability regimes around a critical budget $\ecrit = (1-\rho)/\norm{W}_2$: subcritical (bounded, certifiable), critical (divergent), and supercritical (certificates void). We make the assumptions explicit (ReLU activation, spectral-norm constraint, linear classification head) and quantify the role of non-normality (\cref{sec:theory}).

\item \textbf{AEGIS framework.} We develop a framework for mining the equilibrium structure of contractive implicit GNNs, providing optimal structural attacks via SVD of the constrained sensitivity matrix and deterministic per-node robustness certificates. AEGIS is validated against both surrogate-transfer attacks (Mettack~\cite{zugner2019adversarial}, 30/30 wins) and adaptive white-box attacks that use the same IFT information. Its deterministic certificates are $1.4$--$2.1\times$ larger than randomized smoothing~\cite{bojchevski2020efficient} at $\sigma = 0.25$ (\cref{sec:framework,sec:experiments}).

\item \textbf{Cross-domain validation.} We validate on 9 datasets across 4 domains---citation networks (Cora, Citeseer, Pubmed), e-commerce (Amazon Photo), encyclopedia (WikiCS), and power flow (IEEE case14/30/57/118)---demonstrating that constrained first-order tightness $\approx 1.00$ holds across domains. As a case study, the vulnerability spectrum recovers power grid N-1 contingency rankings with precision@10 = $0.58$--$0.71$ (\cref{sec:power_flow}).
\end{enumerate}
