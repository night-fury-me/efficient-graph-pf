\begin{abstract}
Graph neural networks in safety-critical settings (drug interaction, fraud detection, power-grid contingency screening) are vulnerable to adversarial graph perturbations, yet none derives, from a single analysis, the three diagnostics a practitioner needs: a maximally sensitive first-order direction, per-edge equilibrium-sensitivity rankings, and per-node sensitivity radii. \AEGIS produces all three from one object, the \emph{constrained sensitivity matrix} $S_c$, computed matrix-free via a Neumann-series resolvent and randomized SVD that scale to $N{=}7{,}650$ on a single GPU. For the spectral-norm-constrained IGNN subclass we prove a closed-form three-regime characterisation with critical budget $\ecrit{=}(1{-}\kappa)/\norm{W}_2$ that trained models satisfy with $\mathbf{2\text{--}4\times}$ margin. $S_c$ extends to continuous-edge-weight message passing (GCN, SAGE, GIN, APPNP, IGNN, edge-weighted GAT$^\dagger$): the continuous-to-discrete bridge is positive in $\mathbf{29/33}$ architecture--dataset cells ($p{<}10^{-5}$) and reaches $\tau\!=\!\mathbf{+0.996\!\pm\!0.002}$ (10 seeds) against brute-force N-1 on full-graph Amazon Photo ($N{=}7{,}650$), while the one-query SVD direction matches 50-step PGD attackers. Top-$v_{ij}$ masking cuts $\sigma_1(S_c)$-damage $\mathbf{42{\pm}8\%}$ (vs.\ $11\%$ random) and survives adaptive recomputation; on IEEE power flow $S_c$ recovers N-1 rankings on case14--118 ($\mathbf{P@10}{=}\mathbf{0.66}$--$\mathbf{0.81}$), competitive with industry LODF.
\end{abstract}
