\begin{abstract}
We introduce AEGIS, a framework that mines the equilibrium structure of Deep Equilibrium Graph Neural Networks (DEQ-GNNs) to predict adversarial vulnerability under graph structure perturbation. Unlike explicit GNNs, DEQ-GNNs converge to a fixed point $\zstar = F(\zstar, A)$, enabling construction of a structural sensitivity matrix $S = (I - J_z)^{-1} J_A$ via the implicit function theorem. We characterize three vulnerability regimes around a critical budget $\ecrit = (1-\rho)/\norm{W}_2$: below it, fixed-point shifts are bounded and certifiable; above it, contractivity is lost and certificates become void. Under realistic perturbation constraints (symmetric, edge-only), our first-order sensitivity analysis achieves tightness $1.00 \pm 0.01$ across 9 datasets (5 graph benchmarks $+$ 4 IEEE power grids, 10 seeds each), meaning the predicted adversarial shift matches the actual shift to within 1\%. AEGIS's IFT-based attacks outperform both surrogate-transfer (Mettack, 30/30 wins) and adaptive white-box attacks at small perturbation budgets. Its deterministic per-node certificates are $1.4$--$2.1\times$ larger than randomized smoothing ($\sigma = 0.25$) at comparable coverage. As a case study, the vulnerability spectrum recovers power grid N-1 contingency rankings (precision@10 = $0.58$--$0.71$). Code is available at [URL].
\end{abstract}
