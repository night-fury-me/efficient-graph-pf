\begin{abstract}
Graph neural networks in safety-critical settings (drug interaction, fraud detection, power-grid contingency screening) are vulnerable to adversarial graph perturbations, yet no prior method produces the three diagnostics a practitioner needs from one query: a globally maximally sensitive perturbation direction, per-edge equilibrium-sensitivity rankings, and per-node first-order sensitivity radii (complementary to certificates such as AGNNCert). \AEGIS produces all three from one object, the \emph{constrained sensitivity matrix} $S_c$, computed matrix-free via a Neumann-series resolvent and randomized SVD that scale to $N{=}7{,}650$ on a single GPU. For the spectral-norm-constrained IGNN subclass we prove a closed-form three-regime characterisation with critical budget $\ecrit{=}(1{-}\kappa)/\norm{W}_2$ that trained IGNNs satisfy with $\mathbf{2\text{--}4\times}$ margin; $S_c$ extends to GCN, SAGE, GIN, APPNP, IGNN, and edge-weighted GAT$^\dagger$ ($\mathbf{29/33}$ architecture--dataset transfer cells positive, sign test $p{<}10^{-5}$). The continuous-to-discrete bridge (Prop.~\ref{prop:transfer}) is validated at scale: the edge-weighted ranking $A_{ij}v_{ij}$ achieves $\tau\!\approx\!\mathbf{+0.998}$ vs.\ brute-force N-1 on Amazon Photo's full graph ($N{=}7{,}650$). Across 7 architectures and 9 datasets a 50-step IFT-gradient PGD attacker recovers only $72$--$92\%$ of the one-query SVD direction's damage; top-$v_{ij}$ masking cuts $\sigma_1(S_c)$ by $\mathbf{42{\pm}8\%}$ vs.\ $11{\pm}6\%$ random ($p{<}0.002$), and adaptive recomputation matches within $\mathbf{0.1}$\,pp. On IEEE power-flow benchmarks $S_c$ matches N-1 contingency rankings on case14--118 ($\mathbf{P@10}{=}\mathbf{0.66}$--$\mathbf{0.81}$); code released under tiered disclosure.
\end{abstract}
