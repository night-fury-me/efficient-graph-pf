\begin{abstract}
Graph neural networks now inform safety-critical decisions in drug-interaction screening, fraud detection, and power-grid contingency analysis, where adversarial perturbations of the graph can flip predictions. Auditing such a model means mapping its \emph{adversarial fault lines}: the most sensitive first-order perturbation direction, per-edge equilibrium-sensitivity rankings, and per-node sensitivity radii. Existing tools supply at most one. \AEGIS produces all three from a single object, the \emph{constrained sensitivity matrix} $S_c$, computed matrix-free through a Neumann-series resolvent and randomized SVD that scale to $N{=}7{,}650$ on one GPU. For the spectral-norm-constrained IGNN subclass we prove a closed-form three-regime characterisation with critical budget $\ecrit{=}(1{-}\kappa)/\norm{W}_2$ (rate sharp in the all-active case), which trained models satisfy with $\mathbf{2\text{--}4\times}$ margin. The construction extends to any continuous-edge-weight message passing (GCN, SAGE, GIN, APPNP, IGNN, edge-weighted GAT$^\dagger$): its continuous-to-discrete bridge is positive in \emph{all $\mathbf{39/39}$} cells under the edge-weighted ranking of \cref{prop:transfer} (median $\tau{=}\mathbf{+0.99}$, $p{<}10^{-5}$), beating an edge-weight baseline by $\Delta\tau$ up to $\mathbf{+0.65}$ on a fraud graph and reaching $\mathbf{+0.996}$ on Amazon Photo ($N{=}7{,}650$), while one query recovers the direction 50-step PGD finds. Masking the top edges by $v_{ij}$ cuts $\sigma_1(S_c)$ damage by $\mathbf{42{\pm}8\%}$ (versus $11\%$ for random masking) and survives adaptive recomputation. On IEEE power flow, $S_c$ recovers N-1 rankings on case14--118 ($\mathbf{P@10}{=}\mathbf{0.66}$--$\mathbf{0.81}$) from a learned surrogate, without the admittance matrix exact LODF requires.
\end{abstract}
