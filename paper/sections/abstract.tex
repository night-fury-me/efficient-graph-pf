\begin{abstract}
Graph neural networks deployed in safety-critical settings (drug interaction, fraud detection, power-grid contingency screening) are vulnerable to adversarial graph perturbations, but no existing method unifies the three diagnostic outputs a practitioner needs from a single closed-form computation: a globally maximally sensitive perturbation direction, per-edge vulnerability rankings, and per-node first-order sensitivity radii. We introduce \AEGIS, which derives all three from one object, the \emph{constrained sensitivity matrix} $S_c$, computed matrix-free via a Neumann-series resolvent and randomized SVD that scale to $N{=}7{,}650$ on a single GPU. The analytical construction applies to any GNN with continuous edge-weight-modulated message passing (GCN, SAGE, GIN, APPNP, IGNN, edge-weighted GAT$^\dagger$); predictive transfer is architecture-dependent (deeper-than-2-layer models transfer most reliably). For the spectral-norm-constrained IGNN subclass (which costs ${\sim}6\%$ test accuracy on Cora relative to unconstrained GNNs) we additionally derive a critical perturbation budget $\ecrit{=}(1-\kappa)/\norm{W}_2$ and a \emph{worst-case} three-regime characterisation along the leading sensitivity direction. The first-order envelope is tight ($1.00 \pm 0.01$) at $\varepsilon{=}0.01$ across 7 architectures and 9 datasets and loosens to $\leq 1.39$ at $\varepsilon{=}0.20$, the SVD direction inflicts $2$--$8\times$ more damage than random, and continuous-to-discrete transfer is positive in 29 of 33 architecture-dataset cells (full-graph Kendall $\tau \in [-0.04,+0.89]$). As a proof-of-concept cross-domain demonstration on IEEE power-flow benchmarks, $S_c$ scores correlate with N-1 contingency rankings on case14--118 ($\tau{=}+0.37$ to $+0.62$, P@10 $= 0.66$--$0.81$); code will be released under tiered access.
\end{abstract}
