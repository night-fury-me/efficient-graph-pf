\begin{abstract}
We introduce AEGIS, a framework that mines the equilibrium structure of Deep Equilibrium Graph Neural Networks (DEQ-GNNs) to predict adversarial vulnerability under graph structure perturbation. Unlike explicit GNNs, DEQ-GNNs converge to a fixed point $\zstar = F(\zstar, A)$, enabling construction of a structural sensitivity matrix $S = (I - J_z)^{-1} J_A$ via the implicit function theorem. We characterize three vulnerability regimes around a critical budget $\ecrit = (1-\rho)/\norm{W}_2$: below it, fixed-point shifts are bounded and certifiable; above it, contractivity is lost and certificates become void. Under realistic perturbation constraints (symmetric, edge-only), our first-order sensitivity analysis achieves tightness $1.00 \pm 0.01$ across 9 datasets (5 graph benchmarks $+$ 4 IEEE power grids, 10 seeds each), meaning the predicted adversarial shift matches the actual shift to within 1\%. AEGIS's SVD-optimal attack inflicts $20$--$50\%$ more damage than adaptive white-box PGD across all tested budgets (30/30 wins vs.\ Mettack). Its deterministic per-node certificates provide structurally differentiated robustness guarantees, unlike randomized smoothing which yields uniform constant bounds at comparable noise levels. As a case study, the vulnerability spectrum recovers power grid N-1 contingency rankings (precision@10 = $0.66$--$0.81$). Code is available at [URL].
\end{abstract}
