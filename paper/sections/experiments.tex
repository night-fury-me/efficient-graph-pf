\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
%% ===================================================================

We evaluate AEGIS on 9 datasets across 4 domains with 10 random seeds each (seeds: 42, 137, 271, 314, 1729, 2718, 3141, 5772, 6561, 9999). All graph benchmark experiments use IGNN~\cite{gu2020implicit} with hidden dimension 64, spectral-norm constrained weights, and early stopping on validation accuracy over 200 epochs. Power flow experiments use ContractiveGCN-PF (an IGNN variant for AC power flow).

\textbf{Datasets.} Graph benchmarks: Cora (2,708 nodes), Citeseer (3,327), Pubmed (19,717), Amazon Photo (7,650), WikiCS (11,701). Power flow: IEEE case14 (14 buses), case30 (30), case57 (57), case118 (118), generated via PandaPower with 2,000 samples each.

At $\sigma = 0.25$ (following standard practice~\cite{bojchevski2020efficient}), AEGIS deterministic certificates are $1.4$--$2.1\times$ larger. However, at $\sigma = 0.5$, smoothing radii match or exceed AEGIS on two of three datasets---at the cost of 10--15 percentage points in base accuracy. The key distinction is regime-dependent: AEGIS certificates are first-order and tight for small $\varepsilon$ (exact under constrained perturbations), while smoothing provides valid certificates at arbitrary perturbation magnitude but degrades the underlying classifier. For safety-critical applications requiring both high accuracy and robustness guarantees, the deterministic approach is preferable; for applications tolerating accuracy loss in exchange for large certified radii, smoothing with high $\sigma$ may be suitable.

\subsection{Adaptive Attack Evaluation}
\label{sec:adaptive}

The Mettack comparison (\cref{sec:mettack}) uses a GCN surrogate, so its failure against IGNN may reflect architectural mismatch rather than AEGIS's certificate strength. To rule this out, we implement an \emph{adaptive attacker} that differentiates through the IGNN fixed-point iteration via implicit differentiation---the same IFT gradients AEGIS uses for sensitivity analysis---and optimizes perturbations directly against the IGNN loss surface using projected gradient descent (PGD, 50 steps, step size $\varepsilon/10$).

At $\varepsilon = 0.01$ (the regime where first-order certificates are tight), the adaptive attacker breaches zero certified nodes across all datasets, confirming certificate validity. AEGIS's first-order attack inflicts slightly more damage than PGD at small $\varepsilon$ (ratio $< 1$), because the SVD-optimal direction is globally optimal to first order while PGD can get trapped in local optima. At $\varepsilon = 0.10$, the adaptive attacker exceeds AEGIS damage (ratio $1.14$ on Cora), consistent with the first-order approximation becoming conservative at larger budgets. The 8$\pm$3\% breach rate at $\varepsilon = 0.10$ on Cora is expected: certificates are first-order and explicitly not guaranteed at large perturbation magnitudes.

\subsection{Phase Transition Validation}
\label{sec:phase_transition}

To validate \cref{thm:phase_transition}, we scale the adjacency matrix to achieve target spectral radii $\rho \in \{0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.85, 0.9, 0.95, 0.99\}$ and measure the actual fixed-point shift under a random perturbation of $\varepsilon = 0.01$. The shift amplifies by $83\times$ as $\rho$ increases from 0.3 to 0.99, confirming the three-regime behavior: bounded shift in the subcritical regime, rapid growth near $\rho = 1$, and divergence in the supercritical regime.

\subsection{Hyperparameter Sensitivity}
\label{sec:hyperparams}

We evaluate sensitivity to the key hyperparameters: hidden dimension $d$ and spectral-norm constraint $c$.

\textbf{Hidden dimension.} Varying $d \in \{16, 32, 64, 128\}$ on Cora: tightness is $1.01 \pm 0.01$ for all values. Larger $d$ increases $\ecrit$ (from $0.52$ at $d=16$ to $0.71$ at $d=128$) because the spectral radius decreases with overparameterization, but median certified radius is stable ($0.053$--$0.059$).

\textbf{Spectral norm constraint.} Varying $c \in \{0.5, 0.7, 0.9, 0.95\}$: smaller $c$ yields larger $\ecrit$ and certificates but lower model accuracy. At $c = 0.95$ (near the contractivity boundary), $\rho$ reaches $0.85 \pm 0.05$ and certificates shrink by $3\times$ versus $c = 0.7$, consistent with the $1/(1-\rho)$ amplification in \cref{thm:phase_transition}.

\subsection{DEQ Convergence Diagnostics}
\label{sec:convergence}

We report convergence statistics for the IGNN fixed-point iteration across all datasets. Anderson acceleration (mixing $m=5$) achieves convergence ($\norm{Z_{k+1} - Z_k}_F < 10^{-6}$) in $15 \pm 4$ iterations on graph benchmarks and $12 \pm 3$ iterations on IEEE grids. The fixed point is verified to be a true equilibrium by checking $\norm{\zstar - F(\zstar, A)}_F < 10^{-8}$ post-convergence. On no seed across any dataset did the iteration fail to converge, consistent with the enforced contractivity constraint $\norm{W}_2 < 1$.
