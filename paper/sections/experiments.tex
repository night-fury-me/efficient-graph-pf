\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
%% ===================================================================

Our evaluation follows the three capabilities of \cref{sec:framework}. \AEGIS \emph{audits} (\cref{sec:audit}): one analytic query matches iterative attackers and its $S_c$ ranking transfers to discrete edge removal across architectures. It \emph{certifies} (\cref{sec:certify}): \AEGIS-Conformal coverage holds at the nominal level under the very attack it certifies, where randomized smoothing is vacuous. It \emph{defends} (\cref{sec:defense}): a $\sigma_1(S_c)$ penalty buys certified robustness. A deployed fraud detector (\cref{sec:fraud_case}) closes the loop on real data.

\textbf{Setup.} We evaluate \AEGIS on 6 datasets across 4 domains with 10 seeds each (PyTorch~\cite{paszke2019pytorch}); $\pm$ are seed standard deviations. IGNN~\cite{gu2020implicit} uses $F(Z){=}\mathrm{ReLU}(\Ahat ZW^\top{+}U(X))$, $W\in\R^{64\times64}$ spectral-normalised~\cite{miyato2018spectral}, Adam (lr $0.01$). Datasets: Cora, Citeseer, Pubmed~\cite{sen2008collective}, Amazon Photo~\cite{shchur2018pitfalls}, WikiCS~\cite{mernyei2020wiki}, Amazon Fraud~\cite{dou2020enhancing}; baselines: smoothing (Gaussian $\sigma{=}0.01$), Mettack, GR-BCD/PR-BCD~\cite{geisler2021robustness}. All seven architectures appear only in \cref{fig:tau_heatmap} and \cref{tab:explicit}; the rest use IGNN. Full tables, ablations, and timing are in \cref{app:experiments}; reproducibility in \cref{app:repro}.

\subsection{Audit: One Query Finds the Worst Attack}
\label{sec:audit}\label{sec:adaptive}\label{sec:certificates}\label{sec:cross_domain}

Two prerequisites must hold: the trained models sit in the contractive regime, and \AEGIS's perturbations beat chance. \Cref{tab:cross_domain} confirms both, verifying (A3) on every benchmark ($\kappa{<}1$) and reporting per-dataset $\ecrit$ alongside a $3.2$--$4.1\times$ attack advantage over random. Unless stated otherwise, IGNN experiments use 50-node BFS ego-subgraphs (highest-degree centre) reporting \emph{local vulnerability}, graph-level assessment using the matrix-free pipeline (\cref{app:phase_scal}); all bounds use $\kappa{=}\norm{J_z}_2$.

\textbf{One-query attack.} \AEGIS's attack is one-query and analytic, not a peak optimiser: the SVD direction maximises first-order equilibrium shift by construction (\cref{prop:attack}). A transfer direction from a \emph{separately trained} surrogate (zero shared gradients) recovers $99\%$ of the one-query damage ($\cos{=}0.99$), versus $44{\pm}4\%$ for a $512$-query black-box search, so it is model-intrinsic, not a gradient artifact. Per query it delivers $\mathbf{74}$--$\mathbf{156\times}$ the \emph{equilibrium-shift} damage of a $50$-step PGD~\cite{madry2018towards} (\cref{tab:attack_full}); classification-loss PGD inflicts $15$--$70\%$ less at $50\times$ the cost, and GR-BCD leads only at the larger budgets it targets (\cref{app:baselines}). Per-edge finite differences reproduce the $S_c$ column-norm ranking ($\tau{=}0.999$), and iterative recomputation closes $\leq3$\,pp of the static-vs-greedy gap at $k{\times}$ compute, so the static ranking is the headline.

\AEGIS also beats degree-proportional, edge-betweenness, and spectral rankings at $\varepsilon{=}0.01$ ($+6$--$148\%$ AtkAdv) and inflicts $3$--$10\times$ more equilibrium damage than Mettack~\cite{zugner2019adversarial} in the early-warning regime ($\mathbf{149/150}$ paired wins, $p{<}10^{-43}$); the discrete-removal curves vs.\ a label-aware Greedy attacker are in \cref{fig:greedy_topk}, with GR-BCD/PR-BCD detail in \cref{app:baselines}. The first-order shift bounds agree with actual shifts within $1\%$ at small budgets, loosening to ${\leq}39\%$ on citation graphs at $\varepsilon{=}0.20$.

\textbf{Breach rates.} Every breached node satisfies $\varepsilon > r_v$ across all datasets and budgets, validating $r_v$ as a reliable first-order screen (\cref{sec:certify}). \Cref{fig:breach} reports prediction-flip fractions vs.\ $\varepsilon$ ($95\%$ CI bands): Citeseer and WikiCS stay below $2\%$ throughout; Cora reaches $7.6\%$ at $\varepsilon{=}0.20$; Pubmed is the right-skewed outlier (mean $10.3\%$ at $\varepsilon{=}0.10$, $27.4\%$ at $\varepsilon{=}0.20$).

\textbf{Continuous-to-discrete transfer.}\label{sec:explicit_extension} The construction is not tied to implicit models: for $K$-layer explicit GNNs (GCN, SAGE, GIN, APPNP, GAT$^\dagger$~\cite{kipf2017semi,hamilton2017inductive,xu2019how,klicpera2019predict,velickovic2018graph}) we form $S_K{=}\partial \mathrm{vec}(Z_K)/\partial \mathrm{vec}(A)$ via finite differences and build $S_c$ identically (\cref{prop:explicit}); IGNN additionally carries \cref{thm:phase_transition}. Does the continuous $S_c$ ranking predict discrete N-1 removal? Ranking edges by the edge-weighted $A_{ij}v_{ij}$ of \cref{prop:transfer}, \cref{fig:tau_heatmap} reports the Kendall $\tau$ over 6 datasets and 7 architectures (390 runs): \emph{all $\mathbf{39/39}$ cells positive}, median $\tau{=}\mathbf{+0.99}$ ($p{<}10^{-5}$), vs.\ $34/39$ unweighted. On full-graph Amazon Photo ($N{=}7{,}650$, $\kappa{\approx}1.00$) it reaches $\tau\!=\!\mathbf{+0.996\!\pm\!0.002}$ (out of contractive scope, yet still transferring). Per-architecture detail, the phase-transition and scalability sweeps (matrix-free reaching $N{=}7{,}650$), and ablations---where vulnerability proves \emph{delocalized}, localising risk for triage rather than removing it---are in \cref{app:explicit,app:phase_scal,app:ablations}.

%% ----------------------------- CERTIFY -----------------------------
\subsection{Certify: Distribution-Free Coverage}
\label{sec:certify}

The certificate of \cref{sec:conformal} holds empirically. \Cref{tab:conformal} reports coverage over 10 seeds and four datasets. The \emph{gate}---coverage under the worst-case \AEGIS attack at magnitude $\varepsilon$ (reconverging the equilibrium after the $v_1$ perturbation)---holds at the nominal $1{-}\alpha{=}0.90$ at $\varepsilon{=}0.01$ on every dataset and is conservative ($0.94$--$1.00$) at $\varepsilon{=}0.05$, with \emph{zero} equilibrium divergence across all gate nodes. Sets run ${\sim}1.0$--$1.5$ labels (citation graphs) to ${\sim}3.5$ (WikiCS, $10$ classes), non-vacuous throughout and tightest where the deterministic radius is thin, at zero Monte-Carlo samples. Exchangeability on a single transductive graph is a stated condition---the empirical gate is the evidence---and $S_c$ is formed densely at $N{=}200$, a matrix-free conformal pass being future work.

On the same Frobenius $\varepsilon$-ball, randomized smoothing is \emph{vacuous}: the matched $\sigma{=}\varepsilon/\sqrt{2|E|}$ leaves the smoothed classifier unchanged, abstaining to the full label set (coverage $1.00$, zero certified nodes, zero seed variance). \AEGIS-Conformal stays non-vacuous at $\mathbf{11{,}700}$--$\mathbf{16{,}700\times}$ lower wall-clock; even on a strictly larger per-coordinate ball where smoothing finally certifies, it costs $23{,}000$--$57{,}000\times$ the zero-sample $S_c$ bound (\cref{tab:smoothing}, \cref{app:smoothing}).

\subsection{Defend: A $\sigma_1(S_c)$ Robustness Knob}
\label{sec:defense}

The operator that finds the worst attack also tunes the defense. Penalizing $\sigma_1(S_c)$ during training drives the dominant sensitivity down and, with it, worst-case attack damage down and the certified fraction up (\cref{tab:defense}); the operating point $\lambda{=}3{\times}10^{-4}$ cuts $\sigma_1$ ${\sim}10\times$ ($\ms{319}{59}\!\to\!\ms{32.6}{1.7}$) and raises the certified fraction to $0.82{\pm}0.03$ at a ${\sim}4$-point clean-accuracy cost. The trade is genuinely \emph{certified}, not empirical: at $\varepsilon{\leq}0.1$ few nodes flip for either model (the delocalized vulnerability above), so the penalty raises the certified margin, not small-budget accuracy. Attack and defense couple through $S_c$: across 10 seeds the margin-controlled attack magnitude and certified radius are anticorrelated ($-0.65{\pm}0.12$, negative in $\mathbf{10/10}$ seeds, $p{\approx}10^{-160}$).
