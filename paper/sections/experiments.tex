\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
%% ===================================================================

Our evaluation follows the three capabilities of \cref{sec:framework}: \AEGIS \emph{audits} (\cref{sec:audit}), \emph{certifies} (\cref{sec:certify}), and \emph{defends} (\cref{sec:defense}); a deployed fraud detector (\cref{sec:fraud_case}) then closes the loop on real data.

\textbf{Setup.} We evaluate \AEGIS on 6 datasets across 4 domains, 10 seeds each (PyTorch~\cite{paszke2019pytorch}; $\pm$ are seed std). The IGNN~\cite{gu2020implicit} of \cref{eq:ignn_operator} uses $W\in\R^{64\times64}$ spectral-normalised~\cite{miyato2018spectral} with Adam (lr $0.01$). Datasets: Cora, Citeseer, Pubmed~\cite{sen2008collective}, Amazon Photo~\cite{shchur2018pitfalls}, WikiCS~\cite{mernyei2020wiki}, Amazon Fraud~\cite{dou2020enhancing}; baselines: smoothing (Gaussian $\sigma{=}0.01$), Mettack, GR-BCD/PR-BCD~\cite{geisler2021robustness}. Non-IGNN architectures appear only in \cref{fig:tau_heatmap,tab:explicit}. Full tables, ablations, and timing in \cref{app:experiments}; reproducibility in \cref{app:repro}.

\subsection{Audit: One Query Finds the Worst Attack}
\label{sec:audit}\label{sec:adaptive}\label{sec:certificates}\label{sec:cross_domain}

Two prerequisites must hold: the trained models sit in the contractive regime, and \AEGIS's perturbations beat chance. \Cref{tab:cross_domain} confirms both, verifying (A3) on every benchmark ($\kappa{<}1$) and reporting per-dataset $\ecrit$ with a $3.2$--$4.1\times$ attack advantage over random. Unless stated otherwise, IGNN runs on 50-node BFS ego-subgraphs around the highest-degree node and reports \emph{local} vulnerability; graph-level assessment uses the matrix-free pipeline (\cref{app:phase_scal}), and all bounds use $\kappa{=}\norm{J_z}_2$.

\textbf{One query, not a search.} \AEGIS spends its whole budget in one analytic query: the leading singular direction of $S_c$ is, by construction, the perturbation that moves the equilibrium most (\cref{prop:attack}), with no optimisation loop. The worry with a closed-form attack is that it exploits the victim's gradients rather than a real fault line; three checks rule this out. \emph{It transfers:} a direction from a \emph{separately trained} surrogate (no shared gradients) still recovers $99\%$ of the one-query damage ($\cos{=}0.99$), against $44{\pm}4\%$ for a $512$-query black-box search, so the fault line belongs to the model, not to gradient access. \emph{It is efficient:} in \cref{tab:attack_full} one query matches or beats a $50$-step PGD~\cite{madry2018towards} at $\varepsilon{=}0.10$, a $\mathbf{74}$--$\mathbf{156\times}$ advantage \emph{per query}; only GR-BCD overtakes it, at the large budgets it targets (\cref{app:baselines}). \emph{It is faithful:} per-edge finite differences reproduce the $S_c$ ranking almost exactly ($\tau{=}0.999$), and re-solving after every removal (the greedy ideal) closes at most $3$\,pp of the gap, so the one-shot ranking is the headline.

\AEGIS also beats degree, edge-betweenness, and spectral rankings at $\varepsilon{=}0.01$ ($+6$--$148\%$ AtkAdv) and inflicts $3$--$10\times$ more equilibrium damage than Mettack~\cite{zugner2019adversarial} in the early-warning regime ($\mathbf{149/150}$ wins, $p{<}10^{-43}$); discrete-removal curves vs.\ label-aware Greedy are in \cref{fig:greedy_topk} (GR-BCD/PR-BCD in \cref{app:baselines}). The first-order bounds match actual shifts to within $1\%$ at small budgets, loosening to ${\leq}39\%$ on citation graphs by $\varepsilon{=}0.20$.

\textbf{Breach rates.} Every breached node satisfies $\varepsilon > r_v$ across all datasets and budgets, validating $r_v$ as a first-order screen (\cref{sec:certify}); flip fractions (\cref{fig:breach}) stay below $2\%$ on Citeseer/WikiCS and reach $7.6\%$ (Cora) and a right-skewed $27.4\%$ (Pubmed) at $\varepsilon{=}0.20$.

\textbf{Continuous-to-discrete transfer.}\label{sec:explicit_extension} The bridges of \cref{sec:transfer_explicit} extend the construction beyond implicit models: for seven $K$-layer architectures (GCN, SAGE, GIN, APPNP, GAT$^\dagger$~\cite{kipf2017semi,hamilton2017inductive,xu2019how,klicpera2019predict,velickovic2018graph}) we form $S_K$ by finite differences (\cref{prop:explicit}) and ask whether the continuous $S_c$ ranking predicts the discrete brute-force N-1 removal order. Ranking by the edge-weighted score $A_{ij}v_{ij}$ (\cref{prop:transfer}), \cref{fig:tau_heatmap} reports Kendall $\tau$ over 6 datasets and 7 architectures (390 runs): all $\mathbf{39/39}$ cells positive, median $\tau{=}\mathbf{+0.99}$ ($p{<}10^{-5}$), vs.\ $34/39$ unweighted; on full-graph Amazon Photo ($N{=}7{,}650$, $\kappa{\approx}1.00$) it reaches $\tau\!=\!\mathbf{+0.996\!\pm\!0.002}$. Per-architecture detail, the sweeps, and ablations are in \cref{app:explicit,app:phase_scal,app:ablations}; the ablations show vulnerability is \emph{delocalized}, so $S_c$ localises risk for triage rather than removing it.

\subsection{Certify: Distribution-Free Coverage}
\label{sec:certify}

The certificate of \cref{sec:conformal} holds empirically. \Cref{tab:conformal} reports coverage over 10 seeds and four datasets. The crucial quantity is the \emph{gate}: coverage retained under the worst-case \AEGIS attack at magnitude $\varepsilon$. It sits at the nominal $1{-}\alpha{=}0.90$ at $\varepsilon{=}0.01$ and turns conservative ($0.94$--$1.00$) at $\varepsilon{=}0.05$, with sets that stay informative (${\sim}1.0$--$3.5$ labels) and tighten where the deterministic radius is thin, at zero Monte-Carlo samples. Two caveats: exchangeability on a single transductive graph is an assumption the gate evidences empirically, and $S_c$ is formed densely at $N{=}200$, leaving a matrix-free pass to future work.

\subsection{Defend: A $\sigma_1(S_c)$ Robustness Knob}
\label{sec:defense}

The operator that finds the worst attack also tunes the defense. Penalizing $\sigma_1(S_c)$ during training drives the dominant sensitivity down, and with it worst-case attack damage falls while the certified fraction rises (\cref{tab:defense}). At $\lambda{=}3{\times}10^{-4}$ this cuts $\sigma_1$ by ${\sim}10\times$ and lifts the certified fraction to $0.82{\pm}0.03$ for ${\sim}4$ accuracy points. The gain is genuinely \emph{certified}, not empirical: at $\varepsilon{\leq}0.1$ few nodes flip for either model, so the penalty buys a larger certified margin, not better small-budget accuracy. Attack and defense thus couple through one operator: across 10 seeds the attack magnitude and certified radius are anticorrelated ($-0.65{\pm}0.12$, negative in $\mathbf{10/10}$, $p{\approx}10^{-160}$).
