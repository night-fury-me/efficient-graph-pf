\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
%% ===================================================================

Our evaluation tests four claims in turn: the contractivity assumptions hold on trained models (\cref{sec:cross_domain}); the one-query SVD direction matches iterative attackers (\cref{sec:adaptive}); the continuous $S_c$ ranking transfers to discrete edge removal across architectures (\cref{sec:explicit_extension}); and the same object screens power-grid contingencies (\cref{sec:power_flow}).

\textbf{Setup.} We evaluate \AEGIS on 10 datasets across 5 domains with 10 random seeds each (PyTorch~\cite{paszke2019pytorch}); reported $\pm$ are standard deviations over seeds. IGNN~\cite{gu2020implicit} uses $F(Z){=}\mathrm{ReLU}(\Ahat ZW^\top{+}U(X))$ with $W\in\R^{64\times64}$ spectral-normalised~\cite{miyato2018spectral} and Adam (lr $0.01$); ContractiveGCN-PF mirrors this for power flow. The spectral-norm constraint costs ${\sim}6\%$ Cora accuracy. Datasets: Cora, Citeseer, Pubmed~\cite{sen2008collective}, Amazon Photo~\cite{shchur2018pitfalls}, WikiCS~\cite{mernyei2020wiki}, Amazon Fraud~\cite{dou2020enhancing}, IEEE 14/30/57/118~\cite{ieee2024testcases,thurner2018pandapower}. Baselines: smoothing (Gaussian $\sigma{=}0.01$), Mettack (Meta-Self GCN surrogate), LODF as $\mathrm{PTDF}_{ij}/(1{-}\mathrm{PTDF}_{jj})$ from the DC $B$-matrix~\cite[Ch.~11]{wood2014power}. Coverage is not uniform across studies: all seven architectures appear only in \cref{tab:explicit} and \cref{fig:tau_heatmap} (39 cells), while the other classification studies use IGNN and the power-flow study uses the four IEEE cases.

\subsection{Cross-Domain Adversarial Analysis}
\label{sec:cross_domain}

Two things must hold before the rest of the evaluation means anything: the trained models should sit in the contractive regime the theory assumes, and \AEGIS's perturbations should be genuinely more damaging than chance. \Cref{tab:cross_domain} confirms both, verifying (A3) on every benchmark ($\kappa{<}1$) and reporting the per-dataset closed-form $\ecrit$ alongside an attack advantage of $3.2$--$4.1\times$ over random perturbations. Unless otherwise stated, IGNN experiments in \cref{sec:cross_domain}--\cref{sec:defense_ablation} use 50-node BFS ego-subgraphs centred on the highest-degree node and report \emph{local vulnerability}; graph-level assessment uses the matrix-free pipeline of \cref{sec:scalability}. All bounds use the operator-norm $\kappa{=}\norm{J_z}_2$.

\AEGIS's shift bounds are first-order, so a natural question is how far they hold as the budget grows. Predicted and actual equilibrium shifts agree to within $1\%$ at the small budgets that matter for early warning, and loosen to at most $39\%$ on the citation graphs (within $7\%$ on WikiCS and Amazon) even at $\varepsilon{=}0.20$. This envelope ratio is measured on the equilibrium shift $\norm{\Delta\zstar}_F$, distinct from the prediction-flip (breach) rate of \cref{fig:breach}.

\textbf{Heuristic and surrogate baselines.} \AEGIS beats degree-proportional, edge-betweenness, and spectral rankings at $\varepsilon{=}0.01$ ($+6$--$148\%$ AtkAdv, Wilcoxon $p{<}0.001$) and inflicts $3$--$10\times$ more equilibrium damage than Mettack~\cite{zugner2019adversarial} at the early-warning regime $k\!\in\!\{1,\ldots,5\}$ ($\mathbf{149/150}$ paired wins, $p{<}10^{-43}$); GR-BCD/PR-BCD~\cite{geisler2021robustness} and the discrete-removal curves are in \cref{tab:baselines,fig:greedy_topk}.

\subsection{Four-Quadrant Attack Comparison}
\label{sec:adaptive}\label{sec:certificates}

\textbf{Radius vs.\ smoothing.} Randomized smoothing~\cite{bojchevski2020efficient} gives constant-per-node base radii ($0.123$ vs.\ \AEGIS's $0.078$ at $\sigma{=}0.05$), and localized smoothing~\cite{schuchardt2023localized} needs $10^3$--$10^4$ MC/node; both lack edge structure. \AEGIS radii are instead structurally informative ($r_v{\approx}0.10$ in dense regions, $0.01$ at boundaries), a complementary point on the certificate-versus-diagnostic frontier.

\Cref{tab:attack_full} evaluates \AEGIS across the four quadrants of (gradient-based, gradient-free) $\times$ (equilibrium-shift, classification-loss). The SVD direction maximises first-order equilibrium shift \emph{by construction} (\cref{prop:attack}); what matters is non-circularity and cost. A transfer direction from a separately trained surrogate (zero shared gradients) recovers $99\%$ of the one-query damage ($\cos{=}0.99$), versus $44{\pm}4\%$ for a $512$-query black-box search: model-intrinsic, not a gradient artifact. Per query it delivers $74$--$156\times$ the damage of a $50$-step PGD~\cite{madry2018towards}; classification-loss PGD inflicts $15$--$70\%$ less at $50\times$ the cost, IFT-gradient PGD (solver validation, not independent) $72$--$92\%$, and prediction \emph{flips} stay $0$--$1.8\%$ for all methods. \AEGIS is a one-query \emph{diagnostic}, not a peak attacker: budget-heavy attackers (GR-BCD, \cref{tab:baselines}) exceed it at larger budgets.

\textbf{Classification-gradient edges differ.} Per-edge classification gradients anti-correlate with discrete ground truth on Cora ($\tau{=}\ms{-0.20}{0.05}$ vs.\ \AEGIS's $\ms{+0.32}{0.04}$) and stay near zero elsewhere; \AEGIS achieves $2$--$6\times$ higher P@10, confirming that equilibrium and classification sensitivities are distinct vulnerability surfaces.

Per-edge finite differences reproduce the $S_c$ column-norm ranking ($\tau{=}0.999$); iterative $S_c$ recomputation closes $\leq 3$\,pp of the static-vs-greedy gap at $k{\times}$ compute, so the static ranking is the headline.

\textbf{Breach rates.} Every breached node satisfies $\varepsilon > r_v$ across all datasets and budgets, validating $r_v$ as a reliable first-order screening threshold (\cref{rem:certificates}). \Cref{fig:breach} reports prediction-flip fractions across $\varepsilon$ (bands are $95\%$ CIs): Citeseer and WikiCS stay below $2\%$ throughout; Cora reaches $7.6\%$ (CI $[2.2,13.0]$) at $\varepsilon{=}0.20$; Pubmed is the right-skewed outlier (median $7.8\%$, mean $10.3\%$ at $\varepsilon{=}0.10$; $27.4\%$, CI $[12.3,42.5]$, at $\varepsilon{=}0.20$).

GR-BCD~\cite{geisler2021robustness} aligns with $S_c$ on Pubmed ($\tau{=}{+}0.69$) but decorrelates on Cora ($\tau{=}{+}0.16$); the budgets shown ($k{\leq}10$) are \AEGIS's early-warning regime, where its one-pass diagnostic is competitive, while GR-BCD/PR-BCD dominate raw damage at the high budgets they target (\cref{tab:baselines}). Power-grid LODF/PI comparisons are in \cref{sec:power_flow}.

\subsection{Phase Transition and Scalability}
\label{sec:phase_transition}\label{sec:scalability}

\textbf{Phase transition.} \Cref{fig:phase_transition} sweeps $\kappa_{\max}{\in}[0.30,0.99]$ on Cora (10 seeds). Even when the spectral-normalisation cap is pushed to $0.99$, driving the theoretical $\ecrit$ down $230\times$, the trained ReLU pattern keeps $\rho(J_z)\!\leq\!0.42$, so the resolvent grows only $\mathbf{1.17{\to}1.80}$. This \emph{validates} $\ecrit$ as a closed-form sufficient safety boundary, one that holds with $\mathbf{2{-}4\times}$ empirical margin across the sweep. Training converges throughout, and the matrix-free pipeline reaches its 50-step fixed-point budget at $\kappa_{\max}{\geq}0.85$.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig_phase_transition.pdf}
\caption{Three-regime characterisation of \cref{thm:phase_transition}. The empirical $\kappa$ band (grey) saturates well below the spectral-normalisation ceiling, so the empirical resolvent growth is far milder than the worst-case $1/(1-\kappa)$ divergence at $\varepsilon \to \ecrit$.}
\label{fig:phase_transition}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Scalability and matrix-free self-consistency.} \Cref{fig:scalability} compares dense and matrix-free pipelines. The matrix-free $S_c v$ implements the dense $S_c$ up to Neumann truncation; $\sigma_1$ agrees within $0.03\%$ at $N{=}200$ (per-edge $\tau{=}0.999$), and the analytical truncation residual $\kappa^{200}\!\in\![10^{-105},10^{-48}]$ across the suite (consistent with the trained-IGNN range $\kappa\!\in\![0.14,0.59]$ of \cref{tab:cross_domain}). For larger $N$ the rSVD error~\cite{halko2011finding} is bounded by the spectral gap; Pubmed ($N{=}19{,}717$) exceeds $24$\,GB.


\subsection{Ablations and Defense}
\label{sec:subgraph_ablation}\label{sec:hyperparams}\label{sec:convergence}\label{sec:defense_ablation}

\textbf{Subgraph size.} Varying $N{\in}\{30,50,100,200\}$ on Cora (10 seeds): tightness is stable at $1.01$--$1.02$ for $N{\leq}100$ and degrades to $1.031$ at $N{=}200$; we use $N{=}50$ as default ($66\times$ faster). Subgraph-vs-full-graph agreement is high on dense grids (case14 $\tau{=}0.80$--$0.86$).

\textbf{Full-graph scale.} A 50-node BFS covers only ${\sim}1.8\%$ of a citation graph's edges (Cora $\tau{=}0.16$), so at this scale we run \AEGIS on the full graph. There its edge advantage over degree-proportional ranking \emph{amplifies} to $\mathbf{9.82\times}$ (Citeseer) and $\mathbf{3.25\times}$ (Cora) at $k{=}10$, against ${\approx}1.1\times$ on subgraphs.

\textbf{Hyperparameters.} Tightness is stable across $d{\in}\{16,32,64,128\}$; the spectral-norm ceiling $c{\in}\{0.5,0.9\}$ traces the accuracy--robustness frontier ($r_v{=}0.147$@$72.1\%$ vs.\ $0.089$@$80.6\%$).

\textbf{Defense-informed edge protection: gains survive adaptive attack.} Masking the top-$k$ edges by $v_{ij}$ (Cora IGNN, $N{=}50$, 10 seeds) cuts $\sigma_1(S_c)$-damage by $\mathbf{42{\pm}8\%}$ at $k{=}5$ and $\mathbf{61{\pm}7\%}$ at $k{=}10$, vs.\ $11{\pm}6\%$/$18{\pm}9\%$ for random masking (paired Wilcoxon $p{<}0.002$). An \emph{adaptive} attacker that recomputes $S_c$ on the masked graph matches the non-adaptive attacker within $0.1$\,pp (10 seeds, $\varepsilon{=}0.10$): the $43\%$ spectral gap of $S_c$ (\cref{fig:sc_heatmap}) keeps the dominant direction robust, so the defense \emph{does not erode under recomputation}, the failure mode that sober-look critiques~\cite{mujkanovic2022defenses} flag.

\subsection{Extension to Explicit GNNs}
\label{sec:explicit_extension}

The construction is not tied to implicit models. For $K$-layer explicit GNNs (GCN, SAGE, GIN, APPNP, GAT$^\dagger$~\cite{kipf2017semi,hamilton2017inductive,xu2019how,klicpera2019predict,velickovic2018graph}) we form $S_K{=}\partial \mathrm{vec}(Z_K)/\partial \mathrm{vec}(A)$ via finite differences and build $S_c$ from $S_K$ identically (\cref{prop:explicit}). \Cref{tab:explicit} reports the per-architecture characterisation on Cora: IGNN carries \cref{thm:phase_transition}; the explicit models receive the computational tool without the regime characterisation.

\textbf{GAT$^\dagger$ scope.} Standard GAT uses $A$ as a binary mask, so $\partial Z/\partial A_{ij}{=}0$ on existing edges and $S_c$ is undefined; our GAT$^\dagger$ modulates attention by the edge weight ($h_i' = \sum_{j} \Ahat_{ij} \alpha_{ij} W h_j$), restoring differentiability and yielding tightness $0.99$, AtkAdv $4.4\times$, $\tau{=}+0.36$. Binary-mask architectures (hard attention, max/min aggregation, GATv2~\cite{brody2022how}-style dynamic-attention masking) fall outside the framework.

\textbf{Robust backbones.} Under the $\sigma_1(W){\leq}0.9$ cap, RobustGCN-lite~\cite{zhu2019robust} and GNNGuard-lite~\cite{zhang2020gnnguard} match or exceed IGNN; the cap is a precondition (without it $\kappa{>}1$ voids \cref{thm:phase_transition}).

\textbf{Cross-dataset transfer.} We rank edges by the score \cref{prop:transfer} predicts, the edge-weighted $A_{ij}v_{ij}$. \Cref{fig:tau_heatmap} reports its $\tau$ over 6 datasets and 7 architectures (390 runs): \emph{all $39/39$ cells positive}, median $\tau{=}\mathbf{+0.99}$ (sign test $p{<}10^{-5}$), vs.\ $34/39$ for unweighted $v_{ij}$. It beats a pure edge-weight baseline by $\Delta\tau{\approx}\mathbf{+0.25}$, rising to $\mathbf{+0.65}$ on the fraud graph where uniform weights are uninformative, so $v_{ij}$ adds rank information beyond the weight. Weighting matters most at full-graph scale: on Amazon Photo ($N{=}7{,}650$; full-graph $\kappa{\approx}1.00$, marginally non-contractive, so empirical) it reaches $\tau\!=\!\mathbf{+0.996\!\pm\!0.002}$ (stratified top-$v_{ij}$ N-1). GAT-2$^\dagger$ is the exception: attention reweights edges, so the unweighted $v_{ij}$ transfers better there ($+0.47$ vs.\ $+0.35$).
