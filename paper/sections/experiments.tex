\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
%% ===================================================================

\textbf{Setup.} We evaluate \AEGIS on 9 datasets across 4 domains with 10 random seeds each (PyTorch~\cite{paszke2019pytorch}). IGNN~\cite{gu2020implicit} uses $F(Z){=}\mathrm{ReLU}(\Ahat ZW^\top{+}U(X))$ with $W\in\R^{64\times64}$ spectral-normalised~\cite{miyato2018spectral} and Adam (lr $0.01$); ContractiveGCN-PF mirrors this with 5 bus features and 2-head readout. Subgraphs: 50-node BFS from the highest-degree node. The spectral-norm constraint costs IGNN ${\sim}6\%$ accuracy ($77.5\%$ vs.\ ${\sim}83\%$ on Cora), the price of the guarantees in \cref{thm:phase_transition}. Datasets: Cora, Citeseer, Pubmed~\cite{sen2008collective}, Amazon Photo~\cite{shchur2018pitfalls}, WikiCS~\cite{mernyei2020wiki}, IEEE 14/30/57/118~\cite{ieee2024testcases,thurner2018pandapower}. Baselines: smoothing (Gaussian $\sigma{=}0.01$, 200 MC, Clopper--Pearson $\alpha{=}0.001$), Mettack with Meta-Self GCN surrogate, LODF as $\mathrm{PTDF}_{ij}/(1{-}\mathrm{PTDF}_{jj})$ from the DC $B$-matrix~\cite[Ch.~11]{wood2014power}. Code and checkpoints will be released under tiered access (attack-generation behind institutional-affiliation review).

\subsection{Four-Quadrant Attack Comparison}
\label{sec:adaptive}\label{sec:certificates}

\textbf{Radius vs.\ smoothing.} Against randomized smoothing~\cite{bojchevski2020efficient} ($\sigma{\in}\{0.01,0.05,0.10,0.15\}$, 1000 MC samples, 10 seeds), base smoothing radii are constant per node ($r{=}\sigma{\cdot}\Phi^{-1}(p_\mathrm{lower})$, e.g.\ $0.123$ vs.\ \AEGIS's $0.046$ at $\sigma{=}0.05$) and offer no per-edge differentiation; localized smoothing~\cite{schuchardt2023localized} gives per-node deterministic-radius probabilistic certificates at $10^3$--$10^4$ MC samples per node but still lacks an edge structure or attack direction. \AEGIS radii are structurally informative ($r_v{\approx}0.09$ in dense regions, $0.01$ at boundaries), placing the three methods at complementary points on the certificate-vs-diagnostic frontier.

\Cref{tab:attack_full} evaluates \AEGIS against baselines spanning the four quadrants of (gradient-based, gradient-free) $\times$ (equilibrium-shift, classification-loss). Three observations follow: $1{,}000$ random unit directions reach only $0.45$--$0.49\times$ of $\sigma_1(S_c)$, confirming the SVD direction is well separated; an equilibrium-shift PGD attacker~\cite{madry2018towards} with \AEGIS's IFT gradients (50 steps) recovers only $72$--$92\%$ of the closed-form damage; and a classification-loss PGD through the unrolled iteration inflicts $15$--$70\%$ less equilibrium damage while flipping predictions comparably ($0$--$1.8\%$), so the SVD direction flips predictions effectively even when optimising a proxy. We therefore describe the SVD direction as the \emph{maximally sensitive perturbation direction} rather than ``the optimal attack''.

GR-BCD~\cite{geisler2021robustness} converges with $S_c$ on Pubmed ($\tau{=}{+}0.69$) and diverges on Cora ($\tau{=}{+}0.16$), the dataset-level $\tau$ acting as a self-diagnostic; the labelled attacker dominates \AEGIS on Cora absolute damage by $1.9\times$ at $k{=}5$, as expected for a label-aware attacker vs.\ a label-free closed-form proxy. PR-BCD~\cite{geisler2021robustness} (same family, larger OGB-scale budget) is a natural follow-up benchmark beyond our matrix-free boundary ($N{\approx}7{,}650$). LODF~\cite{wood2014power} retargeted to thermal-overload peaks at $\mathrm{P@10}{=}0.60$ on case57 and collapses on case118; the Ejebe--Wollenberg PI~\cite{ejebe1979automatic} is positively correlated but loses to \AEGIS on both grids.

\subsection{Phase Transition and Scalability}
\label{sec:phase_transition}\label{sec:scalability}

\textbf{Phase transition.} \Cref{fig:phase_transition} sweeps $\kappa_{\max}{\in}[0.30,0.99]$ on Cora (10 seeds each): the theoretical $\ecrit$ drops $230\times$ but $\rho(J_z)$ saturates at ${\approx}0.42$ (the ReLU pattern blocks the Jacobian from its theoretical maximum), so the resolvent rises only $1.17{\to}1.80$ instead of the ${\sim}70\times$ that $1/(1-0.99)$ predicts. The phase transition is therefore a theoretical safety boundary, not an empirically observable discontinuity; training converges throughout the sweep, though the post-hoc \AEGIS analysis exceeds the 50-step fixed-point budget at $\kappa_{\max}{\geq}0.85$.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig_phase_transition.pdf}
\caption{Three-regime characterisation of \cref{thm:phase_transition}. The empirical $\kappa$ band (grey) saturates well below the spectral-normalisation ceiling, so the empirical resolvent growth is far milder than the worst-case $1/(1-\kappa)$ divergence at $\varepsilon \to \ecrit$.}
\label{fig:phase_transition}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Scalability and matrix-free self-consistency.} \Cref{fig:scalability} compares dense and matrix-free pipelines. The matrix-free $S_c v$ implements the dense $S_c$ up to Neumann truncation; $\sigma_1$ agrees within $0.03\%$ at $N{=}200$ and per-edge $\tau{=}0.999$. For larger $N$ the rSVD error~\cite{halko2011finding} is bounded by the spectral gap. Pubmed ($N{=}19{,}717$) exceeds $24$\,GB.


\subsection{Ablations and Defense}
\label{sec:subgraph_ablation}\label{sec:hyperparams}\label{sec:convergence}\label{sec:defense_ablation}

\textbf{Subgraph size.} Varying $N{\in}\{30,50,100,200\}$ on Cora (10 seeds): tightness is stable at $1.01$--$1.02$ for $N{\leq}100$, degrades to $1.031$ at $N{=}200$, coverage stays $82$--$89\%$. We use $N{=}50$ as default ($66\times$ faster than $N{=}200$, tightness $1.013{\pm}0.003$). Subgraph-vs-full-graph agreement is high on dense grids (case14 $\tau{=}0.80$--$0.86$ at $N_\mathrm{sub}{=}8$--$12$; case30 $\tau{=}0.78{\pm}0.07$) but low on citation-scale graphs (Cora $\tau{=}0.16{\pm}0.13$ since a 50-node BFS covers ${\sim}1.8\%$ of edges), so citation-scale users should prefer the full-graph pipeline. BFS-centre choice barely matters ($\tau{=}0.95{\pm}0.02$ across highest-vs-median-degree).

\textbf{Hyperparameters.} Tightness is stable across hidden width $d \in \{16,32,64,128\}$; the spectral-norm ceiling $c{\in}\{0.5,0.9\}$ traces the accuracy-robustness frontier ($r_v{=}0.090$ at $72.1\%$ acc vs.\ $r_v{=}0.051$ at $80.6\%$). All runs converge.

\textbf{Defense-informed edge protection (non-adaptive).} Masking the top-$k$ edges by $v_{ij}$ before re-running the SVD attack on the reduced $S_c$ (Cora IGNN, $N{=}50$, 10 seeds) reduces SVD damage by $42{\pm}8\%$ at $k{=}5$ vs.\ $11{\pm}6\%$ for random masking, and by $61{\pm}7\%$ vs.\ $18{\pm}9\%$ at $k{=}10$ (paired Wilcoxon $p{<}0.002$, all 10 seeds favour vulnerability-guided masking). The attacker is held fixed (same $v_1$ as the unmasked graph); an adaptive attacker recomputing $S_c$ on the masked graph would partially erode the gain, which we leave to future work.

\textbf{GAT$^\dagger$ scope.} Standard GAT uses $A$ as a binary mask, so $\partial Z/\partial A_{ij}{=}0$ on existing edges and $S_c$ is undefined. Our GAT$^\dagger$ variant modulates attention by the edge weight,
\begin{equation}
h_i' = \textstyle\sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} \Ahat_{ij} \cdot \alpha_{ij} \cdot W h_j,
\end{equation}
restoring continuous differentiability with respect to $\Ahat_{ij}$ and yielding tightness $0.99$, AtkAdv $4.4\times$, $\tau{=}+0.36$. Architectures using binary adjacency masks (hard attention, max/min aggregation) and GATv2~\cite{brody2022how}'s dynamic-attention masking fall outside the framework; the architecture-agnostic property applies only to continuous edge-weight-modulated message passing.

\textbf{Cross-dataset transfer.} \Cref{fig:tau_heatmap} reports $\tau$ over 5 datasets and 7 architectures (330 runs); $29/33$ cells positive (one-sided sign test $p{<}10^{-5}$). Cold cells: GCN-2 on sparse citation graphs, IGNN on dense product graphs at 50-node subgraphs (BFS cap $\kappa_\mathrm{sub}{\leq}0.20$, average degree $\sim 31$); full-graph matrix-free analysis ($N{=}7{,}650$, $\rho{=}0.73$, resolvent $3.9\times$) shifts $\tau$ from $-0.14$ to $+0.24$. Heterophilic WebKB~\cite{pei2020geomgcn} GCN-4 attains $\tau{=}+0.21$--$+0.44$.

\Cref{prop:transfer}'s sufficient condition holds for $47$--$62\%$ of edge pairs (conservative, not tight); GCN-2 is the only architecture failing the bridge because 2-hop aggregates produce near-uniform first-order shifts. Gradient-based explainers~\cite{ying2019gnnexplainer} against the same discrete truth give $\tau{=}-0.07$ to $-0.21$, since they optimise prediction fidelity not adversarial vulnerability; the \cref{prop:explicit} bound is $1.4$--$5.9\times$ loose, tighter for shallower models. Per-edge $v_{ij}$ correlates with endpoint degree only moderately, and reweighting $S_c$ columns by degree does not improve rankings.
