\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
%% ===================================================================

Our evaluation tests four claims in turn: the contractivity assumptions hold on trained models (\cref{sec:cross_domain}); the one-query SVD direction matches iterative attackers (\cref{sec:adaptive}); the continuous $S_c$ ranking transfers to discrete edge removal across architectures (\cref{sec:explicit_extension}); and the same object audits a deployed fraud detector (\cref{sec:fraud_case}).

\textbf{Setup.} We evaluate \AEGIS on 6 datasets across 4 domains with 10 seeds each (PyTorch~\cite{paszke2019pytorch}); $\pm$ are standard deviations over seeds. IGNN~\cite{gu2020implicit} uses $F(Z){=}\mathrm{ReLU}(\Ahat ZW^\top{+}U(X))$, $W\in\R^{64\times64}$ spectral-normalised~\cite{miyato2018spectral}, Adam (lr $0.01$); the constraint costs ${\sim}6\%$ Cora accuracy. Datasets: Cora, Citeseer, Pubmed~\cite{sen2008collective}, Amazon Photo~\cite{shchur2018pitfalls}, WikiCS~\cite{mernyei2020wiki}, Amazon Fraud~\cite{dou2020enhancing}. Baselines: smoothing (Gaussian $\sigma{=}0.01$), Mettack, and GR-BCD/PR-BCD~\cite{geisler2021robustness}. All seven architectures appear only in \cref{fig:tau_heatmap} and \cref{tab:explicit}; the rest use IGNN. Full tables, ablations, and timing are in \cref{app:experiments}; reproducibility in \cref{app:repro}.

\subsection{Cross-Domain Adversarial Analysis}
\label{sec:cross_domain}

Two prerequisites must hold: the trained models sit in the contractive regime, and \AEGIS's perturbations beat chance. \Cref{tab:cross_domain} confirms both, verifying (A3) on every benchmark ($\kappa{<}1$) and reporting per-dataset $\ecrit$ alongside a $3.2$--$4.1\times$ attack advantage over random. Unless stated otherwise, IGNN experiments use 50-node BFS ego-subgraphs (highest-degree centre) reporting \emph{local vulnerability}, graph-level assessment using the matrix-free pipeline (\cref{app:phase_scal}); all bounds use $\kappa{=}\norm{J_z}_2$.

The first-order shift bounds agree with actual equilibrium shifts within $1\%$ at the small budgets that matter for early warning, loosening to at most $39\%$ on citation graphs (within $7\%$ on WikiCS and Amazon) even at $\varepsilon{=}0.20$. \AEGIS beats degree-proportional, edge-betweenness, and spectral rankings at $\varepsilon{=}0.01$ ($+6$--$148\%$ AtkAdv) and inflicts $3$--$10\times$ more equilibrium damage than Mettack~\cite{zugner2019adversarial} in the early-warning regime ($\mathbf{149/150}$ paired wins, $p{<}10^{-43}$); GR-BCD/PR-BCD and discrete-removal curves are in \cref{app:baselines}.

\subsection{Attack Efficiency and the One-Query Direction}
\label{sec:adaptive}\label{sec:certificates}

\AEGIS's attack is one-query and analytic, not a peak optimiser: the SVD direction maximises first-order equilibrium shift by construction (\cref{prop:attack}), leaving non-circularity and cost. A transfer direction from a \emph{separately trained} surrogate (zero shared gradients) recovers $99\%$ of the one-query damage ($\cos{=}0.99$), versus $44{\pm}4\%$ for a $512$-query black-box search, so it is model-intrinsic, not a gradient artifact. Per query it delivers $\mathbf{74}$--$\mathbf{156\times}$ the \emph{equilibrium-shift} damage (a per-query efficiency ratio; PGD's absolute classification damage is competitive, \cref{app:baselines}) of a $50$-step PGD~\cite{madry2018towards}; classification-loss PGD inflicts $15$--$70\%$ less at $50\times$ the cost. \Cref{tab:attack_full} (\cref{app:attack_full}) gives the full four-quadrant comparison and contrasts $r_v$ with randomized/localized smoothing radii; budget-heavy attackers (GR-BCD) exceed \AEGIS only at the larger budgets they target (\cref{app:baselines}). Per-edge finite differences reproduce the $S_c$ column-norm ranking ($\tau{=}0.999$), and iterative recomputation closes $\leq3$\,pp of the static-vs-greedy gap at $k{\times}$ compute, so the static ranking is the headline.

\textbf{Breach rates.} Every breached node satisfies $\varepsilon > r_v$ across all datasets and budgets, validating $r_v$ as a reliable first-order screen (\cref{sec:conformal}). \Cref{fig:breach} reports prediction-flip fractions vs.\ $\varepsilon$ ($95\%$ CI bands): Citeseer and WikiCS stay below $2\%$ throughout; Cora reaches $7.6\%$ at $\varepsilon{=}0.20$; Pubmed is the right-skewed outlier (median $7.8\%$, mean $10.3\%$ at $\varepsilon{=}0.10$; $27.4\%$ at $\varepsilon{=}0.20$).

\subsection{Continuous-to-Discrete Transfer Across Architectures}
\label{sec:explicit_extension}

The construction is not tied to implicit models: for $K$-layer explicit GNNs (GCN, SAGE, GIN, APPNP, GAT$^\dagger$~\cite{kipf2017semi,hamilton2017inductive,xu2019how,klicpera2019predict,velickovic2018graph}) we form $S_K{=}\partial \mathrm{vec}(Z_K)/\partial \mathrm{vec}(A)$ via finite differences and build $S_c$ identically (\cref{prop:explicit}); IGNN additionally carries \cref{thm:phase_transition}, the explicit models getting the tool without the regime characterisation (per-architecture detail in \cref{tab:explicit}, \cref{app:explicit}).

The headline test is whether the continuous $S_c$ ranking predicts discrete N-1 removal. Ranking edges by the edge-weighted $A_{ij}v_{ij}$ of \cref{prop:transfer}, \cref{fig:tau_heatmap} reports its Kendall $\tau$ over 6 datasets and 7 architectures (390 runs): \emph{all $\mathbf{39/39}$ cells positive}, median $\tau{=}\mathbf{+0.99}$ ($p{<}10^{-5}$), vs.\ $34/39$ unweighted. Weighting matters most at full-graph scale: on Amazon Photo ($N{=}7{,}650$, $\kappa{\approx}1.00$) it reaches $\tau\!=\!\mathbf{+0.996\!\pm\!0.002}$ (at $\kappa{\approx}1$ the contractive certificate is out of scope; the $S_c$ ranking still transfers empirically). The score adds rank information beyond the weight (beating a pure-weight baseline by $\Delta\tau{\approx}{+}0.25$, up to $+0.65$ on the fraud graph), and \cref{prop:transfer}'s pairwise condition holds for only $47$--$62\%$ of pairs, so the global $\tau$ is empirical. Per-architecture detail, GAT$^\dagger$ scope, robust backbones, and the cross-dataset breakdown are in \cref{app:explicit}.

The phase-transition sweep (a $2$--$4\times$ spectral-radius margin to criticality holding even as $\ecrit$ falls $230\times$) and scalability study (matrix-free reaching $N{=}7{,}650$ at $365$\,s/$5.5$\,GB, $\sigma_1$ within $0.03\%$ at $N{=}200$) are in \cref{app:phase_scal}; subgraph-size, hyperparameter, and edge-protection ablations (vulnerability is \emph{delocalized}, so risk is localised for triage, not removed by masking a few edges) are in \cref{app:ablations}.
