\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
%% ===================================================================

\textbf{Setup.} We evaluate \AEGIS on 9 datasets across 4 domains with 10 random seeds each (PyTorch~\cite{paszke2019pytorch}). IGNN~\cite{gu2020implicit} uses $F(Z){=}\mathrm{ReLU}(\Ahat ZW^\top{+}U(X))$ with $W\in\R^{64\times64}$ spectral-normalised~\cite{miyato2018spectral} and Adam (lr $0.01$); ContractiveGCN-PF mirrors this with 5 bus features and 2-head readout. The spectral-norm constraint costs ${\sim}6\%$ Cora accuracy ($77.5\%$ vs.\ ${\sim}83\%$). Datasets: Cora, Citeseer, Pubmed~\cite{sen2008collective}, Amazon Photo~\cite{shchur2018pitfalls}, WikiCS~\cite{mernyei2020wiki}, IEEE 14/30/57/118~\cite{ieee2024testcases,thurner2018pandapower}. Baselines: smoothing (Gaussian $\sigma{=}0.01$, 200 MC, Clopper--Pearson $\alpha{=}0.001$), Mettack with Meta-Self GCN surrogate, LODF as $\mathrm{PTDF}_{ij}/(1{-}\mathrm{PTDF}_{jj})$ from the DC $B$-matrix~\cite[Ch.~11]{wood2014power}. The 9 datasets and 7 architectures are cumulative: all seven architectures appear only in \cref{tab:explicit} and \cref{fig:tau_heatmap} (33 cells); other studies use IGNN, and power flow the four IEEE cases.

\subsection{Four-Quadrant Attack Comparison}
\label{sec:adaptive}\label{sec:certificates}

\textbf{Radius vs.\ smoothing.} Randomized smoothing~\cite{bojchevski2020efficient} gives constant-per-node base radii ($0.123$ vs.\ \AEGIS's $0.078$ at $\sigma{=}0.05$) and localized smoothing~\cite{schuchardt2023localized} needs $10^3$--$10^4$ MC/node, both lacking edge structure; \AEGIS radii are structurally informative ($r_v{\approx}0.10$ dense, $0.01$ at boundaries), a complementary point on the certificate-vs-diagnostic frontier.

\Cref{tab:attack_full} evaluates \AEGIS against baselines spanning the four quadrants of (gradient-based, gradient-free) $\times$ (equilibrium-shift, classification-loss). The one-query SVD direction dominates: a $50$-step equilibrium-shift PGD~\cite{madry2018towards} using \AEGIS's own IFT gradients recovers only $72$--$92\%$ of its damage (solver validation, not an independent baseline), and a classification-loss PGD inflicts $15$--$70\%$ less while flipping predictions at comparable rates ($0$--$1.8\%$). The decisive test is a \emph{gradient-independent} adversary: a transfer direction from a separately trained surrogate IGNN (zero shared gradients) recovers only $99\%$ of the one-query damage ($\cos{=}0.99$ alignment), and a $512$-query black-box search just $44{\pm}4\%$---the direction is model-intrinsic, not a circular gradient artifact. We accordingly term it the \emph{maximally sensitive perturbation direction}: a first-order guarantee that empirically matches iterative attackers in one query.

\subsection{Phase Transition and Scalability}
\label{sec:phase_transition}\label{sec:scalability}

\textbf{Phase transition.} \Cref{fig:phase_transition} sweeps $\kappa_{\max}{\in}[0.30,0.99]$ on Cora (10 seeds): even when the spectral-normalisation cap is pushed to $0.99$ (driving the theoretical $\ecrit$ down $230\times$), the trained ReLU pattern keeps $\rho(J_z)\!\leq\!0.42$, so the resolvent grows only $\mathbf{1.17{\to}1.80}$, \emph{validating} $\ecrit$ as a closed-form sufficient safety boundary that holds with $\mathbf{2{-}4\times}$ empirical margin across the sweep. Training converges throughout; the matrix-free pipeline reaches its 50-step fixed-point budget at $\kappa_{\max}{\geq}0.85$.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig_phase_transition.pdf}
\caption{Three-regime characterisation of \cref{thm:phase_transition}. The empirical $\kappa$ band (grey) saturates well below the spectral-normalisation ceiling, so the empirical resolvent growth is far milder than the worst-case $1/(1-\kappa)$ divergence at $\varepsilon \to \ecrit$.}
\label{fig:phase_transition}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Scalability and matrix-free self-consistency.} \Cref{fig:scalability} compares dense and matrix-free pipelines. The matrix-free $S_c v$ implements the dense $S_c$ up to Neumann truncation; $\sigma_1$ agrees within $0.03\%$ at $N{=}200$ (per-edge $\tau{=}0.999$), and the analytical truncation residual $\kappa^{200}\!\in\![10^{-105},10^{-48}]$ across the suite (consistent with the trained-IGNN range $\kappa\!\in\![0.14,0.59]$ of \cref{tab:cross_domain}). For larger $N$ the rSVD error~\cite{halko2011finding} is bounded by the spectral gap; Pubmed ($N{=}19{,}717$) exceeds $24$\,GB.


\subsection{Ablations and Defense}
\label{sec:subgraph_ablation}\label{sec:hyperparams}\label{sec:convergence}\label{sec:defense_ablation}

\textbf{Subgraph size.} Varying $N{\in}\{30,50,100,200\}$ on Cora (10 seeds): tightness is stable at $1.01$--$1.02$ for $N{\leq}100$ and degrades to $1.031$ at $N{=}200$; we use $N{=}50$ as default ($66\times$ faster). Subgraph-vs-full-graph agreement is high on dense grids (case14 $\tau{=}0.80$--$0.86$).

\textbf{Full-graph scale.} A 50-node BFS covers only ${\sim}1.8\%$ of a citation graph's edges (Cora $\tau{=}0.16$), so at this scale we run \AEGIS on the full graph, where its edge advantage over degree-proportional \emph{amplifies} to $\mathbf{9.82\times}$ (Citeseer) and $\mathbf{3.25\times}$ (Cora) at $k{=}10$, vs.\ ${\approx}1.1\times$ on subgraphs.

\textbf{Hyperparameters.} Tightness is stable across $d{\in}\{16,32,64,128\}$; the spectral-norm ceiling $c{\in}\{0.5,0.9\}$ traces the accuracy--robustness frontier ($r_v{=}0.147$@$72.1\%$ vs.\ $0.089$@$80.6\%$).

\textbf{Defense-informed edge protection: gains survive adaptive attack.} Masking the top-$k$ edges by $v_{ij}$ (Cora IGNN, $N{=}50$, 10 seeds) cuts $\sigma_1(S_c)$-damage by $\mathbf{42{\pm}8\%}$ at $k{=}5$ and $\mathbf{61{\pm}7\%}$ at $k{=}10$, vs.\ $11{\pm}6\%$/$18{\pm}9\%$ for random masking (paired Wilcoxon $p{<}0.002$). An \emph{adaptive} attacker that recomputes $S_c$ on the masked graph matches the non-adaptive attacker within $0.1$\,pp (10 seeds, $\varepsilon{=}0.10$): the $43\%$ spectral gap of $S_c$ (\cref{fig:sc_heatmap}) keeps the dominant direction robust, so the defense \emph{does not erode under recomputation}---the failure mode sober-look critiques~\cite{mujkanovic2022defenses} flag.

\textbf{GAT$^\dagger$ scope.} Standard GAT uses $A$ as a binary mask, so $\partial Z/\partial A_{ij}{=}0$ on existing edges and $S_c$ is undefined; our GAT$^\dagger$ modulates attention by the edge weight ($h_i' = \sum_{j} \Ahat_{ij} \alpha_{ij} W h_j$), restoring differentiability and yielding tightness $0.99$, AtkAdv $4.4\times$, $\tau{=}+0.36$. Binary-mask architectures (hard attention, max/min aggregation, GATv2~\cite{brody2022how}-style dynamic-attention masking) fall outside the framework.

\textbf{Robust backbones.} Under the $\sigma_1(W){\leq}0.9$ cap, RobustGCN-lite~\cite{zhu2019robust} ($\tau{=}+0.37/+0.54$, Cora/Citeseer) and GNNGuard-lite~\cite{zhang2020gnnguard} ($+0.10/+0.53$) match or exceed IGNN; the cap is a precondition (without it $\kappa{>}1$ voids \cref{thm:phase_transition}).

\textbf{Cross-dataset transfer.} \Cref{fig:tau_heatmap} reports $\tau$ over 5 datasets and 7 architectures (330 runs); $29/33$ cells positive (one-sided sign test $p{<}10^{-5}$). Cold cells (GCN-2 on sparse citation graphs, IGNN on dense product graphs at 50 nodes) recover decisively at full-graph scale: on Amazon Photo ($N{=}7{,}650$, stratified top-$v_{ij}$ ground-truth sampling) the edge-weighted ranking $A_{ij} v_{ij}$ predicted by \cref{prop:transfer} reaches $\tau\!=\!\mathbf{+0.996\!\pm\!0.002}$ (10 seeds) against brute-force fixed-normalization N-1 damage, a near-perfect rank match across seeds. On heterophilic WebKB~\cite{pei2020geomgcn} GCN-4 attains $\tau{=}+0.21$--$+0.44$.
