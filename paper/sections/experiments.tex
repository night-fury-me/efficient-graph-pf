\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
%% ===================================================================

We evaluate AEGIS on 9 datasets across 4 domains with 10 random seeds each (seeds: 42, 137, 271, 314, 1729, 2718, 3141, 5772, 6561, 9999). All graph benchmark experiments use IGNN~\cite{gu2020implicit} with hidden dimension 64, spectral-norm constrained weights, and early stopping on validation accuracy over 200 epochs. Power flow experiments use ContractiveGCN-PF (an IGNN variant for AC power flow).

\textbf{Notation.} All formal bounds (\cref{thm:phase_transition}) use the operator-norm contraction constant $\kappa = \norm{J_z}_2$. Tables report the spectral radius $\rho(J_z)$ as a diagnostic quantity. Since non-normality is mild ($\eta = 1.02$--$1.28$, \cref{sec:convergence}), we have $\kappa \approx \rho$ in practice; the $\ecrit$ values computed with $\rho$ are at most 28\% optimistic relative to the $\kappa$-based formal threshold.

\textbf{Datasets.} Graph benchmarks: Cora (2,708 nodes), Citeseer (3,327), Pubmed (19,717), Amazon Photo (7,650), WikiCS (11,701). Power flow: IEEE case14 (14 buses), case30 (30), case57 (57), case118 (118), generated via PandaPower with 2,000 samples each.

\subsection{Certificate Comparison: Deterministic vs.\ Smoothing}
\label{sec:certificates}

We compare AEGIS's deterministic first-order per-node radii (\cref{prop:radius}) against randomized smoothing~\cite{bojchevski2020efficient} with Gaussian noise on existing edges ($\sigma \in \{0.01, 0.05, 0.10, 0.15\}$, 1000 Monte Carlo samples, Clopper-Pearson confidence $1-\alpha = 0.999$). \cref{tab:certificates} reports median certified radius on Cora, Citeseer, and WikiCS (10 seeds, 50-node subgraphs).

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{AEGIS deterministic vs.\ smoothing certificates (10 seeds). Smoothing at $\sigma \leq 0.10$ produces constant per-node radii (all nodes identical); AEGIS radii vary by node, reflecting structural vulnerability.}
\label{tab:certificates}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrrr@{}}
\toprule
\multirow{2}{*}{Dataset} & \multirow{2}{*}{AEGIS} & \multicolumn{4}{c}{Smoothing median radius} \\
\cmidrule(lr){3-6}
 & & $\sigma{=}.01$ & $\sigma{=}.05$ & $\sigma{=}.10$ & $\sigma{=}.15$ \\
\midrule
Cora     & $\ms{.046}{.010}$ & .025 & .123 & $\ms{.244}{.005}$ & $\ms{.283}{.132}$ \\
Citeseer & $\ms{.045}{.012}$ & .025 & .123 & $\ms{.233}{.013}$ & $\ms{.091}{.069}$ \\
WikiCS   & $\ms{.040}{.004}$ & .025 & .123 & $\ms{.246}{.000}$ & $\ms{.341}{.017}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The comparison reveals a qualitative difference rather than a simple radius ordering. At $\sigma \leq 0.10$, smoothing radii are \emph{constant} across all nodes and datasets: every node receives the identical maximum-possible radius $r = \sigma \cdot \Phi^{-1}(p_{\mathrm{lower}})$ because essentially all 1000 Monte Carlo samples predict correctly. This yields larger absolute radii than AEGIS ($0.123$ vs.\ $0.046$ at $\sigma = 0.05$) but provides \emph{zero per-node differentiation}---the certificates do not distinguish between vulnerable and robust nodes. At $\sigma = 0.15$, smoothing begins to differentiate (some nodes lose consistency), but with high variance.

AEGIS radii, by contrast, are \emph{structurally informative}: median radius $0.041$--$0.046$ with per-node variation reflecting actual equilibrium sensitivity. Nodes in dense, well-connected regions receive larger radii ($r_v \approx 0.09$), while boundary nodes get smaller ones ($r_v \approx 0.01$). This per-node differentiation is the key advantage for vulnerability ranking, not just worst-case bounds.

The two approaches are complementary: smoothing provides uniform probabilistic guarantees scaled by $\sigma$, while AEGIS provides tight, deterministic, structurally differentiated certificates that identify which specific nodes and edges are vulnerable.

\subsection{Phase Transition Validation}
\label{sec:phase_transition}

To validate \cref{thm:phase_transition}, we scale the adjacency matrix to achieve target spectral radii $\rho \in \{0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.85, 0.9, 0.95, 0.99\}$ and measure the actual fixed-point shift under a random perturbation of $\varepsilon = 0.01$. The shift amplifies by $83\times$ as $\rho$ increases from 0.3 to 0.99, confirming the three-regime behavior: bounded shift in the subcritical regime, rapid growth near $\rho = 1$, and divergence in the supercritical regime.

\subsection{Hyperparameter Sensitivity}
\label{sec:hyperparams}

We evaluate sensitivity to the key hyperparameters: hidden dimension $d$ and spectral-norm constraint $c$.

\textbf{Hidden dimension.} Varying $d \in \{16, 32, 64, 128\}$ on Cora: tightness is $1.012$--$1.013$ for all values, confirming robustness to architecture width. Accuracy improves from $74.7\%$ ($d=16$) to $79.1\%$ ($d=64$). The critical budget $\ecrit$ and median radius are stable across $d$: $\ecrit \in [0.53, 0.56]$, $r_v \in [0.037, 0.051]$.

\textbf{Spectral norm constraint.} Varying $c \in \{0.5, 0.7, 0.9, 0.95\}$ reveals the accuracy--robustness tradeoff predicted by \cref{thm:phase_transition}. At $c = 0.5$: $\rho = 0.25$, $\ecrit = 1.50$, median $r_v = 0.090$---the largest certificates, but accuracy drops to $72.1\%$. At $c = 0.9$: $\rho = 0.45$, $\ecrit = 0.61$, $r_v = 0.051$, accuracy peaks at $80.6\%$. Radii shrink by $1.8\times$ from $c = 0.5$ to $c = 0.9$ as $1/(1 - \kappa)$ grows, consistent with the theoretical amplification factor.

\subsection{DEQ Convergence Diagnostics}
\label{sec:convergence}

We report convergence statistics for the IGNN fixed-point iteration across all 9 datasets (10 seeds, relative tolerance $\norm{Z_{k+1} - Z_k}_F < 10^{-5} \cdot \max(\norm{Z}_F, 1)$). Convergence is 100\% across all datasets and seeds. Graph benchmarks require $28 \pm 7$ (Amazon) to $113 \pm 39$ (Cora) iterations, while IEEE grids converge faster: $11 \pm 2$ (case14) to $44 \pm 13$ (case57). The non-normality index is $\eta = 1.02$--$1.05$ on graph benchmarks and $\eta = 1.20$--$1.28$ on IEEE grids, confirming mild non-normality across all domains. No seed on any dataset failed to converge, consistent with the enforced contractivity constraint $\kappa = \norm{J_z}_2 < 1$ (via spectral normalization of $W$).
