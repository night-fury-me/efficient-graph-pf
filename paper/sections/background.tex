\subsection{Deep Equilibrium Graph Neural Networks}

A DEQ-GNN~\cite{bai2019deep} defines a weight-tied operator $F_\theta: \R^{N \times d} \times \R^{N \times N} \to \R^{N \times d}$ and iterates it to convergence:
\begin{equation}
Z_{k+1} = F_\theta(Z_k, A), \quad Z_0 = 0.
\end{equation}
When $F_\theta$ is contractive ($\kappa = \norm{J_z}_2 < 1$, where $J_z = \partial F/\partial z$), the Banach fixed-point theorem guarantees a unique equilibrium $\zstar = F_\theta(\zstar, A)$. The Implicit Graph Neural Network (IGNN)~\cite{gu2020implicit} instantiates this with $F(Z, A) = \sigma(\Ahat Z W^\top + X_{\mathrm{proj}})$, where $\Ahat = D^{-1/2}(A + I)D^{-1/2}$ is the normalized adjacency and $W$ is spectral-norm constrained to ensure $\kappa \leq \norm{\Ahat}_2 \cdot \norm{W}_2 < 1$.

\subsection{Implicit Function Theorem for DEQ Sensitivity}

At the fixed point, the IFT provides the sensitivity of $\zstar$ to any parameter $p$:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial \zstar}{\partial p} = (I - J_z)^{-1} \frac{\partial F}{\partial p}\bigg|_{\zstar},
\label{eq:ift}
\end{equation}
where $J_z = \partial F/\partial z|_{\zstar}$ is the state Jacobian. When $\kappa = \norm{J_z}_2 < 1$, the resolvent $(I - J_z)^{-1}$ exists and is bounded by $\norm{(I-J_z)^{-1}}_2 \leq 1/(1-\kappa)$ via the Neumann series. This bound uses the operator norm $\kappa$ rather than the spectral radius $\rho(J_z)$; the latter yields the weaker bound $1/(1-\rho)$ only for normal $J_z$. The pseudospectral index $\eta = \norm{(I-J_z)^{-1}}_2 \cdot (1-\rho) \geq 1$~\cite{trefethen2005spectra} measures the gap, with $\eta = 1$ for normal matrices. In practice, IGNN's $J_z = \mathrm{diag}(\sigma') \cdot (\Ahat \otimes W)$ exhibits mild non-normality ($\eta = 1.02$--$1.28$ across our experiments). The IFT has been used for hyperparameter optimization in implicit layers~\cite{lorraine2020optimizing} and general implicit differentiation~\cite{gould2021deep}, but not for adversarial robustness analysis of graph structure.

\subsection{Adversarial Structural Perturbation}

We consider an attacker who perturbs the adjacency matrix: $A' = A + \delta A$ with $\norm{\delta A}_F \leq \varepsilon$. For undirected graphs, $\delta A$ must be symmetric. In graph terms, this corresponds to modifying edge weights or adding/removing edges. Unlike input-feature perturbation (studied for DEQs in~\cite{el2021implicit,revay2020lipschitz}), structural perturbation changes the \emph{operator} itself, not just its input---the fixed point depends on $A$ through both $J_z$ and $J_A$.
