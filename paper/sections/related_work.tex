\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
%% ===================================================================

AEGIS sits at the intersection of adversarial robustness of GNNs, robustness of implicit networks, and sensitivity analysis. We review each thread and clarify how AEGIS differs.

\textbf{Adversarial attacks on graph structure.}
Z\"ugner et al.~\cite{zugner2018adversarial} demonstrated GNN vulnerability to structural perturbation with Nettack; Mettack~\cite{zugner2019adversarial} extended this to global poisoning via meta-gradients through a GCN~\cite{kipf2017semi} surrogate. Dai et al.~\cite{dai2018adversarial} framed attacks as reinforcement learning, Bojchevski and G\"unnemann~\cite{bojchevski2019adversarial} proposed global poisoning via spectral shifts, and Xu et al.~\cite{xu2019topology} formulated topology attack as bilevel optimization. Black-box variants include GF-Attack~\cite{chang2020restricted} via graph signal processing and influence-function approaches~\cite{ma2020towards}. Wu et al.~\cite{wu2019adversarial} and Jin et al.~\cite{jin2021adversarial} survey the landscape. All target explicit GNNs with surrogate gradients or heuristic search. AEGIS instead analyzes the implicit equilibrium directly via the IFT, providing a closed-form optimal attack through SVD of $S_c$.

\textbf{Certified robustness for GNNs.}
Bojchevski and G\"unnemann~\cite{bojchevski2019certifiable} pioneered certifiable robustness via convex relaxations for GCN-class models. Z\"ugner and G\"unnemann~\cite{zugner2019certifiable} extended this with interval bound propagation. Both require finite-depth architectures incompatible with implicit models. Randomized smoothing~\cite{bojchevski2020efficient} provides probabilistic certificates for any classifier; Scholten et al.\ refined this with message-interception~\cite{scholten2022randomized} and hierarchical~\cite{scholten2023hierarchical} smoothing. Jia et al.~\cite{jia2020certified} certified community detection via smoothing, Wang et al.~\cite{wang2021certified} proposed message-passing invariance certificates, and Geisler et al.~\cite{geisler2021robustness} scaled certified defenses to large graphs. Li et al.~\cite{li2025agnncert} proposed AGNNCert with deterministic guarantees under arbitrary perturbations. All target explicit architectures. AEGIS fills the gap for implicit models with deterministic first-order radii that are structurally differentiated per node (\cref{sec:certificates}).

\textbf{GNN defense.}
Empirical defenses improve robustness without formal guarantees: Robust GCN~\cite{zhu2019robust} absorbs noise via Gaussian hidden layers, Pro-GNN~\cite{jin2020graph} jointly learns clean structure, GNNGuard~\cite{zhang2020gnnguard} reweights edges during aggregation, PA-GNN~\cite{tang2020transferring} transfers robustness across domains, PTDNet~\cite{luo2021learning} learns denoised topologies, and soft-median aggregation~\cite{geisler2020reliable} replaces mean pooling. Entezari et al.~\cite{entezari2020all} observed that adversarial perturbations primarily affect high-rank spectral components, motivating low-rank SVD defenses. AEGIS is an analytical tool rather than a defense, but its vulnerability spectrum could inform defense design.

\textbf{DEQ and implicit network robustness.}
The DEQ framework~\cite{bai2019deep} introduced fixed-point models; extensions include multiscale DEQs~\cite{bai2020multiscale}, Jacobian regularization for stability~\cite{bai2021stabilizing}, phantom gradients~\cite{geng2021training}, and Jacobian-free backpropagation~\cite{fung2022jfb}. Robustness analysis focuses on \emph{input} perturbation: monotone operator networks~\cite{winston2020monotone} provide built-in Lipschitz bounds, Revay et al.~\cite{revay2020lipschitz} use linear matrix inequalities, Pabbaraju et al.~\cite{pabbaraju2021estimating} estimate tighter Lipschitz constants, and El Ghaoui et al.~\cite{el2021implicit} analyze well-posedness. All address $\norm{\partial \zstar / \partial x}$, not \emph{structural} sensitivity $\norm{\partial \zstar / \partial A}$, which changes the operator itself. AEGIS addresses this distinct perturbation type through $S_c$.

\textbf{Implicit GNNs.}
IGNN~\cite{gu2020implicit} enforces unique equilibria via spectral normalization~\cite{miyato2018spectral}. EIGNN~\cite{liu2021eignn} improves efficiency through eigendecomposition. Liu et al.~\cite{liu2021stability} study GNN stability without per-node radii or vulnerability characterization. None analyze adversarial structural vulnerability at the equilibrium as AEGIS does.

\textbf{Sensitivity analysis.}
AEGIS draws on classical matrix perturbation theory~\cite{stewart1990matrix,trefethen2005spectra}. In deep learning, Novak et al.~\cite{novak2018sensitivity} connected Jacobian sensitivity to generalization and Raghu et al.~\cite{raghu2017expressive} analyzed expressivity via trajectory lengths. These study feedforward architectures; AEGIS applies sensitivity analysis to implicit fixed points, where the resolvent $(I-J_z)^{-1}$ amplifies perturbation effects by $1/(1-\kappa)$.

\textbf{GNNs for power systems.}
Our case study (\cref{sec:power_flow}) connects to GNN-based power flow solvers~\cite{donon2019graph,bolz2019power} and GNN contingency screening~\cite{nakiganda2023graph}. AEGIS contributes by showing that equilibrium sensitivity independently recovers contingency rankings without domain-specific linearization.

\textbf{Positioning.}
AEGIS applies to contractive implicit GNNs with operator $F(Z,A) = \sigma(\Ahat Z W^\top + b)$. Explicit architectures (GAT, GIN, GraphSAGE) lack this structure and are outside scope. Within its scope, AEGIS provides tight, structurally differentiated, first-order vulnerability analysis that no existing method offers.
