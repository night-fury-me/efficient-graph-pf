\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
%% ===================================================================

AEGIS addresses adversarial structural vulnerability analysis for GNNs through first-order sensitivity. We position it against five research threads: structural attacks, certified robustness, empirical defenses, implicit network theory, and sensitivity analysis.

\textbf{Adversarial attacks on graph structure.}
Z\"ugner et al.~\cite{zugner2018adversarial} demonstrated GNN vulnerability to structural perturbation with Nettack; Mettack~\cite{zugner2019adversarial} extended this to global poisoning via meta-gradients through a GCN~\cite{kipf2017semi} surrogate. Dai et al.~\cite{dai2018adversarial} framed attacks as reinforcement learning, Bojchevski and G\"unnemann~\cite{bojchevski2019adversarial} proposed global poisoning via spectral shifts, and Xu et al.~\cite{xu2019topology} formulated topology attack as bilevel optimization. Black-box variants include GF-Attack~\cite{chang2020restricted} via graph signal processing and influence-function approaches~\cite{ma2020towards}. Wu et al.~\cite{wu2019adversarial} and Jin et al.~\cite{jin2021adversarial} survey the landscape. These methods use surrogate gradients or heuristic search to find adversarial edges. AEGIS takes a fundamentally different approach: it computes the closed-form optimal attack direction via SVD of the constrained sensitivity matrix $S_c$, which is architecture-agnostic and applies to any differentiable GNN.

\textbf{Certified robustness for GNNs.}
Bojchevski and G\"unnemann~\cite{bojchevski2019certifiable} pioneered certifiable robustness via convex relaxations for GCN-class models. Z\"ugner and G\"unnemann~\cite{zugner2019certifiable} extended this with interval bound propagation. Randomized smoothing~\cite{bojchevski2020efficient} provides probabilistic certificates for any classifier; Scholten et al.\ refined this with message-interception~\cite{scholten2022randomized} and hierarchical~\cite{scholten2023hierarchical} smoothing. Jia et al.~\cite{jia2020certified} certified community detection via smoothing, Wang et al.~\cite{wang2021certified} proposed message-passing invariance certificates, and Geisler et al.~\cite{geisler2021robustness} scaled certified defenses to large graphs. Li et al.~\cite{li2025agnncert} proposed AGNNCert with deterministic guarantees. These methods provide robustness guarantees but do not identify \emph{which} edges or nodes are vulnerable. AEGIS complements them by providing structurally differentiated per-node radii and per-edge vulnerability rankings that reveal the attack surface geometry (\cref{sec:certificates}).

\textbf{GNN defense.}
Empirical defenses improve robustness without formal guarantees: Robust GCN~\cite{zhu2019robust} absorbs noise via Gaussian hidden layers, Pro-GNN~\cite{jin2020graph} jointly learns clean structure, GNNGuard~\cite{zhang2020gnnguard} reweights edges during aggregation, PA-GNN~\cite{tang2020transferring} transfers robustness across domains, PTDNet~\cite{luo2021learning} learns denoised topologies, and soft-median aggregation~\cite{geisler2020reliable} replaces mean pooling. Entezari et al.~\cite{entezari2020all} observed that adversarial perturbations primarily affect high-rank spectral components. AEGIS is an analytical tool rather than a defense, but its per-edge vulnerability spectrum could inform defense design by identifying which edges to protect, and the SVD structure echoes the spectral observations of Entezari et al.\ from the sensitivity perspective.

\textbf{Implicit networks and equilibrium analysis.}
The DEQ framework~\cite{bai2019deep} introduced fixed-point models; extensions include multiscale DEQs~\cite{bai2020multiscale}, Jacobian regularization~\cite{bai2021stabilizing}, phantom gradients~\cite{geng2021training}, and Jacobian-free backpropagation~\cite{fung2022jfb}. IGNN~\cite{gu2020implicit} enforces unique equilibria via spectral normalization~\cite{miyato2018spectral}; EIGNN~\cite{liu2021eignn} improves efficiency. For robustness, monotone operator networks~\cite{winston2020monotone} provide built-in Lipschitz bounds, Revay et al.~\cite{revay2020lipschitz} use LMIs, Pabbaraju et al.~\cite{pabbaraju2021estimating} estimate tighter constants, and El Ghaoui et al.~\cite{el2021implicit} analyze well-posedness. All address input sensitivity $\norm{\partial \zstar / \partial x}$, not structural sensitivity $\norm{\partial Z / \partial A}$. AEGIS addresses structural sensitivity for both implicit models (via the resolvent $(I-J_z)^{-1} J_A$) and explicit models (via the unrolled Jacobian $S_K$), providing stronger theoretical guarantees for the implicit case (\cref{thm:phase_transition}).

\textbf{Sensitivity analysis.}
AEGIS draws on classical matrix perturbation theory~\cite{stewart1990matrix,trefethen2005spectra}. In deep learning, Novak et al.~\cite{novak2018sensitivity} connected Jacobian sensitivity to generalization and Raghu et al.~\cite{raghu2017expressive} analyzed expressivity via trajectory lengths. Liu et al.~\cite{liu2021stability} studied GNN stability without per-node radii. These works study input sensitivity or generic stability; AEGIS applies constrained structural sensitivity analysis specifically to graph topology perturbation, with the $S_c$ projection from $N^2$ to $|E|$ dimensions enabling tight first-order predictions.

\textbf{GNNs for power systems.}
Our case study (\cref{sec:power_flow}) connects to GNN-based power flow solvers~\cite{donon2019graph,bolz2019power} and GNN contingency screening~\cite{nakiganda2023graph}. AEGIS shows that structural sensitivity independently recovers contingency rankings without domain-specific linearization.

\textbf{Positioning.}
AEGIS provides structural vulnerability analysis for any differentiable GNN via the constrained sensitivity matrix $S_c$ (\cref{prop:explicit}). For contractive implicit GNNs, $S_c$ additionally yields a critical budget and convergence guarantees (\cref{thm:phase_transition}). Validated across 7 architectures (\cref{sec:explicit_extension}), AEGIS offers a combination of generality, structural differentiation, and theoretical grounding that no existing method provides.
