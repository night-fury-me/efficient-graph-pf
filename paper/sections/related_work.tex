\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
%% ===================================================================

AEGIS sits at the intersection of three research threads: adversarial robustness of graph neural networks, robustness analysis of implicit (equilibrium) networks, and implicit GNN architectures. We review each in turn and clarify how AEGIS differs.

\textbf{Adversarial attacks on graph structure.}
The vulnerability of GNNs to structural perturbation was first demonstrated by Z\"ugner et al.~\cite{zugner2018adversarial}, who proposed Nettack for targeted node attacks via greedy edge flips. Mettack~\cite{zugner2019adversarial} extended this to global poisoning attacks using meta-gradients through a GCN~\cite{kipf2017semi} surrogate. Xu et al.~\cite{xu2019topology} formulated topology attack as a bilevel optimization problem, while Wu et al.~\cite{wu2019adversarial} provided deeper analysis of when and why structural attacks succeed. Jin et al.~\cite{jin2021adversarial} survey the landscape comprehensively. All of these methods target explicit (finite-depth) GNNs and rely on surrogate gradients when applied to architectures that differ from the surrogate model. In contrast, AEGIS directly analyzes the equilibrium via the IFT, avoiding surrogate transfer entirely and providing a closed-form optimal attack direction through SVD of the constrained sensitivity matrix.

\textbf{Certified robustness for GNNs.}
Provable robustness guarantees for GNNs have been pursued along several directions. Bojchevski and G\"unnemann~\cite{bojchevski2019certifiable} pioneered certifiable robustness under structural perturbation using convex relaxations for GCN-class models with fixed depth and ReLU activations. Z\"ugner and G\"unnemann~\cite{zugner2019certifiable} extended this with interval bound propagation, propagating perturbation intervals layer by layer. Both approaches require explicit finite-depth architectures and cannot handle the infinite-depth fixed-point computation of implicit models. Randomized smoothing~\cite{bojchevski2020efficient} provides probabilistic certificates by classifying under random edge noise; it applies to any base classifier but trades accuracy for robustness as noise $\sigma$ increases, and yields uniform per-node radii that do not distinguish between structurally vulnerable and robust nodes (\cref{sec:certificates}). Geisler et al.~\cite{geisler2021robustness} scale certified defenses to large graphs but remain within the explicit-GNN paradigm. Li et al.~\cite{li2025agnncert} propose AGNNCert with deterministic guarantees under arbitrary perturbations, again targeting explicit architectures. AEGIS fills the gap for implicit (IGNN-class) models: it provides deterministic first-order radii that are structurally differentiated per node, reflecting the actual sensitivity of each node's equilibrium position.

\textbf{Robustness of implicit and equilibrium networks.}
The DEQ framework~\cite{bai2019deep} introduced weight-tied infinite-depth models that converge to a fixed point. Subsequent work analyzed their robustness properties, primarily under \emph{input} (feature) perturbation. Winston and Kolter~\cite{winston2020monotone} developed monotone operator equilibrium networks with built-in Lipschitz bounds, yielding input robustness certificates by construction. Revay et al.~\cite{revay2020lipschitz} bounded Lipschitz constants via linear matrix inequalities. Pabbaraju et al.~\cite{pabbaraju2021estimating} estimated tighter Lipschitz constants for monotone DEQs. El Ghaoui et al.~\cite{el2021implicit} analyzed implicit deep learning with emphasis on well-posedness and input sensitivity. The key distinction from AEGIS is that all of these works address input sensitivity $\norm{\partial \zstar / \partial x}$, not \emph{structural} sensitivity $\norm{\partial \zstar / \partial A}$. Structural perturbation changes the equilibrium operator itself through the graph topology, altering both the state Jacobian $J_z$ and the adjacency Jacobian $J_A$ simultaneously. AEGIS addresses this fundamentally different perturbation type through the constrained sensitivity matrix $S_c$.

\textbf{Implicit graph neural networks.}
IGNN~\cite{gu2020implicit} introduced contractive implicit GNNs that enforce unique equilibria via spectral normalization~\cite{miyato2018spectral} of the weight matrix $W$. EIGNN~\cite{liu2021eignn} improved computational efficiency through eigendecomposition of the equilibrium equation. Liu et al.~\cite{liu2021stability} studied stability of GNNs under perturbation but without per-node robustness radii or explicit vulnerability characterization. None of these works analyze adversarial structural vulnerability at the equilibrium in the manner AEGIS does: computing the full sensitivity matrix, projecting it to realistic perturbation constraints, and extracting per-edge vulnerability rankings and per-node robustness radii.

\textbf{Positioning and scope.}
AEGIS is specific to contractive implicit GNNs with the factorized operator $F(Z,A) = \sigma(\Ahat Z W^\top + b)$. Most deployed GNNs (GAT, GIN, GraphSAGE) lack this structure and do not have guaranteed fixed points, so AEGIS cannot be directly applied to them. Within its scope, AEGIS provides a capability that no existing method offers: tight, structurally differentiated, first-order vulnerability analysis for implicit GNN equilibria under realistic graph perturbation constraints.
