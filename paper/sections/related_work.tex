\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
%% ===================================================================

Each of three threads supplies at most one of \AEGIS's three diagnostics. \textbf{Structural attacks} --- Nettack~\cite{zugner2018adversarial}, Mettack~\cite{zugner2019adversarial}, GR-BCD/PR-BCD~\cite{geisler2021robustness} and variants~\cite{wu2019adversarial,xu2019topology} --- optimise surrogate gradients to flip predictions but give no analytic maximally-sensitive direction and no per-node radius; sober-look studies~\cite{mujkanovic2022defenses,gosch2023adversarial} flag evaluation pitfalls, whose adaptive-attack discipline we follow in \cref{app:ablations}.

\textbf{Certified robustness and defense.} Convex relaxations~\cite{zugner2019certifiable}, smoothing~\cite{bojchevski2020efficient,bojchevski2019certifiable,schuchardt2023localized}, collective~\cite{schuchardt2021collective} and majority-vote~\cite{li2025agnncert} certificates, and weight-norm bounds~\cite{abbahaddou2024bounding} certify per-node robustness but do not identify vulnerability-driving edges; empirical defenses~\cite{zhu2019robust,jin2020graph,zhang2020gnnguard} harden models without surfacing per-edge sensitivity. Graph conformal prediction~\cite{zargarbashi2023conformal} certifies a \emph{fixed} graph; \AEGIS-Conformal instead covers the adversarial $\varepsilon$-ball via the $S_c$ score-shift bound (\cref{sec:conformal}), complementary to discrete-edit certifiers (\cref{app:baselines}).

\textbf{Implicit networks and influence functions.} DEQs~\cite{bai2019deep}, IGNN~\cite{gu2020implicit}, monotone equilibria~\cite{winston2020monotone}, and well-posedness analyses~\cite{el2021implicit} address \emph{input} sensitivity $\partial \zstar/\partial x$; \AEGIS targets \emph{structural} sensitivity $\partial Z/\partial A$. It descends from the Koh--Liang influence-function lineage~\cite{koh2017understanding} and implicit-differentiation hyperparameter optimisation~\cite{lorraine2020optimizing,gould2021deep}, but those apply IFT to loss perturbations whereas \AEGIS applies it to a \emph{structural-parameter} perturbation via $P_c$. GNN explainers~\cite{ying2019gnnexplainer} give per-edge importance for \emph{fidelity}, not vulnerability; matrix perturbation theory~\cite{stewart1990matrix,trefethen2005spectra} supplies the pseudospectral toolkit; and curvature/over-squashing~\cite{topping2022understanding,digiovanni2023oversquashing} and stability bounds~\cite{gama2020stability,kenlay2021stability} tie edge structure to sensitivity only in aggregate, whereas \AEGIS localises it per edge and node.
