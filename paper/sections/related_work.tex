\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
%% ===================================================================

We position \AEGIS against four threads: structural attacks, certified robustness, implicit-network analysis, and influence-function sensitivity. Across all four, prior methods supply at most one of the three outputs we unify under a single matrix-free pass.

\textbf{Adversarial attacks on graph structure.} Nettack~\cite{zugner2018adversarial}, Mettack~\cite{zugner2019adversarial}, the BCD-family GR-BCD / PR-BCD~\cite{geisler2021robustness}, and related variants~\cite{wu2019adversarial} optimise surrogate gradients to flip predictions, producing per-edge information but no closed-form globally maximally sensitive direction. Sober-look studies~\cite{mujkanovic2022defenses,gosch2023adversarial} show prior defence benchmarks are vulnerable to evaluation pitfalls; our defence-informed-masking ablation (\cref{sec:defense_ablation}) uses paired-Wilcoxon comparisons against random masking for this reason.

\textbf{Certified robustness and defense.} Convex relaxations~\cite{zugner2019certifiable}, smoothing~\cite{bojchevski2020efficient,bojchevski2019certifiable,schuchardt2023localized}, collective certificates~\cite{schuchardt2021collective}, and IBP~\cite{li2025agnncert} guarantee per-node robustness but do not identify which edges drive vulnerability. Empirical defenses~\cite{zhu2019robust,jin2020graph,zhang2020gnnguard} harden models without producing a vulnerability map. \AEGIS complements certification with structural differentiation (\cref{sec:certificates}); $r_v$ is a first-order threshold, not a sound certificate (\cref{rem:certificates}).

\textbf{Implicit networks and equilibrium analysis.} DEQs~\cite{bai2019deep}, IGNN~\cite{gu2020implicit}, and well-posedness analyses~\cite{el2021implicit} address input sensitivity $\norm{\partial \zstar / \partial x}$ rather than structural sensitivity $\norm{\partial Z / \partial A}$. \AEGIS targets the latter via the resolvent $(I-J_z)^{-1} J_A$ for implicit models (\cref{thm:phase_transition}) and the unrolled Jacobian $S_K$ for explicit ones (\cref{prop:explicit}).

\textbf{Sensitivity and influence-function lineage.} The closed-form structural derivative descends from influence-function-style implicit differentiation~\cite{lorraine2020optimizing}, extended here to adjacency under a constrained projection. GNN explainers~\cite{ying2019gnnexplainer} target prediction fidelity rather than adversarial vulnerability; \AEGIS draws on classical matrix perturbation theory~\cite{stewart1990matrix,trefethen2005spectra}.
