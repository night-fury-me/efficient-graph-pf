\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
%% ===================================================================

\textbf{Adversarial attacks on GNNs.} Structural attacks modify graph topology to degrade GNN performance. Nettack~\cite{zugner2018adversarial} targets individual nodes via greedy edge flips; Mettack~\cite{zugner2019adversarial} uses meta-gradients through a GCN surrogate for global poisoning. These methods operate on explicit (finite-depth) GNNs and require surrogate transfer when applied to DEQ models, which we show causes significant performance degradation due to architectural mismatch (\cref{sec:mettack,sec:adaptive}). AEGIS directly analyzes the equilibrium via IFT, avoiding surrogate transfer entirely.

\textbf{Certified robustness for GNNs.} Bojchevski and G\"unnemann~\cite{bojchevski2019certifiable} provide the first certifiable robustness analysis for GNNs under structural perturbation, using convex relaxations for GCN-class models. Randomized smoothing~\cite{bojchevski2020efficient} extends to probabilistic certificates by classifying under random graph perturbation; it provides valid certificates at arbitrary perturbation magnitudes but degrades base accuracy with increasing noise $\sigma$. Interval bound propagation~\cite{zugner2019certifiable} requires layer-by-layer propagation, incompatible with infinite-depth DEQ models. AEGIS provides deterministic first-order certificates that are exact under constrained perturbations---tighter than smoothing at small $\varepsilon$ ($1.4$--$2.1\times$ at $\sigma=0.25$) but not at large budgets where higher-order terms dominate (\cref{sec:certificates}).

\textbf{DEQ robustness and Lipschitz analysis.} Winston and Kolter~\cite{winston2020monotone} develop monotone operator equilibrium networks with built-in Lipschitz bounds, providing robustness certificates for input perturbations by construction. Revay et al.~\cite{revay2020lipschitz} bound the Lipschitz constant of equilibrium networks via linear matrix inequalities. Pabbaraju et al.~\cite{pabbaraju2021estimating} estimate tighter Lipschitz constants for monotone DEQs, directly bounding $\norm{\partial \zstar / \partial x}$. El Ghaoui et al.~\cite{el2021implicit} analyze implicit deep learning with a focus on well-posedness and input sensitivity. All of these works address \emph{input} (feature) perturbation sensitivity $\norm{\partial \zstar / \partial x}$, not \emph{structural} sensitivity $\norm{\partial \zstar / \partial A}$. The key distinction is that structural perturbation changes the operator itself (via $A$), not just its input---the fixed point depends on $A$ through both $J_z$ and $J_A$. AEGIS addresses this gap with the constrained structural sensitivity matrix $S_c$.

\textbf{Implicit GNNs.} IGNN~\cite{gu2020implicit} enforces contractivity via spectral normalization of $W$, guaranteeing unique equilibria. EIGNN~\cite{yang2022efficient} improves computational efficiency. Neither analyzes adversarial structural vulnerability at the equilibrium. Liu et al.~\cite{liu2021stability} study stability of GNNs under graph perturbation but without certified per-node radii or the explicit three-regime characterization.

\textbf{Scope and limitations.} AEGIS's theoretical analysis applies specifically to contractive implicit GNNs (IGNN-class models) that factorize as $F(Z,A) = \sigma(AZW^\top + b)$. Most deployed GNNs (GAT, GIN, GraphSAGE) do not have this well-separated $A$/$W$ structure and lack guaranteed fixed points, so AEGIS cannot be directly applied. Extension to other implicit architectures (monotone operator networks~\cite{winston2020monotone}, neural ODEs on graphs) is a promising direction but requires architecture-specific IFT derivations.
