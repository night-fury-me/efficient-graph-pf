\section{The \textsc{Aegis}-Conformal Coverage Bound}
\label{app:conformal}

This section derives the certificate behind \cref{eq:conformal}: a worst-case conformity-score-shift
bound $L_1^{c}\varepsilon+C_v\varepsilon^2$ and the robust-coverage guarantee it implies. The argument
has three parts, each building on the sensitivity already established. \Cref{def:conf-scores} fixes the
two conformity scores and the two constants. \Cref{lem:score-shift} bounds the worst-case score shift
using the first-order margin sensitivity (the Cauchy--Schwarz step of \cref{prop:radius}) and the
second-order curvature (the complete remainder of \cref{app:proof_transfer}). \Cref{thm:robust-cov}
inflates the split-conformal threshold by that shift and reduces to the binary certificate of
\citet{zargarbashi2023conformal}. We use (A1)--(A3) and operate in the certified regime
$\varepsilon<\ecrit$.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Conformal setup.}\quad
Fix a target node $v$ with true label $y_v\in[C]$, and let $f=W\zstar_v+b\in\R^{C}$ be its pre-softmax
logits (rows $W_1,\dots,W_C$) with class probabilities $\pi=\softmax(f)$. For a feasible perturbation
$\delta\Ahat$ of size at most $\varepsilon$, write the perturbed logits $f'=W\zstar{}'_v+b$ and
probabilities $\pi'=\softmax(f')$, and let $S_{c,v}$ be the block rows of $S_c$ at node $v$. By the
first-order sensitivity \eqref{eq:ift-expand},
\begin{equation}
\Delta\zstar_v=S_{c,v}\,\boldsymbol\delta+R_v,\qquad
\norm{\boldsymbol\delta}_2=\norm{\delta\Ahat}_F\le\varepsilon,
\label{eq:ift-firstorder}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol\delta$ are the edge-basis coordinates of $\delta\Ahat$ and $R_v$ is the remainder
bounded in \cref{app:proof_transfer}. The edge basis is orthonormal, so the coordinate map is an
isometry and the Frobenius budget passes through unchanged, which is the normalization that makes
$\sigma_1(S_c)$ consistent with $\norm{\delta\Ahat}_F$.

\begin{definition}[Conformity scores and the two AEGIS constants]
\label{def:conf-scores}
For node $v$ and candidate label $r\in[C]$, define the two conformity scores (higher means more
conforming; the prediction set is $\Cset(v)=\{r:\score_r(v)\ge\hat q\}$):
\begin{align}
\text{TPS:}\quad&\score^{\mathrm{TPS}}_r(v)=\pi_r,\\
\text{APS:}\quad&\score^{\mathrm{APS}}_r(v)=1-\big(\rho_r+u_v\,\pi_r\big),\\
&\rho_r:=\sum_{c:\,\pi_c>\pi_r}\pi_c,
\label{eq:aps}
\end{align}
with $u_v\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1]$ a per-node tie-break drawn once and shared between calibration and test,
so it cancels under exchangeability. For each competitor $c\ne r$ let $g_c:=f_r-f_c$ be the logit
margin. Define the first-order and curvature constants
\begin{align}
L_1^{(c)}&:=\norm{(W_r-W_c)\,S_{c,v}}_2,
\label{eq:L1def}\\
C_v&:=\norm{W_r-W_c}_2\cdot\frac{L_{J,v}}{2(1-\kappa)^2},
\label{eq:Cvdef}
\end{align}
where $L_{J,v}\le\norm{W}_2^2\norm{\zstar}$ is the per-node curvature of \cref{app:proof_transfer} and
$\kappa=\norm{J_z}_2<1$ (A3). Equation \eqref{eq:L1def} is the first-order margin sensitivity;
\eqref{eq:Cvdef} is the curvature constant $L_J/(2(1-\kappa)^2)$ of \cref{prop:transfer} scaled by the
readout gap. The aggregate node constant in \cref{eq:conformal} takes the competitor-worst values,
$L_1^{c}=\max_{c\ne r}L_1^{(c)}$ and $C_v$ with the worst $\norm{W_r-W_c}_2$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Why the gradient is the readout gap, not $\nabla_z\score$]
\label{rem:margin-not-grad}
The constant \eqref{eq:L1def} uses the logit-margin rows $W_r-W_c$ rather than a softmax-Jacobian row
$\nabla_z\score_r$, for two reasons. First, both scores depend on the logits only through the margins
$\{g_c\}_{c\ne r}$ (softmax sees logits only through differences), so controlling every margin controls
$\pi_r$ and $\rho_r$ and hence both scores (\cref{lem:score-shift}). Second, the margin form needs no
bound on softmax curvature: it yields a sound, one-sided score drop without the $1/4$ softmax-Lipschitz
slack. The $\nabla_z\score$ form is literally correct only for TPS, whereas the margin form covers both.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Worst-case conformity-score shift]
\label{lem:score-shift}
Assume (A1)--(A3), $\varepsilon<\ecrit$, and a linear head $f=W\zstar_v+b$. Then for every feasible
$\delta\Ahat$ with $\norm{\delta\Ahat}_F\le\varepsilon$ and every label $r$,
\begin{equation}
\big|g_c(\Ahat+\delta\Ahat)-g_c(\Ahat)\big|\le L_1^{(c)}\varepsilon+C_v\varepsilon^2
\label{eq:margin-shift}
\end{equation}
for every competitor $c\ne r$, and consequently the worst-case drop of the conformity score of label
$r$ obeys
\begin{multline}
\score_r(v;\Ahat)-\score_r(v;\Ahat+\delta\Ahat)\le\Delta_r(\varepsilon),\\
\Delta_r(\varepsilon):=\Psi_r\!\big(\{L_1^{(c)}\varepsilon+C_v\varepsilon^2\}_{c\ne r}\big),
\label{eq:score-drop}
\end{multline}
where $\Psi_r$ is the monotone map from margin decrements to the induced score decrement. Taking the
competitor-worst constants gives the headline $\Delta_r(\varepsilon)\le L_1^{c}\varepsilon+C_v\varepsilon^2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: the linear term (Cauchy--Schwarz).}
Because the head is affine, $g_c=(W_r-W_c)\zstar_v+(b_r-b_c)$, so
$g_c'-g_c=(W_r-W_c)\Delta\zstar_v$. Inserting \eqref{eq:ift-firstorder} splits this into a first-order
and a remainder part,
\begin{equation}
g_c'-g_c=(W_r-W_c)\,S_{c,v}\,\boldsymbol\delta+(W_r-W_c)\,R_v.
\label{eq:margin-decomp}
\end{equation}
The first summand is a fixed covector paired with $\boldsymbol\delta$, so Cauchy--Schwarz and
$\norm{\boldsymbol\delta}_2\le\varepsilon$ bound it by $L_1^{(c)}\varepsilon$ through definition
\eqref{eq:L1def}. This is the same step that proves the radius $r_v$, now read with the readout gap
$W_r-W_c$ in place of the full $S_v$ image, so the constant is the rate at which the most exposed margin
moves per unit perturbation.

\emph{Step 2: the quadratic term (curvature).}
For the remainder, $\abs{(W_r-W_c)R_v}\le\norm{W_r-W_c}_2\norm{R_v}_2$. By \cref{app:proof_transfer} the
equilibrium-map Hessian is the complete second derivative \eqref{eq:hessian-full}, bounded in operator
norm by $(1-\kappa)^{-2}L_{J,v}$ with $L_{J,v}\le\norm{W}_2^2\norm{\zstar}$ finite under the contraction
(A3). A second-order Taylor expansion along the feasible segment (finitely many ReLU regions,
conservative IFT on each) therefore gives
$\norm{R_v}_2\le\tfrac12(1-\kappa)^{-2}L_{J,v}\,\varepsilon^2$, hence
$\abs{(W_r-W_c)R_v}\le C_v\varepsilon^2$ by \eqref{eq:Cvdef}. Combining the two summands in
\eqref{eq:margin-decomp} with the triangle inequality yields \eqref{eq:margin-shift}.

\emph{Step 3: from margins to scores.}
Both scores read the logits only through the margins $\{g_c\}_{c\ne r}$: the softmax probability is
$\pi_r=(\sum_c e^{-g_c})^{-1}$ with $g_r:=0$, and the rank set $\{\pi_c>\pi_r\}=\{g_c<0\}$ behind
$\rho_r$ turns on the margin signs alone. The adversary minimizing $\score_r$ may therefore lower each
margin freely, $g_c\mapsto g_c-\delta_c$ with $0\le\delta_c\le L_1^{(c)}\varepsilon+C_v\varepsilon^2$
from Steps~1--2; we bound the score over this box of margins and call the largest admissible drop
$\Psi_r$.

For TPS the box has one worst corner: $\pi_r$ is nonincreasing in every decrement $\delta_c$, so lowering
all margins to their budgets and recomputing the softmax is the exact worst case, with no
softmax-Lipschitz constant. APS needs one more observation, because $\rho_r$ sums over a margin-dependent
set. Write the drop as $(\rho_r'-\rho_r)+u_v(\pi_r'-\pi_r)$. Lowering margins only weakens $r$, so
$\pi_r'\le\pi_r$ and the second term is favorable; discarding it leaves the sound bound
$\score_r-\score_r'\le\rho_r'-\rho_r$. The survivor $\rho_r$ is the probability mass of competitors
ranked above $r$, and lowering \emph{every} margin to its budget makes $r$ simultaneously weakest against
all of them, maximizing that mass. The same all-lowered corner therefore dominates APS too, and
evaluating $\Psi_r$ there is a valid upper bound, with no enumeration of rank crossings. Either score
then gives \eqref{eq:score-drop}, and the competitor-worst constants give the headline form.
\end{proof}

The shift bound is a margin budget read in two pieces. The linear constant $L_1^{c}$ is the rate at
which the most exposed decision margin moves, factored as the readout gap $\norm{W_{y_v}-W_c}_2$ times
the equilibrium sensitivity $S_{c,v}$, the same two-amplifier product as the radius $r_v$ but expressed
in score units. The quadratic constant $C_v$ is the price of leaving the linear regime, the curvature
$L_J/(2(1-\kappa)^2)$ with its two resolvents, scaled by the readout gap. Carrying the quadratic term is
what makes the certificate sound rather than merely first-order: the bare $\sigma_1(S_c)\varepsilon$
screen can be breached just past $r_v$, whereas $L_1^{c}\varepsilon+C_v\varepsilon^2$ over-states the
true drop for every $\varepsilon<\ecrit$, which is why the empirical gate is conservative ($0.95$--$1.00$
at $\varepsilon=0.05$) rather than tight.

\begin{remark}[Where rigor stops, honestly]
\label{rem:conf-caveats}
Two load-bearing conditions remain. (1) \textbf{Affine head.} Step 1 needs $f$ affine in $\zstar_v$; a
nonlinear readout would replace $W_r-W_c$ by the head Jacobian and add a Lipschitz factor. Our models
use a linear head, so this holds with equality. (2) \textbf{Contractive regime.} The curvature bound and
the finiteness of $\norm{\zstar}$ both need $\kappa<1$ and $\varepsilon<\ecrit$ (A3); past $\ecrit$ the
resolvent $1/(1-\kappa)$ is meaningless and the bound is void, the same scope as
\cref{thm:phase_transition}. On the conformal subgraph $\kappa\approx0.68$, so $(1-\kappa)^{-2}\approx9.8$
inflates $C_v$, yet the gate confirms the bound is not breached. The earlier worry about ``omitted
higher-order terms'' does not arise: \cref{app:proof_transfer} shows the operator is bilinear on each
linear region, so \eqref{eq:hessian-full} is the \emph{complete} equilibrium Hessian and $C_v$ rests on
the full second derivative, not a dominant-term reduction.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Robust coverage of \AEGIS-Conformal]
\label{thm:robust-cov}
Let $\{(v_i,y_{v_i})\}_{i\in\mathrm{cal}}$ be a split-conformal calibration set and $v$ a test node.
Assume:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(C1)] \emph{Exchangeability.} The clean true-label scores
$\{\score_{y_{v_i}}(v_i)\}_{i\in\mathrm{cal}}\cup\{\score_{y_v}(v)\}$ are exchangeable (an inductive
split, or a transductive split with a permutation-invariant predictor; \citealp{zargarbashi2023conformal}).
\item[(C2)] \emph{Score-shift bound.} For every calibration node $w$ and the test node, simultaneously
over all feasible $\norm{\delta\Ahat}_F\le\varepsilon<\ecrit$,
$\score_{y_w}(w;\Ahat+\delta\Ahat)\ge\score_{y_w}(w;\Ahat)-\Delta_{y_w}(\varepsilon)$ with
$\Delta_{y_w}$ the bound of \cref{lem:score-shift}.
\end{enumerate}
Form the calibration-robust threshold
\begin{multline}
\hat q_{\mathrm{rob}}=\mathrm{Quantile}_{\lceil(n_{\mathrm{cal}}+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil/n_{\mathrm{cal}}}\\
\Big(\big\{\score_{y_{v_i}}(v_i;\Ahat)-\Delta_{y_{v_i}}(\varepsilon)\big\}_{i\in\mathrm{cal}}\Big),
\label{eq:qrob}
\end{multline}
and the robust set $\Cset_\varepsilon(v;\Ahat')=\{r:\score_r(v;\Ahat')\ge\hat q_{\mathrm{rob}}\}$. Then
\begin{multline}
\Pr\!\big[y_v\in\Cset_\varepsilon(v;\Ahat+\delta\Ahat)\big]\ge1-\alpha\\
\text{for all feasible }\norm{\delta\Ahat}_F\le\varepsilon.
\label{eq:robcov}
\end{multline}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: reduce to lowered scores.}
Define the lowered scores
$\tilde\score_{y_w}(w):=\score_{y_w}(w;\Ahat)-\Delta_{y_w}(\varepsilon)$ on calibration and test. By
(C2), the realized perturbed true-label score dominates its lowered clean value,
$\score_{y_w}(w;\Ahat+\delta\Ahat)\ge\tilde\score_{y_w}(w)$, for every feasible $\delta\Ahat$ at once.
This is exactly the guaranteed lower envelope the binary certificate of \citet{zargarbashi2023conformal}
requires; \cref{lem:score-shift} supplies it analytically for the continuous $\varepsilon$-ball,
replacing their combinatorial enumeration of a discrete neighbourhood.

\emph{Step 2: the lowered scores stay exchangeable.}
Each node's lowering $\Delta_{y_w}(\varepsilon)$ is a deterministic function of that node's own
$(W_{y_w}-W_c,\,S_{c,w},\,L_{J,w})$, a per-point measurable transform of its clean score context, and
the tie-break $u_{(\cdot)}$ is shared. Under (C1) the clean scores are exchangeable, and a common
per-point transform preserves exchangeability, so the lowered scores are exchangeable too.

\emph{Step 3: split-conformal validity on the lowered scores.}
By the standard split-conformal guarantee \citep{vovk2005algorithmic} applied to the exchangeable
lowered scores, with $\hat q_{\mathrm{rob}}$ the $\lceil(n_{\mathrm{cal}}+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil$-th smallest
calibration value \eqref{eq:qrob}, $\Pr[\tilde\score_{y_v}(v)\ge\hat q_{\mathrm{rob}}]\ge1-\alpha$.

\emph{Step 4: transfer to the whole ball.}
On the event $\{\tilde\score_{y_v}(v)\ge\hat q_{\mathrm{rob}}\}$, Step 1 gives
$\score_{y_v}(v;\Ahat+\delta\Ahat)\ge\tilde\score_{y_v}(v)\ge\hat q_{\mathrm{rob}}$ for every feasible
$\delta\Ahat$ simultaneously, because the envelope is uniform over the ball rather than per-$\delta$.
Hence $y_v\in\Cset_\varepsilon(v;\Ahat+\delta\Ahat)$ on that event, and \eqref{eq:robcov} follows from
the bound of Step 3. At $\varepsilon=0$, $\Delta\equiv0$ and the statement reduces to the ordinary
split-conformal $1-\alpha$ guarantee, as it must.
\end{proof}

The certificate buys robustness by paying the worst-case score drop up front, on the calibration set.
Lowering every calibration true-label score by its own analytic $\Delta(\varepsilon)$ before taking the
quantile raises the threshold just enough that any in-ball perturbation of the test node, which can
lower its true-label score by at most its own $\Delta(\varepsilon)$, still clears the bar. Because the
lowering is per-node and deterministic it commutes with exchangeability, so the only probabilistic
content is the ordinary split-conformal quantile event; the uniformity in Step 4 is what upgrades
``robust to a fixed attack'' into ``robust over the whole ball.''

\begin{remark}[Honest status of the coverage guarantee]
\label{rem:exchange-honesty}
(C1) is the load-bearing hypothesis, and it does not hold for free on a single fixed transductive graph:
calibration and test nodes share one realized adjacency, and an IGNN's output at $v$ depends on the
whole graph, so the scores are not i.i.d.\ and exchangeability must be imposed by design (inductive
sampling, or a transductive-exchangeability argument as in \citealp{zargarbashi2023conformal}). We
therefore state \eqref{eq:robcov} as sound under (C1), with the empirical coverage (clean coverage
$\approx0.90$) and the worst-case gate ($0.90$ at $\varepsilon=0.01$, $0.95$--$1.00$ at
$\varepsilon=0.05$, zero equilibrium divergence across all $4138$ gate nodes) as evidence that (C1)
holds well enough on these graphs. The reduction to \citet{zargarbashi2023conformal} is faithful: their
construction yields robust coverage from any uniform worst-case score envelope, and
\cref{lem:score-shift} is exactly such an envelope, computed analytically with zero Monte-Carlo
smoothing.
\end{remark}
