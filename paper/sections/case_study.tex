\section{Case Study: Auditing a Fraud Detector}
\label{sec:fraud_case}
%% ===================================================================

GNN fraud detectors are a prime adversarial target: a flagged account can evade detection by perturbing a few behavioural-similarity edges~\cite{zugner2018adversarial,dou2020enhancing}. \AEGIS audits such a detector directly --- no labels, no retraining --- reading from \emph{one} $S_c$ query which edges most threaten its predictions. On an Amazon Fraud cluster the edge-weighted ranking $A_{ij}v_{ij}$ (\cref{prop:transfer}) reproduces the brute-force single-edge-removal damage ranking ($\tau{=}1.0$ on the cluster) at one query's cost rather than $|E|$ reconvergences. The audit is validated at scale and \emph{non-circularly}: the ranking transfers across the full fraud graph (\cref{fig:tau_heatmap}), precisely where uniform edge weights are uninformative so $v_{ij}$ adds the most rank information ($\Delta\tau$ up to $\mathbf{+0.65}$), and the SVD direction transfers from a separately trained surrogate ($\cos{=}0.99$; \cref{sec:adaptive}), so $v_{ij}$ reflects model-intrinsic vulnerability rather than a gradient artifact. The detailed walk-through, with the audited cluster and its fault-line diagram, is in \cref{app:fraud}.
