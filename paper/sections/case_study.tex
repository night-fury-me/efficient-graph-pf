\section{Case Study: Auditing a Fraud Detector}
\label{sec:fraud_case}
%% ===================================================================

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig_fraud_case.pdf}
\caption{\AEGIS auditing an IGNN fraud detector on an Amazon Fraud cluster~\cite{dou2020enhancing}. Icons mark fraud / benign accounts; the audited account sits at the hub. Green edges are the top-5 adversarial fault lines by the edge-weighted score $A_{ij}v_{ij}$; yellow dots mark the three also in the brute-force single-edge-removal damage top-3.}
\label{fig:fraud_case}
\end{figure}

GNN fraud detectors are a prime adversarial target: a flagged account can slip past detection by perturbing a few behavioural-similarity edges~\cite{zugner2018adversarial,dou2020enhancing}. \AEGIS audits such a detector directly, with no labels and no retraining, reading off from a \emph{single} $S_c$ query which edges most threaten its predictions. \Cref{fig:fraud_case} shows the audit on an Amazon Fraud cluster: the edge-weighted ranking $A_{ij}v_{ij}$ (\cref{prop:transfer}) pinpoints the fault lines, reproducing the brute-force single-edge-removal damage ranking ($\tau{=}1.0$ on this cluster) at the cost of one query rather than $|E|$ reconvergences. The audit is \emph{non-circular}: the ranking transfers across the full fraud graph (\cref{fig:tau_heatmap}, where uniform edge weights are least informative, so $v_{ij}$ adds the most rank information, with $\Delta\tau$ up to $\mathbf{+0.65}$), and the SVD direction transfers from a separately trained surrogate (\cref{sec:adaptive}), confirming that $v_{ij}$ is model-intrinsic rather than a gradient artifact. The full walk-through is in \cref{app:fraud}.
