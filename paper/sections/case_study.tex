\section{Case Study: Power Flow Contingency}
\label{sec:power_flow}
%% ===================================================================

A natural question is whether AEGIS transfers beyond standard graph benchmarks. We demonstrate on a fundamentally different domain---AC power flow---where the adversarial vulnerability spectrum recovers a classical engineering problem: N-1 contingency analysis.

\subsection{Background: N-1 Contingency}

Power grid operators must ensure that no single transmission line failure causes system instability. The standard N-1 contingency assessment removes each line in turn, re-solves the power flow equations, and ranks lines by the severity of the resulting voltage/flow deviation. This brute-force procedure scales as $O(|E|)$ full power flow solves---expensive for large grids and infeasible for real-time operation.

We observe that AEGIS's per-edge vulnerability $v_{ij} = \norm{S_{:,iN+j} + S_{:,jN+i}}$ computes the same quantity via a single IFT analysis. The mathematical correspondence is exact:

\small

\toprule
\textbf{ML concept} & \textbf{Power systems concept} \\
\midrule
Edge perturbation $\delta A_{ij}$ & N-1 line outage \\
Vulnerability spectrum $v_{ij}$ & Contingency severity \\
Critical budget $\ecrit$ & Maximum tolerable outage \\
Phase transition $\rho \to 1$ & Voltage collapse threshold \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

We train ContractiveGCN-PF---an IGNN variant for AC power flow with binary adjacency from the admittance matrix and 5 bus features (slack/PV indicators, P, Q, $|V|$)---on four standard IEEE test cases generated via PandaPower (2,000 samples each). Unlike graph benchmarks, power flow grids are small enough (14--118 buses) to analyze without subgraph extraction.

\subsection{Results}

Three observations emerge. First, tightness of $\approx 1.00$ confirms that the exact first-order prediction property is not specific to citation/e-commerce graphs but transfers to an entirely different physical domain. Second, N-1 ranking correlation is strongest on larger grids (case57: $\tau = +0.67$, case118: $\tau = +0.62$), consistent with larger grids exhibiting more heterogeneous edge criticalities for IFT to differentiate. Third, the gap between first-order prediction and brute-force N-1 ($\tau < 1.0$) reflects the nonlinearity of full edge removal---a finite perturbation for which higher-order terms matter. AEGIS captures the correct ranking direction but underestimates the magnitude of large-perturbation effects.

\textbf{Practical implication.} For a 118-bus grid, brute-force N-1 requires 179 full power flow re-solves. AEGIS computes the same ranking in a single IFT analysis (2.3s), providing a $\sim$100$\times$ speedup with 62\% top-5 agreement. This positions AEGIS as a fast screening tool for real-time contingency assessment, with brute-force verification reserved for the top-ranked candidates.
