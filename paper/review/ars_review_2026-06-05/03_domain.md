# R2 — Domain Referee (GNN Adversarial Robustness & Certification)

**Recommendation:** Major Revision. The unification framing is a real and useful contribution to how the community organizes audit/certify/defend, but the paper must (a) sharpen what is *new* versus a careful assembly of known tools, (b) defend the contractive-IGNN restriction against a literature that has largely moved past it, and (c) close several positioning gaps.

## What the field gets from this paper
The community has three siloed threads — structural attacks, certifiers, and robust architectures — and AEGIS's thesis that all three are readings of one sensitivity operator is a genuinely clarifying organizing principle. The seven-axis radar (Fig. 1) is a fair *map* of the silos even if its "nonzero on all axes" claim is weak. If the paper delivers what it claims, "one query on an off-the-shelf model gives an edge ranking + a per-node certificate + a defense knob" is a useful practitioner story. I want to see this published *somewhere*; the question is whether this version earns a top-tier slot.

## Novelty — the central question
`S_c = (I−J_z)^{-1} J_A P_c` is **equilibrium IFT sensitivity (standard for DEQ/implicit models: Koh–Liang influence functions, Gould et al. deep declarative networks, Lorraine et al.) restricted to structural edge perturbations via a duplication-matrix projection** `P_c` (Magnus–Neudecker, also standard). The mathematical primitives — Neumann/resolvent expansion, randomized SVD (Halko et al.), Cauchy–Schwarz margin radii, spectral-norm regularization (Miyato et al.) — are all off-the-shelf. The novelty is therefore:
1. The **structural specialization** (IFT sensitivity to `vec(A)` rather than to inputs/loss) — modest but real; the related-work paragraph correctly notes prior implicit-network work targets `∂z*/∂x`, not `∂Z/∂A`.
2. The **coupling** claim — that the *same* operator's `σ_1` is simultaneously the attack, the (inverse) certified budget, and the defense penalty. This is the paper's strongest novelty and the empirical anticorrelation (−0.65, 10/10 seeds, `sec:defense`) is the right evidence for it. **Make this the headline novelty**, not τ=0.99.

The paper should state plainly: "the components are known; the contribution is that they are one operator, and the coupling is what is new." Right now the contributions list (intro) reads as four independent novelties, inviting each to be knocked down individually.

## The defense pillar is the weakest novelty
`sec:defense` penalizes `σ_1(S_c)` — this is a sensitivity/Lipschitz-style spectral regularizer, conceptually close to existing graph-robustness regularization (the abbahaddou2024bounding weight-norm line you cite, and the broader Lipschitz-regularization literature). The ablation then concedes vulnerability is **delocalized** (participation ratio 41–89 edges; edge-masking cuts damage only 2.4–4.6% at k=5–10 on the full graph, `app:ablations`). So the "find the vulnerable edges and defend them" story does *not* hold at scale — the defense reduces to a global spectral penalty costing ~4–6 accuracy points. This is honest, but it undercuts the per-edge-actionability selling point. Either (a) demonstrate the defense beats a plain spectral-norm / GNNGuard / RobustGCN baseline at matched accuracy (currently `app:explicit` only says they "match or exceed IGNN"), or (b) downgrade the defense from a "pillar" to a corollary.

## Contractive-IGNN restriction vs the field
The closed-form `ε_crit` and the bracket are restricted to contractive implicit GNNs (1 of your 7 architectures). The field overwhelmingly deploys *explicit* message-passing GNNs. `prop:explicit` extends the *operator* (not the boundary) to K-layer nets via the unrolled `S_K`, which is the right move — but then the unique theoretical content (`ε_crit`, the bracket) applies only to a model class most practitioners do not use. The paper should be explicit that the *theory* is an IGNN result and the *tool* is general, and should justify why IGNNs are worth a closed-form treatment (e.g., as a tractable proxy) rather than leaving the reader to infer it.

## Positioning / missing engagement
- **Graph conformal prediction.** You cite Zargarbashi et al. 2023 and reduce to their binary certificate — good. But the robust-conformal-under-adversary space is broader (e.g., conformal under distribution shift / adversarial conformal in the vision literature); a sentence situating AEGIS-Conformal against adversarially-robust conformal generally would help.
- **Certified robustness baselines.** The comparison is essentially smoothing (Bojchevski et al.) + AGNNCert (qualitative). Convex-relaxation certifiers (Zügner & Günnemann 2019, which you cite but do not compare) and the collective/sparse-smoothing line deserve at least a positioning table on *what is certified* (continuous Frobenius budget vs discrete edit count vs per-node vs collective) — the threat-model axis is where AEGIS is genuinely different, so make that table explicit.
- **Over-squashing / curvature.** You cite Topping et al. and Di Giovanni et al. as "aggregate, not per-edge." Fair, but the sensitivity-to-structure connection is close enough that one sentence on why `S_c` is *not* just a per-edge over-squashing measure would preempt a reviewer conflation.
- **Influence functions at scale.** Since `S_c` is an influence-function descendant, engage the known fragility/criticisms of influence functions in deep nets (Basu et al. "Influence functions are fragile") — the natural reviewer question is whether `S_c` inherits that fragility; your transfer/finite-difference checks are the answer, so connect them explicitly.

## Contribution increment — verdict
Real but currently *over-claimed*. The defensible increment is: a structural-sensitivity operator whose `σ_1` couples attack, certificate, and defense, with a matrix-free implementation that scales the *audit* an order of magnitude, plus a sampling-free robust-conformal certificate that is orders of magnitude cheaper than smoothing. That is a solid contribution if framed as coupling + cost, not as winning τ or attack strength. As framed (four independent SOTA-flavored claims), it invites rejection by attrition.

**Scores:** Novelty 6 · Literature coverage 6 (good breadth, thin head-to-head) · Contribution 6 → **Major Revision.**
