# R3 — Cross-Disciplinary / Impact Referee

**Recommendation:** Major Revision. I read this as an applied robustness referee asking "would a deployment team actually use this, and is the motivating story honest?" The tool is real but three assumptions stand between the math and the deployment claim, and the most prominent motivating domain is the one the paper ultimately disclaims.

## 1. The motivating triad vs what is delivered
The intro opens with three deployment stakes: fraud (perturbed transaction edges), drug-interaction (perturbed molecular graphs), and **power grids** (missed contingencies under structural fragility). Of these, only **fraud** is demonstrated (the `sec:fraud_case` audit). Drug-interaction never recurs. And power grids are explicitly *disclaimed* in the conclusion: "AEGIS audits vulnerability, not physics: a contractive surrogate cannot model voltage collapse, so it complements rather than replaces power-flow contingency screening."

Leading with a domain you then concede the method cannot model is a self-inflicted wound. A reviewer who knows power systems will see the intro promise and the conclusion retraction and discount the whole motivation. **Recommendation:** demote power grids (and drug-interaction, if not shown) to a single "potential applications" sentence, and move the honest "we audit the *model*, not the physical system" scoping *into the intro*, not the conclusion. The fraud story is enough to motivate the paper on its own; let it.

## 2. Fundamental-assumption challenge: equilibrium shift ≠ vulnerability
The whole edifice equates "vulnerability" with first-order *equilibrium sensitivity* `‖ΔZ*‖`. But a deployment team cares about *decision* failures. The paper's own numbers say these diverge: the "optimal attack" moves the equilibrium a lot while flipping 0–1.8% of labels at ε=0.10 (`app:attack_full`), and the per-edge *classification* gradient actually *anti-correlates* with the discrete ground truth on Cora (τ=−0.20) while `S_c` gives +0.32 (`app:attack_full`). The paper frames the latter as "equilibrium and classification sensitivities are distinct vulnerability surfaces" — but that cuts both ways: if they are distinct, why is the equilibrium surface the right one to audit for a classifier whose *outputs* are what matter? The paper needs an argument (or evidence) that high-`S_c` edges are the ones a real attacker/operator should care about, beyond "they move the hidden state."

## 3. The contractive-IGNN assumption is a deployment bottleneck
Real GNN fraud/recommendation/grid systems are almost never spectrally-normalized contractive IGNNs. The paper's tool extends to explicit GNNs via `S_K` (good), but: (a) the *certificate* `ε_crit` and the *defense's* cleanest story live in the IGNN world; (b) the explicit-GNN `S_K` is formed by *finite differences* in the experiments (`app:explicit`), which is `O(|E|)` forward passes — at odds with the "one matrix-free query" selling point for the very architectures practitioners deploy. Clarify: for an off-the-shelf 2-layer GCN, is the audit one JVP-based query, or `|E|` finite-difference probes? If the latter, the "one query" headline does not transfer to the deployment-relevant case.

## 4. Delocalization undercuts the actionability promise
`app:ablations` is admirably honest: the leading attack mode is delocalized (participation ratio 41–89 edges), so the audit "localizes risk for triage, not removal." But the practitioner pitch is *actionability* — "which edges to monitor/protect." If protecting the top-10 edges removes 2.4–4.6% of the risk, the operational recommendation is unclear. What *does* a defender do with a delocalized ranking? The paper should give the honest operational answer (monitoring prioritization? aggregate spectral hardening?) rather than implying edge-level remediation.

## 5. Dual-use / disclosure — well-handled, one gap
The 90-day coordinated-notification window and gating attack-direction reconstruction behind ethics review (`conclusion`, `app:repro`) is thoughtful and above the norm. One gap: the diagnostic path (`r_v`, `v_ij`) is released unconditionally, but the case study shows `A_ij·v_ij` *is* an effective edge-attack ranking that reproduces brute-force N-1 (`sec:fraud_case`, τ=1.0). So the "diagnostic-only" release still hands an attacker a high-quality edge-prioritization. Reconcile the threat model of the *release policy* with the demonstrated offensive utility of the diagnostic scores.

## 6. Where the cross-disciplinary value genuinely lands
The sampling-free conformal certificate at 10^3–10^4× lower cost than smoothing is the result with the broadest reach — uncertainty quantification under adversarial structure, computed in ~1s, is attractive well beyond GNNs. If the paper re-centered on *cheap robust coverage from a closed-form sensitivity bound*, with the audit/defense as supporting acts, the impact narrative would be both more honest and more compelling than the current "wins everything a little" radar.

**Scores:** Practical impact 6 · Soundness of motivation 4 (power-grid framing) · Significance of the assumptions challenge: high → **Major Revision.**
