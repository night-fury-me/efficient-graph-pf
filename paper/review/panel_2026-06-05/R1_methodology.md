# Review R1 — Empirical / Adversarial-ML Methodology

**Submission:** "AEGIS: A Matrix-Free Operator to Audit, Certify, and Defend Graph Neural Networks" (AAAI-2026, anonymous)
**Reviewer role:** Peer Reviewer 1 — empirical adversarial-ML methodologist (experimental design, metrics, baseline fairness, statistics, reproducibility)
**Date:** 2026-06-05

---

## Summary

AEGIS proposes a single matrix-free object, the constrained sensitivity matrix `S_c = (I - J_z)^{-1} J_A P_c`, and reads from one randomized-SVD query three things: an attack direction (leading singular vector), a per-edge vulnerability ranking (`v_ij = ||S_c e_ij||`), and a per-node first-order safe radius `r_v`. The same operator is then turned into (i) a distribution-free conformal certificate (AEGIS-Conformal) via a closed-form worst-case conformity-score-shift bound, and (ii) a defense by penalizing `σ_1(S_c)` during training. The paper is well organized, the engineering (matrix-free Neumann/JVP + rSVD) is genuinely useful, and several appendix sections (E_conformal, ablations) are unusually candid about scope.

My core methodological concern is that **the headline efficacy claims are stated almost entirely in a metric — equilibrium / hidden-state shift `||ΔZ*||` — that structurally flatters a hidden-state-sensitivity method and is not shown to be decision-relevant.** The paper concedes that prediction flips stay at 0–1.8% (ε=0.10) for *all* attackers including AEGIS, and only reaches 27.4% in one right-skewed tail (Pubmed, ε=0.20). No table anywhere shows AEGIS changing *decisions* (misclassification / flip rate / accuracy drop) competitively against PGD or Mettack at matched budget. The "74–156×" and "3–10× more than Mettack (149/150 wins)" numbers are advantages on this hidden-state metric, not on any task outcome. Combined with a convenient 50-node-subgraph protocol for nearly all IGNN results and several apples-to-not-quite-apples baseline framings (one query vs 125 epochs; conformal abstaining on the matched ball it is compared on), the empirical case as *presented* over-claims relative to what the experiments establish. The underlying tool (a fast, label-free first-order edge-sensitivity proxy + a cheap conformal certificate) is real and valuable; the framing as an "optimal attack" that "matches/beats PGD" is the part that needs to be reined in or earned with decision-level evidence.

---

## Overall recommendation

**Major Revision** — confidence **4/5**.

The contribution is real and the writing is strong, but the central efficacy claims rest on a metric whose decision-relevance is not demonstrated, and the most-cited numbers (74–156×, τ=0.98, 149/150) each have a framing or fairness issue that a careful reader will catch. These are addressable with experiments the authors are clearly equipped to run (the harness exists), so I do not recommend rejection — but they are not cosmetic, so I cannot recommend minor revision.

## Strengths (specific)

1. **The matrix-free pipeline is a real systems contribution.** `S_c v = (I-J_z)^{-1} J_A P_c v` via Neumann + JVP + rSVD (`k=p=10, n_iter=2`, early-stop at `1e-6`) lifts the dense `O((Nd)^3)` ceiling from N≈300 to N=7,650 at 365 s / 5.5 GB (Fig. scalability, app:phase_scal). Matrix-free σ_1 agrees with dense to 0.03% at N=200 (per-edge τ=0.999). This self-consistency check is exactly right.
2. **Conformal section is honest where it matters.** The "Honest status" remark (rem:exchange-honesty, E_conformal) explicitly states that (C1) exchangeability "does not hold for free on a single fixed transductive graph" and must be imposed by design. The proof (Thm robust-cov) is a clean reduction to Zargarbashi et al. via a uniform worst-case score envelope. This is the most rigorous part of the paper.
3. **Non-circularity checks for the attack direction are well designed.** Transfer from a *separately trained surrogate* with zero shared gradients recovering 99% of one-query damage (cos=0.99), vs 44±4% for a 512-query black-box search (app:attack_full), is a good control against the "you're just reading the victim's gradients" objection.
4. **The decomposition tab:da_decomp is the right ablation** — it isolates whether the sensitivity `v_ij` adds anything over an edge-weight-only baseline (Δτ = τ_Av − τ_A), and shows it is decisive where weights are uninformative (Citeseer τ_A≈0; Amazon Fraud +0.27 to +0.90). This is honest about *why* the ranking works.
5. **10 fixed seeds throughout**, consistent ± reporting, and an explicit limitations paragraph (exchangeability, dense-at-N=200, GAT/binary-mask out of scope, ~5-pt accuracy gap to best explicit arch, "audits vulnerability not physics").

## Weaknesses

### CRITICAL

**C1. The headline efficacy metric (equilibrium / hidden-state shift) is not shown to be decision-relevant; no decision-level head-to-head exists.**
- *What's wrong:* Every comparison establishing AEGIS's "attack" superiority is measured in `||ΔZ*||` (equilibrium shift), the quantity AEGIS's leading singular vector maximizes *by construction* (prop:attack). The "74–156× per-query advantage" (tab:attack_full, sec:audit), "matches/beats 50-step PGD," "3–10× more equilibrium damage than Mettack ... 149/150 wins, p<10^{-43}," and the budget-sweep "damage-equivalence" with GR-BCD/PR-BCD (tab:baselines, ratios ~0.96–1.33) are *all* on this metric. The paper itself concedes (app:attack_full; sec:audit) that **prediction flips stay 0–1.8% for ALL methods at ε=0.10**, and reaches only a tail 27.4% on Pubmed at ε=0.20. A method that maximizes hidden-state shift will win a hidden-state-shift contest; that does not establish it finds the *worst attack* in any decision sense.
- *Where:* abstract; sec:audit (tab:attack_full); app:attack_full; tab:baselines / fig:exp3_budget_sweep; "Breach rates" paragraph (fig:breach).
- *Why it matters:* In adversarial ML the operative question is whether perturbations change *decisions* at a given budget. On that question the paper's own evidence shows AEGIS, PGD, and Mettack are all roughly equally ineffective at small ε (flips ≤2%). The abstract's "one query inflicts 74–156× the per-query equilibrium damage of a 50-step PGD attack" reads as an attack-strength claim but is a hidden-state-shift-per-query claim where PGD is deliberately run at 50 steps and AEGIS at one. This is the difference between "AEGIS is a strong attack" (not demonstrated) and "AEGIS is a cheap first-order *sensitivity proxy*" (demonstrated). The paper is sold as the former.
- *Concrete fix:* (a) Add a head-to-head **flip-rate / accuracy-drop vs ε** table for AEGIS, Cls-PGD, Mettack, GR-BCD at **matched budget** (matched k or matched ||δ||_F, matched compute), on the *same* axis, with CIs. If AEGIS is competitive on decisions, that is the headline and it is strong. (b) If AEGIS is *not* competitive on decisions (likely, given the 0–1.8% concession), then reframe throughout: AEGIS is a label-free first-order *audit/attribution proxy and certificate*, not an attack that "beats PGD." Drop or heavily qualify "optimal attack" / "finds the worst attack" / "74–156×" in the abstract and intro; state the metric inline every time. (c) Report `||ΔZ*||` alongside flip rate in the same table so readers can see the gap.

**C2. "Matches/beats Mettack/PGD" rests on a metric mismatch that is favorable to AEGIS, and Shift-PGD is excluded from being a baseline precisely because it would be the fair comparison.**
- *What's wrong:* The four-quadrant table (tab:attack_full) has two PGD columns: Cls-PGD (classification-loss) and Shift-PGD (IFT-gradient, i.e. PGD optimizing the *same* equilibrium-shift objective AEGIS maximizes). Shift-PGD is the genuinely matched baseline on this metric — and AEGIS only modestly edges it (Cora 3.70 vs 3.05; WikiCS 2.10 vs 1.93; ~5–20%). The paper labels Shift-PGD "solver validation, not an independent baseline" and instead foregrounds the comparison to Cls-PGD (3.70 vs 2.51), which optimizes a *different* objective and is therefore expected to underperform on equilibrium shift. So the impressive-looking margins are partly an artifact of comparing against an attacker optimizing the wrong loss.
- *Where:* tab:attack_full caption and sec:audit; app:attack_full ("classification-loss PGD inflicts 15–70% less ... IFT-gradient PGD 72–92%").
- *Why it matters:* The fair statement is "AEGIS's one closed-form query recovers 72–92% (and sometimes exceeds) what a 50-step PGD on the same objective achieves" — a *solver-efficiency* result, not an attack-superiority result. Calling the matched comparison "not a baseline" while headlining the mismatched one inverts which number is load-bearing.
- *Concrete fix:* Promote Shift-PGD to a first-class baseline and report the AEGIS/Shift-PGD ratio as the honest efficiency claim. Demote the Cls-PGD comparison to "different objective, expected gap." Apply the same logic to Mettack: Mettack optimizes a meta-learned classification loss, so "3–10× more equilibrium damage" is again a cross-objective comparison; either add an equilibrium-shift-optimizing discrete attacker or state that the comparison is cross-objective.

### MAJOR

**M3. External validity: nearly all IGNN results use 50-node BFS ego-subgraphs around the highest-degree node, an easy/convenient regime the paper's own ablations show does not generalize.**
- *What's wrong:* "Unless stated otherwise, IGNN runs on 50-node BFS ego-subgraphs around the highest-degree node and reports *local* vulnerability" (sec:audit). The subgraphs in tab:baselines have only |E| = 53–85 edges. The paper's own ablation (app:ablations) states a 50-node BFS covers only ~1.8% of a citation graph's edges, that subgraph-to-full-graph rank agreement is **τ=0.16 on Cora**, and that the few-edge defense effect collapses from 42–61% (subgraph) to 2.4–4.6% (full graph) because the leading attack mode is *delocalized* (participation ratio 41–89 edges). The single full-graph result (Amazon Photo, N=7650) sits at **κ≈1.00**, i.e. outside the contractive regime the break theory (thm:phase_transition) and prop:transfer require.
- *Where:* sec:audit (protocol); tab:cross_domain, tab:attack_full, tab:baselines, tab:conformal (all 50-node); app:ablations (subgraph τ=0.16, delocalization, 12–46× but far below 42–61%); experiments.tex sec:explicit_extension (Amazon Photo τ=0.996 at κ≈1.00, "empirical regularity, not theory").
- *Why it matters:* If τ between the subgraph audit and the full graph is 0.16, then the 50-node results do not transfer to the object a practitioner audits (the full deployed graph). The defense pillar in particular is reported on subgraphs where k=5 is 10% of edges; at scale the same masking buys 2.4–4.6%. The paper's strongest defensive numbers live in the regime that doesn't generalize, and the one in-the-wild-scale regime violates the theory's contractivity assumption.
- *Concrete fix:* Move full-graph results to the foreground for every claim that is supposed to generalize (audit ranking, defense, certificate), report subgraph numbers only as a microscope. Explicitly tabulate subgraph-vs-full-graph τ for *all* datasets (not just Cora's 0.16). For the κ≈1.00 full-graph result, state clearly which claims (transfer ranking) are empirical-only and which (ε_crit, break theory) are simply inapplicable there.

**M4. Compute / "one query vs 125 epochs" framing is not a matched-compute comparison and is mildly misleading.**
- *What's wrong:* tab:compute reports AEGIS at 0.24 s vs GR-BCD (125 ep) at 0.19 s — i.e. GR-BCD is actually *faster* per run on the subgraph — yet the narrative everywhere is "one query vs 125-epoch optimizer," implying a large compute saving on the attack/ranking axis. The "~700×" headline is dominated entirely by the randomized-smoothing certificate's 10^4 forward passes (169 s), not by the attack/ranking comparison. So the compute story conflates two very different axes: (a) attack/ranking, where AEGIS and GR-BCD are within 1.3× of each other, and (b) certificate, where AEGIS is genuinely orders of magnitude cheaper than smoothing.
- *Where:* tab:compute (framework.tex); app:ablations ("~700× gap ... dominated by the randomized-smoothing certificate's 10^4 forward passes"); app:baselines ("one query reaches what their 125-epoch optimizer converges to").
- *Why it matters:* "One query vs 125 epochs" suggests AEGIS is ~100× cheaper than the iterative attackers; tab:compute shows it is not (0.24 s vs 0.19 s for GR-BCD). The real efficiency win is the certificate (vs smoothing) and the *label-free* property (no labels needed), not raw attack wall-clock. Stating it as a query-count advantage overstates the compute case.
- *Concrete fix:* Separate the two axes explicitly. State plainly: "On the attack/ranking axis AEGIS is compute-comparable to GR-BCD (0.24 s vs 0.19 s) but label-free and certificate-bearing; the ~700×/10^4× gap is specifically the certificate vs randomized smoothing." Stop using "one query vs 125 epochs" as a compute claim; it is a *label-access* and *artifact-completeness* claim.

**M5. The smoothing comparison (tab:smoothing) is not apples-to-apples: AEGIS-Conformal abstains on the matched Frobenius ball and certifies only on a strictly larger per-coordinate ball.**
- *What's wrong:* On the *matched* Frobenius ball (σ = ε/√(2|E|)) AEGIS-Conformal's Cert column is "--" (it abstains / does not certify a fraction), while randomized smoothing also degenerates (Cov 1.00 but Cert 0.00, full label set). AEGIS only reports a meaningful certified fraction implicitly via coverage on the matched ball; the *certified-fraction* comparison (0.77–0.96) is on the **per-edge ball (σ=ε), which the caption admits is "favorable to smoothing"** and strictly larger. The "10^3–10^4× cheaper" headline is then comparing a ~1 s zero-sample bound against 7,600–57,000 s smoothing — but on balls where the certified outcomes differ, so "cheaper" is not on equal certified content.
- *Where:* tab:smoothing (experiments.tex) and app:smoothing; abstract ("non-vacuous on the matched Frobenius ball where smoothing abstains, at 10^4× lower cost").
- *Why it matters:* "X× cheaper" is only meaningful at matched guarantee. On the matched ball *both* methods effectively fail to certify per-node (AEGIS Cert "--", smoothing Cert 0.00); on the larger ball where smoothing does certify, the comparison is on a ball chosen to favor smoothing. The abstract's framing ("non-vacuous where smoothing abstains") is true for *coverage* but the *certified fraction* picture is muddier than the abstract implies.
- *Concrete fix:* Report, on the *same* ball, a matched quantity — e.g., certified fraction at equal radius, or coverage *and* mean set size *and* certified fraction side by side for both methods on both balls — and state the cost ratio only for the configuration where both deliver a comparable guarantee. Soften the abstract to "comparable guarantee at 10^3–10^4× lower cost on the per-edge ball; non-vacuous coverage where smoothing abstains on the matched ball."

**M6. The τ=0.98 headline is largely carried by the edge weight (the paper concedes this) and is validated against an oracle (single-edge-removal) that the score is essentially built to predict; CIs are absent.**
- *What's wrong:* The abstract/intro headline "edge-weighted τ=0.98 ... sensitivity adds +0.16 to +0.90 over the weight" is the median Kendall τ of the *edge-weighted* `A_ij v_ij` ranking vs brute-force single-edge removal (42/42 cells positive, fig:tau_heatmap). The paper itself says "the edge weight carries much of this, since a heavier edge is the larger edit" (sec:explicit_extension). Meanwhile rank agreement vs the *independent* GR-BCD attacker is only τ=0.35–0.81 (mean ≈0.5, tab:baselines). So there are two very different τ's and the abstract leads with the higher (0.98), which is partly tautological: prop:transfer shows `d_k = w_k v_k + O(w_k²)`, i.e. for small-weight edges the edge-weighted score is *designed* to reproduce single-edge-removal order. The genuinely informative number is the increment Δτ (+0.16 median), and even that is reported without confidence intervals (only per-cell sd 0.0003–0.068 for the τ heatmap; no CI on the +0.16/+0.90 increments or on τ=0.98 itself).
- *Where:* abstract; sec:explicit_extension (τ=0.98, "+0.16 to +0.90"); tab:da_decomp (Δτ per dataset); tab:baselines (τ=0.35–0.81 vs GR-BCD); case_study (τ=1.0 on one hand-picked cluster); theory prop:transfer.
- *Why it matters:* The reader is led to believe the *sensitivity* achieves τ=0.98; in fact the *weight* does most of the work, and the sensitivity's marginal contribution is the modest +0.16 median. Leading with 0.98 against the brute-force oracle (which `w_k v_k` is constructed to match) overstates what `v_ij` adds, and burying the τ=0.5 agreement with the truly independent attacker (GR-BCD) hides the less flattering comparison.
- *Concrete fix:* Lead with the *increment* Δτ (with bootstrap CIs over seeds×datasets), not the raw 0.98. Report τ-vs-GR-BCD (the independent oracle) with equal prominence to τ-vs-single-edge-removal. State explicitly in the abstract that the edge weight carries most of the 0.98. Add CIs to every τ and Δτ.

**M7. Statistics: the p<10^{-43} (149/150) and −0.65 (10/10 seeds) claims lack a stated test, multiplicity control, and adequate justification of n; ± is seed-std only.**
- *What's wrong:* (a) "149/150 wins, p<10^{-43}" — the test is not named in the main text (app:baselines later says "paired wins," and a separate degree/betweenness/spectral comparison uses "Wilcoxon p<0.001"). 149/150 looks like a sign/binomial test, which under independence is astronomically significant by construction for n=150 — but the 150 cells are *not independent* (6 datasets × budgets × seeds, heavily correlated across k and seeds), so the effective n and the p-value are both overstated. No multiple-comparison control is reported across the 6×7 (datasets × architectures) grid or the budget sweep. (b) The defense anticorrelation "−0.65 ± 0.12, 10/10 seeds" — a correlation estimated from a *λ-sweep* (5 λ values, tab:defense) is being reported with a seed-std; with so few points per seed the correlation CI is wide, and "10/10 seeds have negative sign" is a weak claim (sign agreement, not magnitude). (c) Throughout, ± is *seed standard deviation only* — not a CI, not accounting for the dataset/architecture sampling that the aggregate claims (τ=0.98, +0.16) average over.
- *Where:* sec:audit / app:baselines (149/150, p<10^{-43}, Wilcoxon p<0.001); sec:defense / abstract (−0.65 ± 0.12, 10/10); app:repro ("± are standard deviations over these seeds").
- *Why it matters:* p<10^{-43} from a sign test over correlated cells is not evidence of a 43-order-of-magnitude effect; it is an artifact of treating correlated wins as independent Bernoulli trials. Reporting it to that precision invites exactly the "sober-look" critique the paper cites (mujkanovic2022defenses). Seed-std ± understates uncertainty for claims that generalize across datasets/architectures.
- *Concrete fix:* Name the test and its independence assumptions; use a paired test at the *dataset* level (n=6) or a hierarchical/cluster-robust test, and report effect sizes with CIs rather than a headline p-value. Apply a multiplicity correction (Holm/BH) across the grid. For −0.65, report the correlation with a bootstrap CI over the (λ, seed) points and drop "10/10 seeds" as the primary evidence. Replace seed-std ± with 95% CIs (bootstrap over seeds; and over datasets for aggregate claims).

### MINOR

**m8. Code release is gated, which impedes independent reproduction of the headline attack.** app:repro / conclusion: only the diagnostic path (`r_v`, `v_ij`) releases unconditionally; the attack-direction reconstruction (alg:aegis steps 3–4) is "gated behind institutional-affiliation review." This is a defensible responsible-disclosure stance, but it means the most-contested claim (the attack) cannot be independently reproduced from the artifact. Recommend at minimum releasing seeds, exact subgraph-construction code, and the evaluation harness for the *damage/flip* metrics even if the reconstruction direction is gated, so the comparison itself is reproducible.

**m9. Subgraph / calibration split construction underspecified.** "50-node BFS ego-subgraphs around the highest-degree node" — ties broken how? One subgraph per graph or per seed? For conformal, the calibration/test split sizes and the exchangeability-imposing sampling (inductive vs transductive) are stated in principle (E_conformal) but the *actual* split used for tab:conformal (n_cal, n_test) is not given. Add these to app:repro.

**m10. "420 runs" provenance.** Intro contribution (4) says "420 runs" = 6 datasets × 7 architectures × 10 seeds. Worth stating explicitly that this is the tau-heatmap grid and not the count behind every table, to avoid overstating coverage.

**m11. Abstract precision.** Several abstract numbers (74–156×, τ=0.98, 10^4× cheaper, −0.65) are stated without the metric/ball/test qualifier that makes them defensible. Even keeping the numbers, append the qualifier inline (e.g., "74–156× the *equilibrium-shift* damage *per query*"; "τ=0.98 vs single-edge removal, *of which the edge weight carries most*").

**m12. WikiCS conformal set sizes (~3.5 labels) vs 10 classes** are barely informative; worth noting which datasets the certificate is actually useful on rather than averaging.

## Questions for authors

1. **Decision relevance.** Can you provide a flip-rate / clean-accuracy-drop-vs-ε curve for AEGIS, Shift-PGD, Cls-PGD, Mettack, and GR-BCD at **matched budget and matched compute**, on the *same* axis, with CIs? If AEGIS is not competitive on flips at small ε, why is "finds the worst attack / matches-beats PGD" the right framing rather than "label-free first-order sensitivity proxy"?
2. **Shift-PGD.** Why is Shift-PGD (the matched-objective attacker, which AEGIS only edges by ~5–20%) labeled "not an independent baseline" while the mismatched-objective Cls-PGD is headlined? What is the AEGIS/Shift-PGD ratio across all 6 datasets and budgets?
3. **Subgraph generalization.** You report subgraph-vs-full-graph τ=0.16 on Cora. What is it on the other five datasets? For which claims (audit, defense, certificate) do full-graph results match the subgraph story, and where do they diverge (the defense effect already drops from 42–61% to 2.4–4.6%)?
4. **κ≈1.00 full graph.** On Amazon Photo (κ≈1.00) the contractivity assumption (A3) fails. Which reported claims survive there empirically, and do ε_crit / the break characterization have *any* meaning in that regime?
5. **Statistics.** What exact test yields p<10^{-43} for 149/150, and how do you account for the strong correlation among the 150 cells (shared seeds, adjacent k, shared datasets)? What is the effect size with a CI at the dataset level (n=6)? Is any multiplicity correction applied across the grid?
6. **−0.65 anticorrelation.** This is a correlation over a 5-point λ sweep. What is its bootstrap CI over (λ, seed)? Is "10/10 seeds negative" anything more than sign agreement over a monotone sweep?
7. **Smoothing matched-ball.** On the *matched* Frobenius ball, what certified fraction does AEGIS-Conformal achieve (the table shows "--")? State the cost ratio only where both methods deliver a comparable guarantee.
8. **Compute framing.** Given tab:compute shows GR-BCD (0.19 s) is faster than AEGIS (0.24 s) on the attack axis, will you stop framing the attack/ranking advantage as "one query vs 125 epochs" and confine the 700×/10^4× claim to the certificate-vs-smoothing axis?
9. **Reproducibility under gating.** Will you release the evaluation harness, seeds, and subgraph-construction code for the damage/flip metrics (even with the reconstruction direction gated) so the comparisons are independently reproducible?
