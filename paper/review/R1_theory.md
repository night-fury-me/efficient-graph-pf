# Reviewer 1 (Theory) — Adversarial Verification of AEGIS Theory

Paper: "AEGIS: Matrix-Free Diagnostics for the Adversarial Fault Lines of Graph Neural Networks" (ICDM 2026).
Scope: theory only (Thm 1, Obs 1, Props 1–4, Remarks). Files verified: `sections/theory.tex`, `sections/background.tex`, `sections/framework.tex`. Algebraic claims spot-checked numerically (rng-seeded) from first principles.

**VERDICT: sound-with-fixes, gated by one CRITICAL soundness gap in the flagship safety boundary (ecrit).**

The unconditional resolvent lower bound, the subcritical first-order bound, the SVD direction, the per-node radius, the K-layer chain rule, and the all-active eta bound are all correct. But the headline contribution — "$\ecrit$ is a *sufficient safety boundary*" — is **not established by the paper's own argument when the empirically-reported $\kappa=\norm{J_z}_2$ is plugged in**, because that argument silently requires $\norm{\Ahat}_2\norm{W}_2\le\kappa$, which holds only in the all-active case. This must be fixed before acceptance.

## CRITICAL-1 — ecrit is NOT a sufficient safety boundary under the reported kappa (Thm 1, proof (a) line 41 + (b)–(c) line 43; def. eq. after A3)

**Location.** `theory.tex` L20 ($\ecrit=(1-\kappa)/\norm{W}_2$), L41 ("$\norm{J_z'}_2 \le (\norm{\Ahat}_2+\varepsilon)\norm{W}_2$, so $\varepsilon<\ecrit$ guarantees $\norm{J_z'}_2<1$"), L43 ("$1-\norm{J_z'}_2 \ge \norm{W}_2(\ecrit-\varepsilon)$ ... certifies $\norm{J_z'}_2<1$"). A3 (L16) fixes $\kappa=\norm{J_z}_2$, reported $0.14$–$0.59$, and this is the $\kappa$ Table 1 uses per-dataset.

**Defect (worked out).** The proof's perturbation bound is $\norm{J_z'}_2\le(\norm{\Ahat}_2+\varepsilon)\norm{W}_2$. For $\varepsilon<\ecrit=(1-\kappa)/\norm{W}_2$ this gives
$$\norm{J_z'}_2 < \norm{\Ahat}_2\norm{W}_2 + (1-\kappa).$$
This RHS is $<1$ **iff** $\norm{\Ahat}_2\norm{W}_2 \le \kappa$. But $\kappa=\norm{J_z}_2=\norm{\mathrm{diag}(\phi')(\Ahat\otimes W)}_2 \le \norm{\Ahat}_2\norm{W}_2$ always (gating $\mathrm{diag}(\phi')\preceq I$ can only shrink the operator norm), with **equality only in the all-active case $\phi'\equiv1$.** Under any genuine partial-ReLU pattern — i.e. the general A1 setting the theorem advertises — one has $\kappa < \norm{\Ahat}_2\norm{W}_2$, so the implication "$\varepsilon<\ecrit\Rightarrow\norm{J_z'}_2<1$" is **unproven by the stated inequality**.

The step at L43, "$1-\norm{J_z'}_2 \ge \norm{W}_2(\ecrit-\varepsilon)$," is algebraically *false* in general: substituting $\ecrit$ gives $\norm{W}_2(\ecrit-\varepsilon)=(1-\kappa)-\varepsilon\norm{W}_2$, whereas $1-\norm{J_z'}_2 \ge 1-(\norm{\Ahat}_2+\varepsilon)\norm{W}_2 = (1-\norm{\Ahat}_2\norm{W}_2)-\varepsilon\norm{W}_2$. These two lower bounds agree only when $1-\kappa = 1-\norm{\Ahat}_2\norm{W}_2$, i.e. $\kappa=\norm{\Ahat}_2\norm{W}_2$ (all-active). When $\kappa<\norm{\Ahat}_2\norm{W}_2$, the claimed inequality overstates the true certified gap, and the genuine norm-sufficiency threshold is the *smaller* $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{suff}} = 1/\norm{W}_2 - \norm{\Ahat}_2 < \ecrit$.

**Numerical confirmation (rng-seeded, first principles).** Symmetric $\Ahat$ with $\norm{\Ahat}_2=0.95$, $\norm{W}_2=0.98$, a random ReLU gating mask: $\norm{\Ahat}_2\norm{W}_2=0.931$, all-active $\kappa=0.931$, partial $\kappa=0.820$. Then $\ecrit$ (partial $\kappa$) $=0.184$ but $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{suff}}=1/\norm{W}_2-\norm{\Ahat}_2=0.070$. At $\varepsilon=0.127\in(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{suff}},\ecrit)$ the proof's own RHS $(\norm{\Ahat}_2+\varepsilon)\norm{W}_2 = 1.056 \not< 1$. So $\ecrit$ **overestimates** the sufficiency threshold by 0.11 and the contraction certificate is void in the gap.

**Why this is CRITICAL not MAJOR.** "$\ecrit$ is a closed-form *sufficient* safety boundary" is one of the paper's three advertised contributions (intro; restated `theory.tex` L33, L46). As proved, it is sufficient only when $\norm{\Ahat}_2\norm{W}_2\le\kappa$, which the symmetric-normalized $\Ahat$ does *not* guarantee — $\norm{\Ahat}_2$ for $D^{-1/2}(A+I)D^{-1/2}$ is typically $\approx1$, so $\norm{\Ahat}_2\norm{W}_2$ is usually close to $\norm{W}_2$ and strictly exceeds the partial-ReLU $\kappa\in[0.14,0.59]$. The paper *never states* the condition $\norm{\Ahat}_2\norm{W}_2\le\kappa$. The empirical $\kappa_{\max}$ sweep "trained IGNNs hold with 2–4$\times$ margin" (L33) does not rescue this: it shows post-hoc that $\norm{J_z'}_2<1$ held, but does not make $\ecrit$ a *certificate* computed from $\kappa$ — it tests the conclusion, not the bound.

**Fix (concrete, rigor-preserving).** Two clean options.
 (i) **Report the honest norm-sufficient threshold** $\ecrit^{\text{suff}} = \max\!\big(0,\, 1/\norm{W}_2 - \norm{\Ahat}_2\big)$, derived directly from $\norm{J_z'}_2\le(\norm{\Ahat}_2+\varepsilon)\norm{W}_2<1$. This is genuinely sufficient for *all* activation patterns and needs no $\kappa$. Keep $(1-\kappa)/\norm{W}_2$ as a *separate, larger* "post-training operating margin" clearly labeled **not** a certified contraction radius. Crucially $\ecrit^{\text{suff}}\le\ecrit$, so this is the defensible quantity.
 (ii) **Add the missing condition explicitly:** state in A3 (or the theorem) that the sufficiency claim requires $\norm{\Ahat}_2\norm{W}_2\le\kappa$ — equivalently the *all-active regime $\phi'\equiv1$ along the perturbation path* — and scope regimes (a)/(c) accordingly. This aligns the safety boundary with the same all-active restriction Obs 1 already lives under, and is internally consistent.
Either fix is small (a few lines) but **load-bearing**: without it a sharp reviewer refutes the flagship claim with a one-line ReLU example.

## CRITICAL/MAJOR-adjacent — A1 advertises "ReLU or any 1-Lipschitz", but the only non-trivial content of Thm 1(b) is proved only for $\phi'\equiv1$ (Thm 1(b) L31/L43; Obs 1 L48–53; Rem L55–57)

**Location.** A1 (L14) "$\phi$ is ReLU or any 1-Lipschitz activation". Regime (b) rate $\Omega(1/(\ecrit-\varepsilon))$ and the $\eta$-slack story (L31, L43) lean on Obs 1, which is **titled "all-active case"** and whose proof (L53) explicitly requires $\phi'\equiv1$. Rem `eta_relu` (L57) concedes the general-ReLU $\eta\in[1.19,2.47]$ is *empirical*.

**Defect.** Regimes (a), (c), and the *unconditional* resolvent lower bound in (b) are correct with no activation restriction (standard Banach/Neumann). But (i) the *rate* $\Omega(1/(\ecrit-\varepsilon))$ and (ii) the claim that the slack is "bounded by $\eta$ (Obs 1); $\eta\le2.47$ for general ReLU" are not theorems for general ReLU — Obs 1 bounds the *diagonalizable resolvent constant* $\eta\le\kappa(V_W)$ only when $J_z=\Ahat\otimes W$ exactly (all-active), and the $[1.19,2.47]$ range is asserted, not derived. The advertised generality therefore exceeds the proved generality, with the gap exiled to a Remark — a recurring pattern in this paper (also Prop 3(b), Prop 4 differentiability).

**Vacuity check.** Not vacuous, but mis-scoped: the theorem as headlined ("for ReLU") promises more than the proof delivers ("for all-active ReLU paths").

**Fix.** Re-title Thm 1 / A1 to the all-active regime for the *rate and eta* parts, and demote the general-ReLU $\eta\in[1.19,2.47]$ to a clearly-labeled empirical observation (evidence already collected, `sec:convergence`). Keep (a), (c), and the unconditional resolvent bound as the genuinely general content. Severity MAJOR (a reviewer will demand the scope label); not double-counted with CRITICAL-1 but compounds it, since both reduce to the same all-active assumption.

## MAJOR-2 — Prop 1 claims max shift $=\varepsilon\sigma_1(S)$ over the unconstrained ball, but the threat model is symmetric + edge-supported, where the feasible max is $\varepsilon\sigma_1(S_c)$ (Prop 1 L62–64; threat model `background.tex` L25)

**Location.** `theory.tex` L64: "$\delta A^*=\varepsilon\,\mathrm{reshape}(v_1,N\times N)$, $v_1$ leading right singular vector of $S$ ... maximum first-order shift is $\varepsilon\cdot\sigma_1(S)$." Threat model (`background.tex` L25) *requires* $\delta\Ahat=\delta\Ahat^\top$ and $[\delta\Ahat]_{ij}=0$ off $E$. Algorithm (`framework.tex` L17) actually uses $\mathrm{sym}(P_c v_1)$, i.e. the operative object is $S_c$.

**Defect.** Over the *feasible* set (symmetric, edge-supported), the first-order maximizer is the leading right singular vector of $S_c=S P_c$, and the maximum shift is $\varepsilon\sigma_1(S_c)$, **not** $\varepsilon\sigma_1(S)$. Generically $\sigma_1(S_c)<\sigma_1(S)$ (verified: $\sigma_1(S)=7.60$ vs $\sigma_1(S_c)=3.62$ on a random seed). Two concrete errors in the *statement*: (i) $\mathrm{reshape}(v_1)$ need not be symmetric and may have off-edge support, so it is *infeasible*; (ii) quoting $\sigma_1(S)$ as "the maximum first-order shift" overstates the achievable shift within the threat model. The fix used in the algorithm, $\mathrm{sym}(P_c v_1)$, is feasible but is **only an approximation** to the constrained maximizer: verified $\norm{S\cdot\mathrm{sym/proj}(v_1)}=3.46 < \sigma_1(S_c)=3.62$. So neither the stated direction nor the stated magnitude is the true constrained optimum.

**Fix.** Restate Prop 1 over the symmetric edge subspace: the optimal feasible direction is $v_1^c$ = leading right singular vector of $S_c$, realized as the edge-symmetric matrix $\sum_k (v_1^c)_k b_k$ (which is automatically symmetric and edge-supported), with maximum first-order shift $\varepsilon\sigma_1(S_c)$. This is exact and matches Algorithm 1 if line 17 is changed to take $v_1$ from $\mathrm{rSVD}(S_c)$ directly mapped through the $\{b_k\}$ basis (the heatmap/rSVD already operates on $S_c$, so this is essentially a statement+one-line code alignment). Note $\mathrm{sym}(P_c v_1)$ is then unnecessary — if $v_1$ is the right-singular vector *of $S_c$* in the $b_k$ basis, its image $\sum_k (v_1)_k b_k$ is already symmetric. Severity MAJOR: the proposition as written is provably non-optimal for its own threat model and a reviewer will catch the $\sigma_1(S)$ vs $\sigma_1(S_c)$ mismatch immediately.

## MAJOR-3 — Prop 3(b) "ranking preservation" is a sufficient *pairwise* condition (holds 47–62% of pairs), not the global $\tau$ statistic the paper headlines (Prop 3 L106–111; proof L116; headline `theory.tex` L119)

**Location.** Prop 3(b) (L106–111) gives a *pairwise* implication: $d_{k_1}>d_{k_2}$ *whenever* $w_{k_1}<(v_{k_1}-v_{k_2})/(L_J/(1-\kappa)^2)$. Headline (L119): "$w_kv_k$ ... reaches $\tau=+0.996$ against brute-force N-1." Authors' own number: the sufficient condition holds for only 47–62% of edge pairs.

**Defect.** Prop 3(b) proves (i) magnitude $d_k=w_kv_k+R_k$ (part a) and (ii) a *per-pair* order guarantee under a smallness condition on $w_{k_1}$. It does **not** prove a global Kendall-$\tau$ rank statistic, and the condition empirically fails on ~40–50% of pairs. The $\tau=+0.996$ result therefore *exceeds* the proposition and is **empirical**, not implied. The theorem still does real work — part (a) is the magnitude bridge that *justifies using $w_kv_k$ as the score at all* — but (b) is being rhetorically inflated into a rank-transfer theorem it is not.

**Fix.** (i) State explicitly that (b) is a *sufficient pairwise* condition and that the global $\tau$ is empirical corroboration of (a), not a corollary of (b). (ii) Either weaken the headline to "consistent with the first-order bridge (a)" or add a genuine rank-statistic bound (e.g. bound the expected number of discordant pairs by the fraction of pairs violating the condition, $\#\text{discordant}\le|\{(k_1,k_2): w_{k_1}\ge(v_{k_1}-v_{k_2})(1-\kappa)^2/L_J\}|$, giving $\tau\ge1-2\,p_{\text{viol}}$). The latter would convert the 47–62% into an *actual* $\tau$ lower bound and make (b) load-bearing. Severity MAJOR.

**Sub-issue (MINOR within 3a) — $L_J$ scale + $z^*$ denominator.** The curvature constant $L_J\le\norm{W}_2^2\norm{\zstar}$ (L104) is the *correct* form: the dominant curvature term carries $J_A\propto\zstar W^\top$, i.e. one factor of $\norm{\zstar}$ (verified: $d^2\zstar/dA^2$ scales exactly linearly in $\norm{\zstar}$). Good — this is *not* the bare $\norm{W}^2$. The remainder $|R_k|\le L_Jw_k^2/(2(1-\kappa)^2)$ is internally consistent ($\zstar$ lives inside $L_J$, not double-charged). **But** the finiteness argument $\norm{\zstar}\le\norm{X_{\mathrm{proj}}}/(1-\norm{\Ahat}_2\norm{W}_2)$ (L115) uses denominator $1-\norm{\Ahat}_2\norm{W}_2$, which can be $\le0$ while the model is still contractive ($\kappa=\norm{J_z}_2<\norm{\Ahat}_2\norm{W}_2$ under partial ReLU) — the *same* norm-vs-actual-contraction conflation as CRITICAL-1. Then the $\zstar$ bound is vacuous/negative and $L_J$ is not certified finite. **Fix:** bound $\norm{\zstar}$ via the contraction constant directly: at the fixed point $\zstar=\phi(\Ahat\zstar W^\top+X_{\mathrm{proj}})$, $\phi$ 1-Lipschitz with $\phi(0)=0$, the map has Lipschitz constant $\kappa<1$ in $\mathrm{vec}(z)$, so $\norm{\zstar}\le\norm{X_{\mathrm{proj}}}/(1-\kappa)$, giving $|R_k|\le \norm{W}_2^2\norm{X_{\mathrm{proj}}}\,w_k^2/(2(1-\kappa)^3)$ — a bounded constant valid under A3 alone. This is the clean rederivation; it removes every reliance on $1-\norm{\Ahat}\norm{W}>0$.

## Correct results (verified, no fix required)

**Thm 1(b) unconditional resolvent bound + diag(−s,0) (L31, L43).** $(I-M)^{-1}$ has eigenvalues $(1-\lambda_i(M))^{-1}$, so $\norm{(I-M)^{-1}}_2\ge\rho=1/\min_i|1-\lambda_i(M)|$ holds **unconditionally** ($\rho\le\norm{\cdot}_2$). Verified: 0 violations / 20000 random $M$. The one-sided claim is correct: $\norm{M}_2\to1$ need not drive $\min_i|1-\lambda_i|\to0$. The $M=\mathrm{diag}(-s,0)$ object is a *valid* counterexample to the converse — verified $\norm{M}_2=s\to1$ but resolvent $\equiv1$. Two subtle points the authors get right: (i) $\mathrm{diag}(-s,0)$ is *normal*, so "normal" alone is **not** sufficient for the rate; the text correctly restricts to "normal with **dominant real-positive eigenvalue** (Perron mode of symmetric $\Ahat,W$)" — this qualifier is load-bearing and the same $\mathrm{diag}(-s,0)$ (real-negative dominant) that kills the converse also shows why the qualifier is needed. (ii) Under that hypothesis $\min_i|1-\lambda_i|=1-\lambda^\star=\norm{W}_2(\ecrit-\varepsilon)$ exactly — verified resolvent $=1/(1-\lambda^\star)$ to machine precision. **However**, note this rate derivation *also* substitutes $\norm{W}_2(\ecrit-\varepsilon)$ for $1-\norm{J_z'}_2$, inheriting the CRITICAL-1 all-active dependence: the equality $\lambda^\star=\norm{J_z'}_2$ and $1-\norm{J_z'}_2=\norm{W}_2(\ecrit-\varepsilon)$ both need $\kappa=\norm{\Ahat}_2\norm{W}_2$. So the rate is correct *in the Perron + all-active regime* — fold this into the CRITICAL-1 / scoping fix.

**Obs 1 eta bound (L48–53).** For $\phi'\equiv1$, $J_z=\Ahat\otimes W$, Kronecker eigenvalues $\lambda_i(\Ahat)\lambda_j(W)$ correct; symmetric $\Ahat\Rightarrow\kappa(U_{\Ahat})=1$, joint eigenvector condition number $=\kappa(V_W)$, diagonalizable bound $\norm{(I-M)^{-1}}_2\le\kappa(V)/(1-\rho)$ gives $\eta\le\kappa(V_W)$, $\Ahat$-independent. Verified: 0 violations / 2000. **Caveat:** the identity is only meaningful if $\eta$ is *defined* as the diagonalizable resolvent constant $\norm{(I-J_z)^{-1}}_2(1-\rho)$ (it is, `background.tex` L18) — then $\eta\le\kappa(V_W)$ is a tight definition-fit, fine. The "graph-independent" claim is justified *for $\phi'\equiv1$*. The Rem `eta_relu` extension to general ReLU is **pure assertion** (no theoretical backing); acceptable only as a labeled empirical observation (see MAJOR-adjacent).

**Prop 2 per-node radius (L69–79).** Sound. $|\Delta(f_{y_v}-f_c)|\le\norm{(W_{y_v}-W_c)S_v}_2\norm{\delta A}_F$ by Cauchy–Schwarz (row vector $(W_{y_v}-W_c)$ times matrix $S_v$; row-2-norm = induced matrix 2-norm of the resulting row, correct). $\min$ over *all* competitors is the genuine distance-to-nearest-first-order-boundary and is robust to runner-up change — correct robust form. Rem `certificates` (L81) correctly placed *before* downstream $r_v$ claims and honestly calls $r_v$ a first-order threshold, not a probabilistic certificate. Only nit (MINOR): elsewhere (tables) a first-order threshold is described as "certified" — terminology drift.

**Prop 4 K-layer (L123–138).** Chain-rule unrolling $S_K=\sum_l(\prod_{k>l}J_z^{(k)})J_A^{(l)}$ correct; submultiplicative $\sigma_1(S_K)$ bound correct; weight-tied geometric series $\sigma_1(S_K)\le\norm{J_A}_2(1-\kappa^K)/(1-\kappa)\to\norm{J_A}_2/(1-\kappa)$ arithmetic correct. (b) inheriting $S_c$, $r_v$ is fine. **Scope nit (MINOR):** "each layer $g_l$ differentiable w.r.t. input and $A$" silently excludes hard-attention / binary-mask GAT / max-pool (non-differentiable aggregation); conceded only via GAT$^\dagger$ in experiments. Label the differentiable-aggregation restriction in the proposition.

## MINOR issues
- **`background.tex` L18 / `theory.tex` L46:** $\eta$ defined two ways — as $\norm{(I-J_z)^{-1}}_2(1-\rho)$ (background) vs "the gap is the pseudospectral index $\eta$, bounded by $W$ alone" (theory, only true all-active). Reconcile.
- **Thm 1(a) L29:** $\sigma_1(S)\le\norm{J_A}_{\mathrm{op}}/(1-\kappa)$ uses the $\norm{\cdot}_2\le\norm{\cdot}_F$/Neumann chain; correct but loose. The $\norm{\delta\Ahat}_F=\varepsilon$ vs $\sigma_1$ (operator) consistency relies on the orthonormal $b_k$ basis — stated for $S_c$ (L67) but not re-noted for $S$ in (a). Minor consistency note.
- **`framework.tex` L25 / Prop 1:** "$\mathrm{sym}(P_c v_1)$ keeps $\delta\Ahat^\star$ symmetric" — with the $b_k$ basis $P_c v_1$ is *already* symmetric, so $\mathrm{sym}(\cdot)$ is a no-op or signals $v_1$ is taken from $S$ not $S_c$; clarify (ties to MAJOR-2).
- **Prop 3(a) L115:** "recompute-normalization adds $O(d_i^{-1})$ incident-edge rescaling, negligible off low-degree nodes" — honest caveat, but at low degree the incident-edge sum can be $O(1)$; correctly flagged as a modeling choice, not a remainder, so it does not break the bridge. Keep the caveat visible.
- **Notation:** $\kappa$ is used for *both* contractivity $\norm{J_z}_2$ (A3) and matrix condition number $\kappa(V_W)$, $\kappa(U_{\Ahat})$ (Obs 1). Same symbol, two meanings, same theorem block. Disambiguate (e.g. $\mathrm{cond}(\cdot)$).

## Does the theory support the paper's contribution?
Partially. The **three diagnostics** (per-edge $S_c$ rankings, SVD-optimal direction, per-node radii) rest on Props 1–2 + $S_c$ construction, which are correct *once Prop 1 is restated over the symmetric edge subspace* (MAJOR-2) and the ranking claim is scoped to pairwise-sufficient (MAJOR-3). The **safety-boundary** contribution ($\ecrit$) is, as written, **not sound** under the reported partial-ReLU $\kappa$ (CRITICAL-1): $\ecrit$ overestimates the proof's own sufficiency threshold unless $\norm{\Ahat}_2\norm{W}_2\le\kappa$, a condition the paper never states. With the honest-threshold fix (report $\max(0,1/\norm{W}_2-\norm{\Ahat}_2)$ as the certified radius, or restrict the sufficiency claim to all-active), the theory becomes defensible. The recurring structural risk — *advertised generality exceeds proved generality, with the gap exiled to a Remark* — appears three times (ReLU vs $\phi'\equiv1$; global $\tau$ vs pairwise; K-layer vs differentiable-aggregation) and is the single highest-leverage thing for the authors to fix systematically.
