# Editor-in-Chief Review (R2)

## Persona
Senior NeurIPS/ICLR Area Chair for adversarial robustness and graph learning; reading this submission cold against the venue's bar for novelty, technical depth, abstract-versus-body scope alignment, and ethics posture. I do not audit proofs or power-systems numerics in this report.

## Summary of the paper (≤200 words)
The paper proposes AEGIS, a closed-form structural-vulnerability analyzer for GNNs centred on a single object: the constrained sensitivity matrix $S_c \in \mathbb{R}^{Nd\times|E|}$, obtained from the resolvent $(I-J_z)^{-1}J_A$ restricted to the symmetric, edge-only perturbation space. From one matrix-free pass (Neumann series + randomised SVD) the framework simultaneously yields (i) the top-singular direction as a globally maximally sensitive perturbation, (ii) per-edge $L_2$ column scores as a vulnerability ranking, and (iii) per-node first-order radii $r_v$ as sensitivity thresholds. For a contractive-IGNN subclass with spectral-norm cap on $W$ and post-training contractivity $\kappa<1$, the authors prove a worst-case three-regime characterisation around $\varepsilon_{\rm crit}=(1-\kappa)/\|W\|_2$. The empirical study spans 9 datasets and 7 architectures (GCN/SAGE/GIN/APPNP/IGNN/edge-weighted GAT$^\dagger$), reports 29/33 architecture--dataset cells with positive sign-transfer of $S_c$ rankings, and includes a $\kappa_{\max}\in[0.30,0.99]$ sweep on Cora plus an IEEE case14--case118 power-flow case study aligned with N-1 contingency rankings ($\tau{=}{+}0.37$ to ${+}0.62$, $\mathrm{P@10}{=}0.66$--$0.81$).

## Significance and venue fit
The paper is a credible fit for an ICDM/NeurIPS adversarial-robustness audience and a borderline-interesting submission for the certification community. The substantive editorial claim is *unification*: per-edge ranks, the SVD attack direction, and per-node radii fall out of the same closed-form object computed in one matrix-free pass. The positioning matrix (Fig.~1 in §Related Work) makes this concrete against Nettack, Mettack, GR-BCD, localised smoothing, and AGNNCert, and the new AGNNCert head-to-head (Tab.~III, $5$--$10\times$ tighter radii at weak Kendall $0.08$--$0.14$) honestly frames AEGIS as the first-order, ranking-tight complement to IBP certifiers rather than a replacement. That is a sensible niche.

An AC metareview would almost certainly read: *"Mid-tier acceptance candidate. The unified-object framing is genuinely useful; the theory is reformulated honestly as a safety boundary rather than an empirical claim; the empirical study is broad if not deep on any single axis. The headline phase-transition contribution is materially weaker than the title suggests after rescoping; the paper's editorial weight rests almost entirely on contribution (1) ($S_c$ and the matrix-free pipeline) and the case study. Acceptable, not exciting."* For NeurIPS that places it on the borderline; for ICDM it is comfortably above bar.

## Originality assessment
The "vulnerability spectrum from one closed-form object" framing is *partially* new. The individual ingredients (implicit-function-theorem sensitivity, resolvent of $J_z$, randomised SVD for the dominant left/right pair, Neumann-series matrix-free products) are textbook; the contribution is the constrained-edge subspace projection that makes the same $S_c$ simultaneously deliver three outputs that the prior literature has split across attack methods, robustness certificates, and influence functions. The §Related Work positioning is correct that no single 2024--2025 method produces all three. I would still mark this as *incremental rather than landmark* originality: it is a well-engineered consolidation, not a new analytical primitive. Against Geisler et al.\ (GR-BCD, 2021), Schuchardt et al.\ (localised smoothing, 2023), and Li et al.\ (AGNNCert, 2025), the closed-form direction is the clearest novelty; the per-edge ranking and per-node radius are reparameterisations of objects the field already computes. The IGNN-specific phase-transition is, in the rescoped form, a corollary of standard Banach contraction analysis on the resolvent rather than a new theorem.

## Abstract↔body scope alignment
The retitle from "Structural Sensitivity..." to "Closed-Form Adversarial Diagnostics over the GNN Vulnerability Spectrum" is the right move and the abstract earns *most* of the new title. Specifically: the abstract now separates "the analytical construction applies to any GNN with continuous edge-weight-modulated message passing... predictive transfer is architecture-dependent" from "for the spectral-norm-constrained IGNN subclass we additionally derive $\varepsilon_{\rm crit}$ and a worst-case three-regime characterisation." That clean partition (one $S_c$ construction, formal guarantees only on the IGNN subclass, empirical transfer for everything else) matches the body (intro contributions block, §3 Theorem assumptions A1--A3, §4.4 explicit extension, Tab.~II).

Where the alignment still strains: the title noun "Diagnostics... over the GNN Vulnerability Spectrum" implies a coverage claim that the empirical evidence does not quite ratify. *Diagnostics* is fine for ranking and direction; *radii* are framed honestly as first-order thresholds rather than certificates. The word *spectrum*, however, carries a connotation of behaviour across the budget range, and the body now explicitly disclaims that ε > ε_crit is not empirically demonstrated; the spectrum is therefore explored on the $\kappa$ axis, not the $\varepsilon$ axis. The title is defensible but the connotation is slightly stretched.

The GAT$^\dagger$ footnote is a minor scope leak: the abstract lists GAT$^\dagger$ alongside GCN/SAGE/GIN/APPNP/IGNN in a way that a casual reader will mistake for "standard GAT". The body discloses (§4.4 and Limitations (ii)) that standard GAT is out of scope; the abstract should not.

## Significance of the rescoped phase-transition claim
After R2, Theorem 3.1(c) is defensive ("the certificate fails; we do not claim divergence as a generic post-threshold behaviour"); the empirical anchor is the $\kappa_{\max}\in[0.30,0.99]$ sweep that pushes $\varepsilon_{\rm crit}$ down $230\times$ and observes the resolvent grow only $1.17{\to}1.80$, well short of the worst-case $\sim 70\times$. The body now states that the phase transition is "a theoretical safety boundary, not an empirically observable discontinuity" (§5, Phase-transition paragraph).

Editorially this is *defensible but materially deflating*. The honest reading is: contribution (2) has been demoted from "we exhibit a phase transition" to "we provide a sufficient certificate that never empirically fires, and observe the bound is loose because ReLU pattern stability prevents Jacobian saturation." That is a correct, sober R2 conclusion, and the team deserves credit for not painting over it. But it converts the headline theoretical result into closer to a footnote: the resolvent simply does not blow up on trained models, and the body now says so. The paper's editorial weight therefore consolidates on contribution (1) ($S_c$ + matrix-free pipeline + three diagnostics from one pass) and on the case study. Both can carry an ICDM-grade acceptance; for a NeurIPS bar the deflation matters more.

## Ethics posture
Adequate, not performative. The Conclusion's "Ethical considerations and dual-use risks" paragraph is one of the cleaner ML-paper ethics statements I have read recently: it (i) acknowledges the power-grid attack-tool concern explicitly, (ii) commits to a 90-day pre-release notification to PandaPower/Grid2Op maintainers and NERC/ENTSO-E working groups, (iii) tiers the release so attack-generation code is gated by institutional-affiliation review while analysis tools are openly available, and (iv) *correctly self-deprecates* by noting that tiered access is not a strong technical control after publication and is therefore primarily a coordination mechanism. That last clause is the marker of a serious ethics treatment; most papers either over-claim the protection or omit the limitation. I would prefer one additional sentence on what triggers re-evaluation of the tier (e.g., post-disclosure findings from grid stakeholders), but the current text is sufficient.

## Strengths (top 3)
1. **Genuine unification of three previously-siloed outputs from one closed-form object.** The $S_c$ construction (§4) and matrix-free routing (Algorithm 1) make the unification concrete, not rhetorical. Per-edge column-norm rank vs.\ brute-force finite-difference $\tau{=}0.999$ on every dataset is a strong sanity check (§5).
2. **Sober R2 rescoping.** Theorem 3.1(c) is now defensive; the abstract separates "analytical construction (broad)" from "formal guarantees (IGNN-only)"; the case-study Tab.~IV separates the credible operating range (case14--case118) from the un-converged case300 stress test; the AGNNCert head-to-head is framed as complementary rather than superseding. These are mature editorial choices.
3. **Operational ethics posture.** Tiered release, 90-day stakeholder notification to named bodies, and explicit acknowledgment that tiering is coordinative not technical.

## Weaknesses (top 5, ordered by editorial weight)
1. **The headline theoretical contribution has been reduced to a non-firing certificate.** MAJOR. The $\kappa_{\max}$ sweep (`paper/sections/experiments.tex` §Phase-transition paragraph; Fig.~3) shows resolvent growth $1.17{\to}1.80$ where the worst-case bound predicts $\sim 70\times$, and the body now states the phase transition is "not an empirically observable discontinuity." This is correct, but the introduction still lists "(2) Phase-transition theory" as a primary bolded contribution. Fix: either demote (2) to a sub-bullet under (1) framed as "a safety-boundary certificate for the IGNN subclass," or add a single sentence in §1 (Contributions) and §5 (just before Fig.~3) explicitly stating that the phase transition is a sufficient certificate that does not fire in practice on trained IGNNs and is intended as a deployment-time safety margin, not as predictive of behaviour at the threshold. Without that disclosure in the contributions block, a reader who skims will overweight (2).

2. **Title connotation versus evidence on the "Spectrum" axis.** MAJOR. The title promises coverage *over the vulnerability spectrum*; the body explores the $\kappa$ axis (sweep), the architecture axis (Tab.~II), and the dataset axis (Tab.~I), but reports breach rates only at subcritical $\varepsilon$. The credible reading of "spectrum" in the abstract is *range of structural perturbations*; the evidence-based reading is *range of contractivity*. Fix: in §1 paragraph 3, add one sentence clarifying that "spectrum" refers to the three classes of diagnostic output (direction / edge rank / node radius) and to the $\kappa$ axis, not to a budget sweep across $\varepsilon_{\rm crit}$.

3. **Standard GAT silently elided from abstract.** MINOR. The abstract lists "GCN, SAGE, GIN, APPNP, IGNN, edge-weighted GAT$^\dagger$." A casual reader will not register that GAT$^\dagger$ is non-standard. Fix: add four words to the abstract: "...edge-weighted GAT variant (standard GAT requires continuous edge weights and is out of scope)."

4. **Originality of contribution (1) is real but not yet positioned against the strongest baselines.** MINOR-to-MAJOR depending on AC. The §Related Work positioning matrix (Fig.~2) covers Nettack, Mettack, GR-BCD, localised smoothing, AGNNCert. Missing is an explicit *one-paragraph* positioning against IFT-based influence functions for GNNs (Koh--Liang lineage, which the §Background acknowledges has been used for hyperparameter optimisation but, per the authors, "not for adversarial structural analysis"). Given that this claim is the load-bearing originality argument, the related-work section should defend it head-on rather than relegate it to a background sentence. Fix: add a "Influence functions vs.\ structural sensitivity" sub-paragraph in §Related Work that names two or three recent influence-function-for-GNN papers (e.g., 2023--2025) and explains why their edge-level output does not deliver the closed-form SVD direction.

5. **Case-study scope qualifier is honest but the abstract still sells the upper end.** MINOR. The abstract reports "$\tau{=}{+}0.37$ to ${+}0.62$, P@10 ${=}0.66$--$0.81$" without "proof-of-concept" qualification appearing in the same sentence. The body (Tab.~IV caption, Limitations (v)) does qualify. Fix: insert "(proof-of-concept; operator-grade deployment requires admittance-weighted edges)" in the abstract sentence, mirroring the conclusion wording. (Actually present in the abstract — "(proof-of-concept, not operator-grade)" — but it sits before the numbers; consider moving it after to bind the qualifier to the metrics in readers' parsing.)

## Recommendation
**Minor Revision**
**Confidence:** 4
**Brief rationale (≤100 words):** The paper has earned its rescoped title. The unification claim is real and well-executed; the matrix-free pipeline and the diagnostic triple from one $S_c$ are a credible contribution. The R2 deflation of the phase-transition theorem is honest but leaves the contributions block over-selling (2); a one-paragraph demotion fixes that. The abstract↔body alignment is mostly clean with two small scope leaks (standard-GAT elision; case-study qualifier placement). The case study is appropriately framed as proof-of-concept after the case300 disclosure. Ethics is sufficient. Net editorial judgment: acceptable at ICDM with the five fixes; borderline at NeurIPS.

## Open questions the author must answer
1. After the $\kappa_{\max}$ sweep result, what is your *predictive* claim for the phase transition? If the resolvent never blows up on trained IGNNs in practice, what does Theorem 3.1 *forecast* that a practitioner should design around — and why is that forecast worth a top-level contribution rather than a remark?
2. The abstract's "29/33 architecture--dataset cells positive, sign-test $p<10^{-5}$" is reassuring, but the four anti-correlated cells are concentrated on shallow GCN-2 on sparse citation graphs (per the abstract). Is this a deployment caveat (do not use $S_c$ on shallow GCN-2 / sparse-citation regimes), and if so, why is it not stated in §1 as a scope condition rather than in the abstract as a fraction?
3. In the AGNNCert head-to-head (Tab.~III, $r_v$ comparison), AEGIS radii are $5$--$10\times$ tighter but rank-correlation with AGNNCert is weak ($\tau\in[0.08,0.14]$). Your framing is "complementary, not competing." For a practitioner, what is the decision rule for *which* to trust on a given node? Without that rule the complementarity claim is rhetorical.
4. The threat model excludes edge insertion. Given that two of the three motivating domains in §1 (fraud, drug-interaction) are inherently insertion-dominated, why is the edge-only restriction not flagged in the abstract scope clause rather than disclosed in Limitations (vi)?
5. Standard GAT is excluded because of continuous-differentiability requirements; the explicit-extension §4.4 handles GAT via the edge-weighted variant. Is the $S_c$ framework recoverable for standard GAT via a soft-attention relaxation, and what is the empirical cost of that relaxation? If the answer is "out of scope for this paper," say so in §4.4 rather than in the Limitations paragraph.
