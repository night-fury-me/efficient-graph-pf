# Peer Review Report — Domain

## Identity & Focus

**Reviewer:** Peer Reviewer 2 — Domain Expert (adversarial graph machine learning).
**Expertise:** structural attacks (Nettack, Mettack, PR-BCD/GR-BCD), certified robustness (randomized smoothing, convex relaxations, IBP/AGNNCert), robust-architecture defenses (GNNGuard, Pro-GNN, RGCN), and "sober-look" evaluation critiques (Mujkanović et al., Gosch et al.).
**Mandate:** literature coverage & positioning, baseline fairness, honesty of the AGNNCert complementarity framing, net contribution to the graph-adversarial field, and missing/mis-cited references. Independent review; no reference to other reviewers.

## Recommendation + Confidence

**Recommendation: Minor Revision** (weighted score 76/100, just below the Accept line).
**Confidence: High (4/5).** I am very familiar with the attack/certification literature this paper positions against; I am less expert on the implicit-network (IFT/DEQ) machinery and the power-flow case study, so I defer numerical claims about the resolvent construction to the methodology reviewer.

The core unification idea is sound, the literature coverage is unusually thorough for a 10-page paper, and the authors have been commendably honest about the first-order-vs-certificate distinction. What holds it back from a clean Accept is that the *single most quotable result*—the one-query direction "dominating" a 50-step PGD—is partly true-by-construction and is not stress-tested against the strongest *independent* attacks in their *intended* budget regime. That is a fixable framing/evaluation issue, not a fatal flaw.

## Summary Assessment

AEGIS reads a single analytical object, the constrained sensitivity matrix \(S_c\) (the edge-projected unrolled-Jacobian resolvent of a spectral-norm-constrained implicit GNN), and extracts three diagnostics from it: (i) a leading-singular-vector perturbation direction (\cref{prop:attack}), (ii) an edge-sensitivity ranking from column norms of \(S_c\), and (iii) a per-node first-order radius \(r_v\) (\cref{prop:radius}). The positioning argument (\cref{sec:related}, \cref{sec:intro}) is that three existing threads—structural attacks, certified robustness/defenses, and implicit-network/influence work—each supply *at most one* of these three, so the contribution is "all three from one object, one query."

The taxonomy is mostly fair and the literature net is wide (91 references, including the sober-look critiques `mujkanovic2022defenses`/`gosch2023adversarial`, the BCD family `geisler2021robustness`, smoothing `bojchevski2020efficient`/`schuchardt2023localized`/`schuchardt2021collective`, IBP `li2025agnncert`, and the robust-architecture trio `zhu2019robust`/`jin2020graph`/`zhang2020gnnguard`). The empirical honesty is a genuine strength: the paper explicitly labels Shift-PGD "solver validation, not an independent baseline" (\cref{tab:attack_full} caption), concedes \(r_v\) is "a first-order threshold, not a sound certificate" (\cref{rem:certificates}), reports the certifier tightness ratio \(r_\text{cert}/r_v\in[4.4,15.0]\) and near-zero Kendall \(\tau\), and concedes AEGIS is "competitive rather than dominant under direct attack damage" against GR-BCD (\cref{sec:prior_comparison}, l.114).

My three substantive concerns: (1) the headline "dominates a 50-step PGD" is, in the small-\(\varepsilon\) equilibrium-shift regime, optimal *by construction*—both the PGD and the SVD direction maximize the same first-order objective using the same IFT gradients, so it is closer to a sanity check than a competitive win; (2) GR-BCD/PR-BCD are evaluated at small budgets (\(k=5\)–\(10\)) where they decorrelate from \(S_c\) (\(\tau=+0.16\) on Cora), i.e. *outside* their intended large-budget regime, which flatters the "competitive" claim while quietly conceding the regime where they would dominate; (3) the AGNNCert "complementary, not interchangeable" decision rule is honest but borders on a hedge, since the near-zero \(\tau\) shows \(r_v\) and a sound certificate measure genuinely different objects and the paper never demonstrates the decision rule adds operational value.

## Strengths

- **Unusually complete literature net for the page budget.** The bibliography covers the field's load-bearing works: poisoning/evasion attacks (`zugner2018adversarial`, `zugner2019adversarial`, `dai2018adversarial`, `xu2019topology`, `wu2019adversarial`, `bojchevski2019adversarial`, `ma2020towards`, `chang2020restricted`), scalable BCD (`geisler2021robustness`), robust aggregation (`geisler2020reliable`), the full smoothing line (`bojchevski2020efficient`, `bojchevski2019certifiable`, `schuchardt2023localized`, `schuchardt2021collective`, `scholten2022randomized`, `scholten2023hierarchical`, `wang2021certified`, `jia2020certified`), convex/IBP certification (`zugner2019certifiable`, `li2025agnncert`), robust architectures (`zhu2019robust`, `jin2020graph`, `zhang2020gnnguard`, `luo2021learning`, `entezari2020all`, `tang2020transferring`), explainers (`ying2019gnnexplainer`, `luo2020parameterized`, `pope2019explainability`, `li2025xgnncert`), and crucially the sober-look critiques (`mujkanovic2022defenses`, `gosch2023adversarial`). It is rare to see a unification paper cite the work that would be used to attack it.
- **Empirical honesty that pre-empts reviewer objections.** Three concessions stand out: (a) Shift-PGD is explicitly demoted to "solver validation, not an independent baseline" (\cref{tab:attack_full}); (b) \(r_v\) is explicitly "a first-order sensitivity threshold, not a probabilistic certificate" with the AGNNCert semantics-differ caveat repeated in \cref{rem:certificates}, the \cref{tab:baselines} footnote, and \cref{sec:prior_comparison}; (c) the GR-BCD decorrelation on Cora and the "competitive rather than dominant" admission (l.114). This is the discipline the sober-look papers ask for.
- **Adaptive-attack discipline in the defense ablation.** \cref{sec:defense_ablation} (l.140) reports an *adaptive* attacker that recomputes \(S_c\) on the masked graph and matches the non-adaptive attacker within 0.1 pp, with paired Wilcoxon vs. random masking. Addressing the "defenses evaluated only against static attacks" failure mode (`mujkanovic2022defenses`) head-on is exactly right and is the single most credible defense result in the paper.
- **A genuinely decisive independence test exists.** The transfer-surrogate result in \cref{tab:attack_full} (separately trained IGNN, zero shared gradients, recovers 99% of damage at \(\cos=0.99\); 512-query black-box recovers only \(44\pm4\%\)) is the strongest evidence that the direction is model-intrinsic rather than a circular gradient artifact. This rebuts the obvious "you are just measuring your own gradient" objection.
- **The continuous-to-discrete transfer is well validated at scale.** \(\tau=+0.996\) on Amazon Photo against brute-force N-1 removal (\cref{fig:tau_heatmap}, l.172), with 29/33 positive cells over 330 runs, is a strong, falsifiable claim that the edge ranking is real and not an artifact of the 50-node subgraph.

## Weaknesses

### W1. The headline "one-query direction dominates 50-step PGD" is partly true-by-construction.
- **Problem.** \cref{tab:attack_full} and \cref{sec:adaptive} (l.43) present the SVD direction "dominating" a 50-step equilibrium-shift PGD that "uses AEGIS's own IFT gradients." But \cref{prop:attack} states the SVD direction is *the* first-order maximizer of \(\|\Delta z^\star\|\). A PGD on the same first-order objective with the same gradients cannot exceed it at small \(\varepsilon\); that Shift-PGD recovers only 72–92% reflects PGD step-size/projection slack, not a genuine adversary losing. The paper's own caption concedes this ("solver validation, not an independent baseline"), yet the abstract/intro framing ("matches/dominates iterative attackers") leans on it as a competitive result.
- **Why it matters.** To this community, "our one-query direction beats a 50-step PGD" is the kind of claim that draws immediate skepticism precisely because a leading singular vector is *optimal by construction* in the first-order regime. The classification-loss PGD (Cls-PGD) is the only genuinely independent gradient adversary in the table, and AEGIS beats it "by 15–70%" *only* in the small-\(\varepsilon\), first-order regime where the singular-vector direction is provably optimal—a regime that structurally favors \(S_c\). Outside it (the larger-\(\varepsilon\) regime where the envelope ratio loosens to 1.36–1.39, \cref{tab:tightness_eps}), the guarantee weakens and no comparison is shown.
- **Suggestion.** Reframe \cref{tab:attack_full} explicitly as "first-order optimality verification" rather than a head-to-head win. Move the *independent* comparisons (Cls-PGD, transfer-surrogate, 512-query black-box) to the foreground as the real evidence, and demote Shift-PGD to a single confirmatory line. State plainly in the abstract that the "match/dominate" claim is scoped to the small-\(\varepsilon\) first-order regime.
- **Severity: Major.**

### W2. GR-BCD/PR-BCD are evaluated outside their intended budget regime.
- **Problem.** \cref{tab:baselines} compares against GR-BCD at "Pubmed \(k{=}10\)" and "Cora \(k{=}5\)". The BCD family of `geisler2021robustness` is designed for *large* perturbation budgets and *large* graphs (its whole contribution is scaling to budgets where Mettack/Nettack are intractable). Evaluating it at \(k=5\)–\(10\) edges is its weakest regime. The paper notes GR-BCD "aligns on Pubmed (\(\tau=+0.69\)) but decorrelates on Cora (\(\tau=+0.16\))" (l.114) and that PR-BCD is "the same paper's larger-budget variant, see text" (\cref{tab:baselines} caption)—but PR-BCD itself is never given a numeric row, and the large-budget regime where BCD would dominate \(S_c\) is never reported.
- **Why it matters.** Presenting only the small-budget cells where the gap is narrow (\(0.356\) vs. \(0.350\) on Pubmed) reads as choosing the regime that flatters \(S_c\). A domain reader will ask: what is the gap at \(k=50\)–\(500\) edges, where PR-BCD operates? If \(S_c\) loses there, the honest framing ("competitive, not dominant") needs the losing numbers to be credible.
- **Suggestion.** Add a PR-BCD row at its intended budget (or a budget-sweep curve, e.g. \(k\in\{5,10,50,100,500\}\)) and state explicitly the budget range where \(S_c\) is competitive vs. where the dedicated attacker pulls ahead. The Cora \(\tau=+0.16\) decorrelation should be explained mechanistically (homophily/degree structure), not just reported.
- **Severity: Major.**

### W3. The AGNNCert "complementarity" framing risks masking that \(r_v\) and a sound certificate measure different objects.
- **Problem.** The paper reports \(r_\text{cert}/r_v\in[4.4,15.0]\) (l.114; \cref{tab:baselines} footnote gives per-dataset mean \([4.9,10.2]\), per-cell \([1.5,22.5]\times\)) and per-seed Kendall \(\tau\in[-0.08,0.23]\)—i.e. essentially *no* rank agreement with the certifier. It then offers a decision rule ("if AGNNCert certifies \(v\) safe, trust it; if \(r_v<\rho\) and AGNNCert does not certify, \(v\) is first-order fragile..."). The near-zero \(\tau\) is itself the proof that \(r_v\) (local Jacobian sensitivity) and AGNNCert (worst-case \(L_\infty\)-IBP tolerance) measure different quantities.
- **Why it matters.** "Complementary, not interchangeable" is a legitimate position *if* the complementarity is shown to add operational value. As written, the decision rule is asserted, not validated: there is no experiment showing that the \(\{r_v\text{-fragile}, \text{not-IBP-certified}\}\) quadrant actually concentrates true breaches better than either signal alone. Without that, a skeptical reader will read it as a hedge that converts a non-result (no correlation with the gold-standard certificate) into a claimed feature.
- **Suggestion.** Run the obvious validation: partition nodes into the four quadrants of (\(r_v\) below/above \(\rho\)) × (IBP-certified/not), then report empirical breach rate per quadrant under a strong attack. If the "\(r_v\)-fragile & uncertified" quadrant has the highest breach rate, the decision rule earns its place; if not, drop it and present \(r_v\) purely as a fast first-order screen (which the breach-rate result in \cref{fig:breach} already supports: every breach satisfies \(\varepsilon>r_v\)).
- **Severity: Major.**

### W4. The explanation/attribution thread is under-engaged given it is the closest prior art to diagnostic (ii).
- **Problem.** The edge-sensitivity ranking (diagnostic ii) is functionally an attribution/explanation method, and the bib *contains* the relevant works (`ying2019gnnexplainer`, `luo2020parameterized` = PGExplainer, `pope2019explainability`, `li2025xgnncert`, plus the influence line `koh2017understanding`, `yeh2018representer`, `pruthi2020estimating`). But the "three threads" taxonomy (\cref{sec:related}) folds these into the implicit-network/influence paragraph and frames them only as "input sensitivity \(\partial z^\star/\partial x\)" / loss-IFT, not as edge-attribution competitors. Gradient-saliency and edge-mask explainers *do* produce an edge ranking, and arguably also a direction (signed saliency).
- **Why it matters.** A domain reader will object that the clean "each thread supplies at most one diagnostic" claim is weakest exactly here: a gradient-saliency map supplies both a direction (sign of the gradient) *and* an edge ranking (magnitude). The taxonomy's load-bearing premise ("no single method returns all three") survives only because explanation methods give no per-node *radius*, which should be stated explicitly rather than left implicit.
- **Suggestion.** Add one or two sentences to \cref{sec:related} acknowledging that gradient-saliency/GNNExplainer-style attribution yields a ranking (and a crude direction) but no per-node first-order radius, and that AEGIS's distinction is the *radius* plus the *equilibrium* (fixed-point) sensitivity rather than logit-gradient sensitivity. This converts a potential strawman objection into a sharpened claim.
- **Severity: Moderate.**

### W5. Net contribution to the graph-adversarial field beyond unification is thin.
- **Problem.** Stripped of the implicit-network machinery, the new diagnostics are: a leading-singular-vector direction (standard variational fact), a column-norm edge ranking (a sensitivity/saliency ranking), and a first-order radius (a linearization-based margin). Each is individually classical; the novelty is (a) deriving all three from the *same* resolvent object and (b) the matrix-free \(S_c v\) computation that scales to \(N=7{,}650\). For *this* community specifically (as opposed to the implicit-network community, which owns the IFT/resolvent novelty), the genuinely new artifact is the label-free, one-query *equilibrium* sensitivity and its empirical alignment with N-1 removal at scale.
- **Why it matters.** The contribution is real but narrower than the abstract implies. A reader expecting a new *attack* or a new *certificate* will find neither; AEGIS is a fast diagnostic/screening proxy. That is valuable, but the framing should not let "unification" carry weight it cannot bear.
- **Suggestion.** State the contribution to the adversarial-graph field precisely: AEGIS is a *label-free, one-query screening proxy* whose edge ranking tracks N-1 damage at scale and whose direction is model-intrinsic (transfer test), not a new attack or certificate. The novelty is the unification + the matrix-free scaling, not the individual diagnostics.
- **Severity: Moderate.**

### W6. Soft-Median / GUARD-class robust aggregation under-covered as a defense baseline.
- **Problem.** The robust-architecture coverage cites RGCN (`zhu2019robust`), Pro-GNN (`jin2020graph`), GNNGuard (`zhang2020gnnguard`), and reliable/robust aggregation (`geisler2020reliable`). The R2 experiment log indicates a "GNNGuard-lite / RobustGCN-lite" ablation was actually *run* (Kendall-\(\tau\) of \(S_c\) ranking vs. brute-force on those backbones), but I do not see this surfaced as a labeled table/figure in `experiments.tex`. GUARD (the attack-aware defense) and Soft-Median aggregation are not distinctly engaged.
- **Why it matters.** Demonstrating that the \(S_c\) ranking still tracks true edge importance *on robustified backbones* is a strong robustness-generalization result and directly answers "does your diagnostic survive on defended models?" Leaving it in the supplementary log rather than the paper undersells a result the authors apparently have.
- **Suggestion.** Surface the RobustGCN/GNNGuard-lite \(\tau\) result as a short labeled row in \cref{tab:cross_domain} or the architecture table, and cite Soft-Median (`geisler2020reliable` covers the family) when claiming generality across defended aggregators.
- **Severity: Minor.**

## Detailed Comments

**On the taxonomy (literature positioning).** The "three threads, each at most one diagnostic" frame (\cref{sec:related}, \cref{sec:intro}) is *mostly* accurate and is not a pure strawman, but it has one soft seam (W4): saliency/explanation methods straddle "direction" and "ranking." The frame holds because (a) attacks give a ranking but no analytic continuous direction and no radius; (b) certifiers/smoothing give a radius but no edge ranking or direction; (c) explainers give a ranking (and crude direction) but no radius. The paper should make point (c) explicit rather than burying explainers in the influence paragraph. The implicit-network paragraph correctly distinguishes AEGIS (structural/edge sensitivity \(\partial z^\star/\partial A\)) from DEQ/IGNN input-sensitivity (`bai2019deep`, `gu2020implicit`, `winston2020monotone`, `el2021implicit`) and from loss-IFT (`koh2017understanding`, `lorraine2020optimizing`, `gould2021deep`)—this is a fair and well-drawn boundary.

**On baseline fairness (the crux).** The four-quadrant design (\cref{tab:attack_full}) is conceptually the right framework. The problem is *which cell carries the headline*. The strongest, fairest evidence in the whole paper is the transfer-surrogate + black-box pair (independent of AEGIS gradients): that is what should be quoted, not the Shift-PGD self-comparison. The Cls-PGD comparison is fair but regime-limited (small \(\varepsilon\)); the authors should show the cross-over \(\varepsilon\) beyond which the first-order direction is no longer best. For GR-BCD/PR-BCD, the honest move is a budget sweep (W2). I credit the authors for the Mettack early-warning comparison (149/150 paired wins at \(k\in\{1..5\}\)), which *is* a fair small-budget regime for Mettack and a legitimate win.

**On the certificate framing (honesty).** \cref{rem:certificates} is exemplary in its candor. The remaining gap is validation of the *decision rule* (W3). The breach-rate result—every breached node satisfies \(\varepsilon>r_v\) (l. in \cref{sec:explicit_extension}/\cref{fig:breach})—is the right kind of evidence that \(r_v\) is a sound *screening* threshold (no false "safe"), and I would lead the \(r_v\) story with *that* rather than with the certifier comparison, since the certifier comparison only establishes that the two measure different things.

**On net contribution.** For the adversarial-graph community specifically, the durable contributions are: (1) the label-free, one-query equilibrium-sensitivity edge ranking with \(\tau=+0.996\) vs. N-1 at \(N=7{,}650\) scale; (2) the matrix-free \(S_c v\) operator enabling that scale; (3) the adaptive-attack-robust edge-protection defense. The "direction" and "radius" are nice corollaries but are not, individually, new to this community. I would not call this a paradigm contribution, but it is a useful, honestly-reported tool.

**On missing / mis-citations (see also Minor).** Coverage is strong. The most defensible *additions* are: PGD-on-graphs / topology-attack-as-optimization is cited (`xu2019topology`) — good. GUARD and Soft-Median as named methods are not distinctly cited (the family is via `geisler2020reliable`). Integrated-gradients-style attribution for GNNs is implicit via `pope2019explainability` but not named. None of these is disqualifying.

## Questions for Authors

1. **(W1)** In \cref{tab:attack_full}, since the SVD direction is the analytic first-order maximizer (\cref{prop:attack}), is the Shift-PGD comparison anything more than a numerical check that PGD converges to the same direction? At what \(\varepsilon\) does the Cls-PGD (independent) adversary *overtake* the one-query direction in classification damage, given the envelope ratio loosens to ~1.39 by \(\varepsilon=0.20\) (\cref{tab:tightness_eps})?
2. **(W2)** What is the AEGIS-vs-PR-BCD gap at PR-BCD's *intended* large budgets (e.g. \(k\in\{50,100,500\}\) on Pubmed/Amazon)? Does \(S_c\) remain "competitive" there, or does the dedicated attacker dominate? Why does GR-BCD decorrelate on Cora (\(\tau=+0.16\)) but align on Pubmed (\(\tau=+0.69\))—homophily, degree distribution, or budget?
3. **(W3)** Can you report empirical breach rate partitioned into the four quadrants of (\(r_v \lessgtr \rho\)) × (AGNNCert-certified or not)? Does the "\(r_v\)-fragile & uncertified" quadrant actually concentrate breaches more than either signal alone? Without this, what operational decision does the decision rule of \cref{tab:baselines} improve?
4. **(W4)** Does a signed gradient-saliency map (or GNNExplainer edge mask) not supply both a ranking *and* a crude direction? If so, the precise differentiator is the per-node radius and the equilibrium (fixed-point) vs. logit-gradient distinction—can you state this explicitly in \cref{sec:related}?
5. **(W6)** The R2 log indicates a GNNGuard-lite / RobustGCN-lite \(\tau\) experiment was run. Why is it not in the paper? It would directly answer "does the \(S_c\) ranking survive on robustified backbones?"
6. The transfer-surrogate test uses a second *IGNN*. Does the direction also transfer to a *different architecture* (e.g. surrogate GCN/APPNP attacking the IGNN), or is the 0.99 alignment specific to same-family surrogates?

## Minor Issues

- **\cref{tab:baselines} numbers appear inconsistent across indexed snapshots:** the AGNNCert "AEGIS" cell shows \(0.187\) in one rendering and \(0.163\) in another, and the prose says \(r_\text{cert}/r_v\in[4.4,15.0]\) while the table footnote says per-dataset mean \([4.9,10.2]\). Reconcile the headline range with the table footnote so the two cannot be read as contradictory.
- **PR-BCD is named in the \cref{tab:baselines} caption and \cref{sec:prior_comparison} but has no numeric row.** Either give it a row or rename the entries "GR-BCD/PR-BCD (BCD family)" to avoid implying a PR-BCD measurement that is not shown.
- **\cref{sec:adaptive} and \cref{sec:certificates} share the same `\label`** (l.39: `\label{sec:adaptive}\label{sec:certificates}`), as do several labels in \cref{sec:defense_ablation} (l.132 stacks four `\label`s). This will make `\cref` targets ambiguous; assign distinct labels.
- **"maximally sensitive first-order direction"** (\cref{sec:adaptive}, l.43) — the R2 log (N2/C4) says this phrasing and "no prior method" were meant to be retired/scoped in the abstract; verify the body text matches the scoped abstract so the bold claim is not reintroduced in \cref{sec:adaptive}.
- **`li2025xgnncert` (XGNNCert, robust explainable GNN)** is in the bib but I do not see it cited in the related-work explanation discussion; if it is the closest "robust attribution" prior art it should be engaged, not just listed.
- **Power-grid LODF comparison (\cref{tab:baselines}, case118 P@10 = 0.81 vs. \(\le 0.20\))** is outside my domain; I flag only that the "\(\le 0.20\)" baseline entry is unusually round and should state how the LODF P@10 was thresholded.
